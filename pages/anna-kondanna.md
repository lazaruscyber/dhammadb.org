**Aññā Koṇḍañña** ({{lang|Pāli|Aññā Koṇḍañña}}) was the first person to understand the Dhamma taught by the Buddha and the first monk of the dispensation. At the end of the [[Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta]] the vision of the Dhamma arose in him, and the Blessed One exclaimed "Koṇḍañña has understood!", from which he received his name.[^sn5611] The Blessed One later declared him foremost among the monks of long standing.[^an1]

## Early life

According to the commentaries, Koṇḍañña was born before the Bodhisatta, into a wealthy brahmin family of the village of Doṇavatthu near Kapilavatthu; Koṇḍañña was his family name. He mastered the three Vedas and was skilled in reading bodily marks.[^aa]

When the Bodhisatta was born, he was one of the eight brahmins invited to examine the marks on the child's body. The others gave two possibilities, that the child would become either a universal monarch or a Buddha; Koṇḍañña, the youngest, declared with certainty that the child would become a Buddha. When the Bodhisatta went forth, Koṇḍañña went forth too, together with four sons of the other brahmins: Vappa, Bhaddiya, Mahānāma and Assaji. These became the group of five monks.[^jn]

The five attended the Bodhisatta through the six years of striving. When the Bodhisatta gave up the extreme austerities and took solid food, they left in disappointment and went to Isipatana near Bārāṇasī.[^jn][^apa]

## The first to understand

After the awakening, the Blessed One went to Isipatana and taught the five monks the [[Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta]]. While the discourse was being spoken, the dustless, stainless vision of the Dhamma arose in Koṇḍañña: "Whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation." The Blessed One then uttered, "Koṇḍañña has indeed understood! Koṇḍañña has indeed understood!" ({{term|aññāsi vata bho Koṇḍañño}}), and so he came to be known as Aññā Koṇḍañña.[^sn5611] The commentaries add that eighteen crores of Brahmās attained the fruit of stream-entry together with him.[^ud]

Koṇḍañña then asked for the going forth, and the Blessed One ordained him with the words "Come, monk" ({{term|ehi bhikkhu}}), making him the first monk of the dispensation.[^mv] A few days later, at the end of the [[Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta]], he and the other four monks attained arahantship.[^mv]

## Later life

At a great assembly at the Jetavana the Blessed One declared Koṇḍañña foremost among those who first comprehended the Dhamma, and foremost among the disciples of long standing ({{term|rattaññūnaṃ}}).[^an1][^aa]

In the assembly of monks he sat behind the two chief disciples. Finding that his presence near the Blessed One had become inconvenient for himself and others, he obtained the Blessed One's permission to live on the banks of the Mandākinī lake in the Chaddanta forest in the Himalaya, where he stayed for twelve years.[^aa] He ordained his nephew Puṇṇa, son of his sister Mantānī, who became the Venerable Puṇṇa Mantāniputta, later declared foremost among the teachers of the Dhamma.[^aa]

At the end of the twelve years he returned to take leave of the Blessed One, then went back to the Chaddanta forest, where he attained parinibbāna. The commentaries relate that all the Himalaya mourned his passing, and that his funeral rites were performed by elephants of the forest, led by the deva Nāgadatta.[^sa]

## Verses

Verses attributed to the Venerable Aññā Koṇḍañña are preserved in the Theragāthā.[^thag]
