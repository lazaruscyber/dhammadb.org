**Asubha** ({{lang|Pāli|asubha}}) is foulness or unattractiveness. As a meditation, it is the contemplation of the unattractive nature of the body, the direct remedy for sensual lust.[^vism]

## The ten kinds

The [[Visuddhimagga]] lists ten kinds of foulness, each a stage in the decay of a corpse: the bloated, the livid, the festering, the cut up, the gnawed, the scattered, the hacked and scattered, the bleeding, the worm-infested, and the skeleton. Each can lead to the first [[Jhāna|jhāna]] only, since the object is so repulsive that it can be held only with the help of applied thought.[^vism]

## The thirty-two parts

Closely related is the contemplation of the thirty-two parts of the body ({{term|dvattiṃsākāra}}), included in the [[Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta]] and in the [[Khuddakapāṭha]]: head hairs, body hairs, nails, teeth, skin, flesh, sinews, bones and so on. The Blessed One compared the meditator to a man with good eyes opening a bag full of various grains and seeing "this is rice, these are beans, this is sesame".[^mn10]

## Cautions

The Vinaya relates that once, after the Blessed One praised the contemplation of foulness and went into solitude, some bhikkhus became so disgusted with their bodies that they took their own lives. On returning the Blessed One taught the bhikkhus mindfulness of breathing instead.[^vin] The tradition therefore teaches that foulness must be practised under a teacher's guidance and balanced with other subjects.
