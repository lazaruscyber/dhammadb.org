The **Sāmaññaphala Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Sāmaññaphala Sutta}}), "the Fruits of the Contemplative Life", is the second discourse of the [[Dīgha Nikāya]]. In it the Blessed One answers King Ajātasattu's question about the visible fruits of the life of a recluse, and in doing so sets out the whole gradual path.[^dn2]

## Occasion

On the night of the full moon of [[Kattikā]], the {{term|komudī}} full moon at the end of the rains, King Ajātasattu sat on the palace roof and asked his ministers which teacher he might visit. The physician [[Jīvaka]] proposed the Blessed One, who was staying in Jīvaka's mango grove with 1,250 bhikkhus. The king went there and asked whether there is any fruit of the contemplative life visible here and now, saying he had asked the same of six other teachers without a satisfying answer.[^dn2]

## The fruits

The Blessed One answered with a graduated series of fruits, each higher than the last:

1. A slave or a farmer who goes forth is honoured by the king himself: a fruit visible here and now.
2. Virtue, restraint of the senses, mindfulness and clear comprehension, and contentment.
3. The abandoning of the five hindrances ([[Nīvaraṇa]]), described with five similes such as a debt repaid and recovery from illness.
4. The four [[Jhāna|jhānas]].
5. Insight knowledge, the mind-made body, the supernormal powers, the divine ear, knowledge of others' minds, recollection of past lives and the divine eye.
6. The knowledge of the destruction of the taints: the highest fruit.

## Conclusion

The king went for refuge to the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Saṅgha and confessed the killing of his father. After he had left, the Blessed One told the bhikkhus that had the king not killed his father, the vision of the Dhamma would have arisen in him on that very seat.[^dn2]
