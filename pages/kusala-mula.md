The **kusala-mūlas** ({{lang|Pāli|kusala-mūla}}) are the three wholesome roots: non-greed ({{term|alobha}}), non-hatred ({{term|adosa}}) and non-delusion ({{term|amoha}}). They are the opposites of the [[Akusala-mūla|unwholesome roots]] and are the sources of all wholesome action.[^an369]

## Their expressions

| Root | Positive expression |
|---|---|
| Non-greed | Generosity ([[Dāna]]), renunciation, detachment |
| Non-hatred | Loving-kindness ([[Mettā]]), patience |
| Non-delusion | Wisdom ([[Paññā]]) |

The [[Visuddhimagga]] describes non-greed as the mind not sticking to its object, like a drop of water on a lotus leaf; non-hatred as absence of ferocity, like a gentle friend; and non-delusion as penetrating the true nature of things, like an arrow shot by a skilled archer.[^vism]

## In consciousness

In the [[Abhidhamma Piṭaka|Abhidhamma]] every beautiful consciousness has at least non-greed and non-hatred; those accompanied by knowledge also have non-delusion. Consciousness with all three roots is the most powerful, and supramundane consciousness always has all three.[^abhs]

## The meaning of kusala

The commentaries give the meanings of kusala as healthy, blameless, skilful and productive of happy results.[^dhsa]
