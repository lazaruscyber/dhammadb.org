The **Sigālovāda Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Sigālovāda Sutta}}), "the Advice to Sigāla", is the thirty-first discourse of the [[Dīgha Nikāya]]. The commentary calls it the discipline of the householder ({{term|gihivinaya}}), because it covers the whole duty of lay life.[^dn31a]

## Occasion

Early one morning the Blessed One saw the young Sigāla, with wet hair and clothes, worshipping the six directions as his dying father had asked. The Blessed One explained how the six directions are worshipped in the discipline of the noble ones.[^dn31]

## Teaching

The Blessed One taught Sigāla to give up the four defilements of action (killing, stealing, sexual misconduct and lying), not to act from desire, hatred, delusion or fear, and to avoid the six ways of squandering wealth, such as intoxicants, roaming the streets at night and bad company. The Blessed One described false friends and true friends, and how wealth should be divided and kept.

## The six directions

| Direction | Represents |
|---|---|
| East | Mother and father |
| South | Teachers |
| West | Wife and children |
| North | Friends and companions |
| Below | Servants and workers |
| Above | Ascetics and brahmins |

For each relationship the sutta sets out five duties on each side, so that each direction is protected and made secure.[^dn31]
