The **Milindapañha** ({{lang|Pāli|Milindapañha}}), "the Questions of King Milinda", records a series of dialogues between King Milinda, who ruled at Sāgala, and the arahant Nāgasena. The king, a skilled debater, puts to the elder many difficult questions on the teaching, and the elder answers them with similes drawn from everyday life.

It is highly respected in the Theravāda tradition and is quoted by the commentaries. In Myanmar it is included in the [[Khuddaka Nikāya]], and it was recited at the [[Sixth Council]].

## The chariot simile

At their first meeting the king asks the elder his name. The elder replies that he is called Nāgasena, but that this is only a designation; no person is found there. When the king objects, the elder asks him whether the axle, the wheels, the body or any other part is the chariot in which he came. The king agrees that "chariot" is only a name for the parts in combination, and the elder replies that "Nāgasena" likewise is only a name for the aggregates in combination.
