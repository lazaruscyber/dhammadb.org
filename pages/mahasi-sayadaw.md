The **Mahāsi Sayadaw** (Pāli name Sobhana) was among the most influential meditation teachers of modern Theravāda. The method taught at the Sayadaw's centre in Yangon, based on the [[Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta]] and its commentary, spread throughout Myanmar and the world.[^ms]

## Life

The Sayadaw was born on 29 July 1904 in Seikkhun village in Shwebo district, became a novice at twelve and received higher ordination at nineteen. After studying the scriptures, the Sayadaw practised insight meditation under the Mingun Jetavana Sayadaw in Thaton. The name Mahāsi comes from a large drum ({{term|si}}) at the monastery in Seikkhun where the Sayadaw taught.[^ms]

## The method

In 1949, at the invitation of the Prime Minister U Nu and the Buddha Sāsana Nuggaha Organization, the Sayadaw moved to the Sāsana Yeiktha in Yangon. The method is that of bare insight ({{term|suddhavipassanā}}): the meditator notes the rising and falling of the abdomen as a primary object, and notes every other mental and physical phenomenon as it arises, such as "hearing", "thinking" or "lifting, moving, placing" in walking meditation. Momentary concentration is developed and leads through the stages of insight knowledge ([[Vipassanā ñāṇa]]).[^ms]

## The Sixth Council

At the [[Sixth Council]] (1954 to 1956), the Sayadaw served as the questioner ({{term|pucchaka}}), asking the questions on the texts that were answered by the [[Mingun Sayadaw]], in the role played by the Venerable [[Mahākassapa]] at the [[First Council]].

## Works

The Sayadaw wrote more than seventy works, including *The Progress of Insight* (Visuddhiñāṇakathā) in Pāli, a Burmese translation and commentary on the [[Visuddhimagga]], and a manual of insight meditation.
