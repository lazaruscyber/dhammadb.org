**Assayuja** ({{lang|Pāli|Assayuja}}) is the seventh month of the Theravāda lunar calendar in the traditional Pāli listing, falling in about September to October in the Gregorian calendar. Like every month of the calendar, it takes its name from the constellation ({{term|nakkhatta}}) near which the moon is full in that month. Because it comes just before [[Kattikā]], it is also called the early Kattikā ({{term|pubba-kattikā}}).[^abh]

## In the calendar

Assayuja has 29 days and belongs to the rainy season ({{term|vassāna}}), one of the three seasons of four months each into which the year is divided. It follows [[Poṭṭhapāda]] and is followed by [[Kattikā]]. Like every month, it contains four uposatha observance days: the eighth day of the waxing moon, the full moon, the eighth day of the waning moon and the new moon.

## The Pavāraṇā

The earlier rains residence ends on the full-moon day of Assayuja with the Pavāraṇā, the "invitation", at which each bhikkhu invites the community to point out any fault seen, heard or suspected in him.[^vin4] Bhikkhus who have completed the rains residence then enter the robe season, during which the kathina robe may be offered and made.[^vin7]

## Events

According to the commentaries, after spending the rains residence in the Tāvatiṃsa heaven teaching the Abhidhamma, the Blessed One descended to the human world at Saṅkassa on the day of the great Pavāraṇā, the full moon of Assayuja.[^dhpa] In Myanmar this full moon, Thadingyut, is celebrated as a festival of lights in remembrance of that descent.
