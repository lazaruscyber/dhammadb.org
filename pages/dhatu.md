**Dhātu** ({{lang|Pāli|dhātu}}), "element", is something that bears its own nature. The term is used for several sets in the teaching.

## The four great elements

The four great elements ({{term|mahābhūta}}) are the primary constituents of all [[Rūpa|matter]]:[^vism]

| Element | Characteristic |
|---|---|
| Earth ({{term|pathavī}}) | Hardness and softness |
| Water ({{term|āpo}}) | Cohesion and flowing |
| Fire ({{term|tejo}}) | Heat and cold |
| Air ({{term|vāyo}}) | Support and motion |

Their contemplation is the meditation subject [[Catudhātuvavatthāna]]. The [[Mahāhatthipadopama Sutta]] examines them, internal and external, as not self.

## The eighteen elements

The eighteen elements analyse experience into each sense door, its object and the corresponding consciousness:

| Faculty | Object | Consciousness |
|---|---|---|
| Eye | Visible form | Eye-consciousness |
| Ear | Sound | Ear-consciousness |
| Nose | Odour | Nose-consciousness |
| Tongue | Taste | Tongue-consciousness |
| Body | Tangible | Body-consciousness |
| Mind | Mental object | Mind-consciousness |

The [[Vibhaṅga]] and [[Dhātukathā]] treat the elements alongside the [[Khandha|aggregates]] and [[Āyatana|bases]].[^vbh]

## Relics

The word dhātu also means the relics of the Buddha and of arahants, as divided after the cremation described in the [[Mahāparinibbāna Sutta]].
