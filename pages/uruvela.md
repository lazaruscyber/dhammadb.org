**Uruvelā** ({{lang|Pāli|Uruvelā}}) was a region on the bank of the Nerañjarā river in Magadha. There the Bodhisatta practised austerities for six years and attained full awakening beneath the Bodhi tree. It is the second of the four places of pilgrimage named in the [[Mahāparinibbāna Sutta]].[^dn16]

## The search

In the [[Ariyapariyesana Sutta]] the Blessed One recalls: "Wandering by stages in the Magadhan country, I eventually arrived at Senānigama near Uruvelā. There I saw an agreeable piece of ground, a delightful grove with a clear-flowing river with pleasant, smooth banks and nearby a village for alms resort. I considered: this will serve for the striving of a clansman intent on striving."[^mn26]

## The awakening

After giving up the austerities and accepting milk-rice from Sujātā, the Bodhisatta sat beneath the {{term|assattha}} tree, later called the Bodhi tree, and resolved not to rise until awakening was attained. On the night of the full moon of [[Vesākha]], having defeated [[Māra]], the Bodhisatta attained the three true knowledges and became the Buddha.[^jn]

## The seven weeks

The commentaries relate that the Blessed One spent seven weeks in the vicinity enjoying the bliss of liberation: at the Bodhi tree, gazing at it, walking on a jewelled walk, in a jewelled house reflecting on the Abhidhamma, at the Goatherd's banyan tree, at the Mucalinda tree sheltered by the nāga king, and at the Rājāyatana tree, where the merchants Tapussa and Bhallika offered the first meal.[^jn]

## Later events

The Blessed One returned to Uruvelā after the first rains and converted the three Kassapa brothers and their thousand followers, before teaching the [[Ādittapariyāya Sutta]] at nearby Gayāsīsa.
