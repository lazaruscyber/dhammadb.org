The **Second Council** ({{lang|Pāli|Dutiya Saṅgāyana}}) was held a hundred years after the parinibbāna at the Vālikārāma in [[Vesālī]]. It was convened to settle ten practices of the Vajjian bhikkhus that were contrary to the [[Vinaya Piṭaka|Vinaya]].[^vin]

## The ten points

The Venerable Yasa Kākaṇḍakaputta found the Vajjian bhikkhus of Vesālī asking lay people for gold and silver. When he objected, they had him expelled, but he gathered support from bhikkhus of the west and south. The Vajjian bhikkhus held ten practices to be allowable, including:

1. Storing salt in a horn.
2. Eating when the shadow of the sun had passed two fingers' breadth beyond noon.
3. Going to another village for a second meal.
4. Holding separate Uposathas within one boundary.
5. Carrying out a formal act with an incomplete assembly, and obtaining consent afterwards.
6. Following a practice because a teacher had followed it.
7. Drinking unchurned milk after the meal.
8. Drinking unfermented palm wine.
9. Using a rug without a fringe.
10. Accepting gold and silver.[^vin]

## The decision

A committee of eight elders, four from the east and four from the west, was appointed. The Venerable Revata questioned the Venerable Sabbakāmī, the most senior bhikkhu on earth, who had been a pupil of the Venerable Ānanda. Each point was found to be against the Vinaya. Seven hundred arahants then recited the Dhamma and Vinaya.[^vin]

## Later tradition

The [[Mahāvaṃsa]] and the commentaries relate that the bhikkhus who rejected the decision held their own great assembly, and that this was the beginning of division within the Saṅgha. Those who upheld the decision are the elders, the Theravāda.[^mhv]

[^vin]: Vinaya Piṭaka, Cūḷavagga 12, *Sattasatikakkhandhaka*.
[^mhv]: *Mahāvaṃsa*. Chapter IV.
