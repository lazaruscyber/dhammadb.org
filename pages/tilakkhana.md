**Tilakkhaṇa** ({{lang|Pāli|tilakkhaṇa}}) are the three characteristics or marks of existence: [[Anicca|impermanence]], [[Dukkha|suffering]] and [[Anattā|non-self]]. Seeing them directly is the work of insight meditation ([[Vipassanā]]).

## The Dhammapada verses

:::quote Dhammapada 277 to 279
*Sabbe saṅkhārā aniccā.* All formations are impermanent.
*Sabbe saṅkhārā dukkhā.* All formations are suffering.
*Sabbe dhammā anattā.* All phenomena are non-self.

When one sees this with wisdom, one turns away from suffering. This is the path to purity.
:::

The third verse says "all phenomena" ({{term|dhammā}}) rather than "all formations", because non-self applies also to the unconditioned, Nibbāna, which is not impermanent or suffering.[^dhpa]

## Their relation

In the [[Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta]] the Blessed One asks whether form is permanent or impermanent; the bhikkhus answer impermanent. Is what is impermanent suffering or happiness? Suffering. Is it fitting to regard what is impermanent, suffering and subject to change as "this is mine, this I am, this is my self"? No. In this way the three characteristics are seen to follow from one another.[^sn5559]

## What conceals them

The [[Visuddhimagga]] explains that each characteristic is hidden by something: impermanence by continuity, suffering by the changing of postures, and non-self by compactness. When continuity is broken up by discerning rise and fall, when the postures are examined, and when compactness is resolved into elements, the characteristics appear in their true nature.[^vism]

[^sn5559]: Saṃyutta Nikāya 22.59, *Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta*.
[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*.
