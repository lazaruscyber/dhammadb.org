The **Dhammapada** ({{lang|Pāli|Dhammapada}}), "Verses of the Dhamma", is the second book of the [[Khuddaka Nikāya]] and one of the best-known texts of the [[Tipiṭaka]]. It contains 423 verses spoken by the Blessed One, arranged in twenty-six chapters ({{term|vagga}}) by theme.

## The first verse

:::quote Dhammapada, verse 1
*Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā, manoseṭṭhā manomayā;*
*manasā ce paduṭṭhena, bhāsati vā karoti vā,*
*tato naṃ dukkhamanveti, cakkaṃva vahato padaṃ.*

Mind precedes all states; mind is their chief; they are mind-made. If one speaks or acts with a corrupted mind, suffering follows, as the wheel follows the foot of the ox.
:::

## Chapters

The chapters include the Pairs ({{term|Yamaka}}), Heedfulness ({{term|Appamāda}}), the Mind ({{term|Citta}}), Flowers, the Fool, the Wise, the Arahant, the Thousands, Evil, Punishment, Old Age, the Self, the World, the Buddha, Happiness, Affection, Anger, Impurity, the Just, the Path, Miscellaneous, Hell, the Elephant, Craving, the Bhikkhu and the Brahmin.

## The commentary

The *Dhammapadaṭṭhakathā* tells, for each verse or group of verses, the occasion on which the Blessed One spoke it. Its stories are a rich source for the lives of the disciples and are widely told in Theravāda countries.[^dhpa]

## Well-known verses

- Verse 5: hatred is never appeased by hatred, but by non-hatred.
- Verse 183: not doing any evil, undertaking the good, purifying the mind: this is the teaching of the Buddhas.
- Verses 277 to 279: all formations are [[Anicca|impermanent]] and [[Dukkha|suffering]], and all phenomena are [[Anattā|non-self]].

[^dhpa]: *Dhammapadaṭṭhakathā*, the commentary to the Dhammapada.
