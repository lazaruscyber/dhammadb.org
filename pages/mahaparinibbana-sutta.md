The **Mahāparinibbāna Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Mahāparinibbāna Sutta}}), "the Great Discourse on the Final Passing", is the sixteenth and longest discourse of the [[Dīgha Nikāya]]. It records the last year of the Blessed One's life: the final journey from Rājagaha to Kusinārā, the last teachings, the parinibbāna, and the cremation and division of the relics.[^dn16]

## The last journey

| Place | Events |
|---|---|
| [[Rājagaha]] | The seven conditions of welfare, taught first for the Vajjians and then for the Saṅgha |
| Pāṭaligāma | The five benefits of [[Sīla|virtue]] taught to the lay followers; the prophecy of the future city |
| [[Vesālī]] | The courtesan Ambapālī gives her mango grove to the Saṅgha |
| Beluva | During the last rains residence a severe illness; the teaching "be islands unto yourselves" |
| Cāpāla shrine | The relinquishing of the life process, three months before the parinibbāna |
| Pāvā | The last meal, offered by Cunda the smith |
| [[Kusinārā]] | The parinibbāna between the twin sāla trees |

## The final teachings

At Kusinārā the Blessed One recommended four places for pilgrimage: where the Tathāgata was born, attained awakening, set the Wheel of the Dhamma in motion, and attained final Nibbāna. The wanderer Subhadda was the last person to be ordained by the Blessed One. The Blessed One told the bhikkhus that after the parinibbāna the Dhamma and Vinaya would be their teacher, invited them three times to ask any final question, and then spoke the last words:[^dn16]

:::quote Mahāparinibbāna Sutta
*Vayadhammā saṅkhārā, appamādena sampādetha.*

Formations are subject to vanishing. Strive on with heedfulness.
:::

## After the parinibbāna

The Mallas of Kusinārā honoured the body for six days and cremated it on the seventh, when the Venerable [[Mahākassapa]] had arrived. The relics were divided into eight portions by the brahmin Doṇa, and stūpas were built over them.[^dn16]

[^dn16]: Dīgha Nikāya 16, *Mahāparinibbāna Sutta*.
