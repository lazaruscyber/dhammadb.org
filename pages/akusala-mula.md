The **akusala-mūlas** ({{lang|Pāli|akusala-mūla}}) are the three unwholesome roots: greed ({{term|lobha}}), hatred ({{term|dosa}}) and delusion ({{term|moha}}). Every unwholesome state of mind is rooted in one or more of them.[^an369]

## Their characteristics

| Root | Characteristic | Simile |
|---|---|---|
| Greed | Grasping an object | Like monkey lime that sticks |
| Hatred | Ferocity | Like a provoked snake |
| Delusion | Blindness, not understanding | Like darkness |

These descriptions are from the [[Visuddhimagga]] and the [[Atthasālinī]].[^vism]

The Blessed One explained that greed is a lesser fault but slow to fade, hatred a great fault but quick to fade, and delusion a great fault and slow to fade. Greed arises through unwise attention to an attractive object, hatred through unwise attention to a repulsive object, and delusion through unwise attention in general.[^an368]

## Unwholesome consciousness

In the [[Abhidhamma Piṭaka|Abhidhamma]] there are twelve unwholesome types of consciousness: eight rooted in greed, two in hatred and two in delusion. Delusion is present in all twelve.[^abhs]

## Abandoning

Greed, hatred and delusion are fully destroyed in the [[Arahant|arahant]]. In the suttas, [[Nibbāna]] is defined as "the destruction of lust, the destruction of hatred, the destruction of delusion".[^sn381]
