**Suddhodana** ({{lang|Pāli|Suddhodana}}) was the ruler of the Sakyan clan at [[Kapilavatthu]] and the father of the Bodhisatta. His first queen, Mahāmāyā, died seven days after the birth, and her sister [[Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī]] raised the child.[^dn14]

## The prophecy

At the naming ceremony, the brahmins predicted that the child would become either a wheel-turning monarch or a Buddha. The youngest, Koṇḍañña, later the Venerable [[Aññā Koṇḍañña]], predicted with certainty that the child would become a Buddha. Wishing his son to become a monarch, the king surrounded the Bodhisatta with luxury and kept all sight of suffering away.[^jn]

## The ploughing festival

During the royal ploughing festival, the young Bodhisatta, left alone beneath a rose-apple tree, sat cross-legged and attained the first jhāna. The king, seeing that the shadow of the tree had not moved, paid homage to the young prince for the second time. The Blessed One recalled this experience before the awakening as the way to awakening.[^mn36]

## Conversion

After the awakening the king sent messengers to invite the Blessed One to Kapilavatthu. On the Blessed One's first visit, the king was distressed to see the Blessed One walking for alms in the street. The Blessed One explained that this was the custom of the lineage of Buddhas, and the king attained stream-entry. He later became a non-returner, and on his deathbed, after the Blessed One visited and taught him, he attained arahantship and passed away as a lay arahant.[^jn]
