**Anurādhapura** ({{lang|Pāli|Anurādhapura}}) was the capital of Sri Lanka for more than a thousand years. It was the seat of the Mahāvihāra, the great monastery that preserved the Theravāda tradition.[^mhv]

## Arrival of the Dhamma

The Venerable [[Mahinda]] taught King Devānampiya Tissa and the people of Anurādhapura after their meeting at Mihintale. The king gave the Mahāmegha park to the Saṅgha, where the Mahāvihāra was founded.[^mhv]

## The Bodhi tree

The nun [[Saṅghamittā]] brought a branch of the Bodhi tree from [[Uruvelā]], which was planted in the Mahāmegha park. It still stands, venerated as the Jayamahābodhi.[^mhv]

## The great stūpas

| Stūpa | Builder |
|---|---|
| Thūpārāma | King Devānampiya Tissa, enshrining the right collarbone relic |
| Mahāthūpa (Ruvanvalisāya) | King Duṭṭhagāmaṇī |
| Abhayagiri | King Vaṭṭagāmaṇī Abhaya |
| Jetavana | King Mahāsena |

The [[Mahāvaṃsa]] devotes many chapters to the building of the Mahāthūpa by King Duṭṭhagāmaṇī.[^mhv]

## The Mahāvihāra tradition

The Mahāvihāra preserved the Tipiṭaka and the Sinhala commentaries. In the fifth century [[Buddhaghosa]] came to the Mahāvihāra and rendered the commentaries into Pāli, so that the Theravāda of today is the tradition of the Mahāvihāra.[^cv] At the Aluvihāra, to the south, the Tipiṭaka was first written down at the [[Fourth Council]].
