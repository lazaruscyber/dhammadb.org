**Samatha** ({{lang|Pāli|samatha}}) is tranquillity or calm: the development of concentration ([[Samādhi]]) that unifies the mind on a single object, suppresses the five hindrances ([[Nīvaraṇa]]) and leads to the [[Jhāna|jhānas]]. It is one of the two kinds of meditation, the other being insight ([[Vipassanā]]).[^an232]

## Samatha and vipassanā

:::quote Aṅguttara Nikāya 2.32
When tranquillity is developed, what benefit does it bring? The mind is developed. When the mind is developed, lust is abandoned. When insight is developed, what benefit does it bring? Wisdom is developed. When wisdom is developed, ignorance is abandoned.
:::

The Yuganaddha Sutta describes four ways in which bhikkhus reach arahantship: insight preceded by tranquillity, tranquillity preceded by insight, the two yoked together, and a mind seized by agitation about the Dhamma that then settles.[^an4170]

## The forty subjects

The [[Visuddhimagga]] lists forty meditation subjects ([[Kammaṭṭhāna]]) for samatha: ten [[Kasiṇa|kasiṇas]], ten kinds of foulness ([[Asubha]]), ten [[Anussati|recollections]], four [[Brahmavihāra|divine abidings]], four immaterial states, one perception (of repulsiveness in nutriment) and one defining ([[Catudhātuvavatthāna|of the four elements]]).[^vism]
