**Rūpa** ({{lang|Pāli|rūpa}}) means matter or form. It is the first of the five aggregates ([[Khandha]]) and the third of the four ultimate realities ([[Paramattha]]). Matter is so called because it is "deformed" or changes under adverse physical conditions such as cold and heat.[^abhs]

## The twenty-eight material phenomena

| Group | Number | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| The great elements | 4 | Earth, water, fire, air ([[Dhātu]]) |
| Sensitive matter | 5 | Eye, ear, nose, tongue, body |
| Objective matter | 4 | Visible form, sound, odour, taste (tangible being three of the elements) |
| Sexual matter | 2 | Femininity, masculinity |
| Heart base | 1 | The base of mind-consciousness |
| Life faculty | 1 | Physical life |
| Nutriment | 1 | Nutritive essence |
| Limiting | 1 | Space |
| Communicating | 2 | Bodily and vocal intimation |
| Mutable | 3 | Lightness, malleability, wieldiness |
| Characteristics | 4 | Production, continuity, decay, impermanence |

The first eighteen are concretely produced matter; the last ten are non-concrete.[^abhs]

## Four causes

Matter arises from four causes: [[Kamma|kamma]], consciousness, temperature ({{term|utu}}) and nutriment.

## Groups

Material phenomena do not arise singly but in groups ({{term|kalāpa}}). The smallest group, the pure octad, consists of the four elements with colour, odour, taste and nutritive essence.[^abhs]

[^abhs]: Anuruddha, *Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha*. Chapter VI.
