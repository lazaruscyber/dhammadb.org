**Vipāka** ({{lang|Pāli|vipāka}}), "ripening", is the result of [[Kamma|kamma]]. Kamma is the seed and vipāka the fruit. In the [[Abhidhamma Piṭaka|Abhidhamma]], vipāka means specifically the resultant states of consciousness and their mental factors, which are themselves morally neutral: they are neither wholesome nor unwholesome, but are the experience of pleasant or unpleasant objects that past kamma brings.[^abhs]

## Resultant consciousness

Of the eighty-nine types of [[Citta|consciousness]], thirty-six are resultants:

| Kind | Number |
|---|---|
| Unwholesome resultants | 7 |
| Wholesome resultants without roots | 8 |
| Sense-sphere resultants with roots | 8 |
| Fine-material resultants | 5 |
| Immaterial resultants | 4 |
| Supramundane fruits | 4 |

Rebirth-linking ([[Paṭisandhi]]), the life-continuum ([[Bhavaṅga]]) and death consciousness are always resultants.[^abhs]

## The four imponderables

The precise working out of the results of kamma is one of the four imponderables ({{term|acinteyya}}) which, the Blessed One said, should not be speculated about, because one who tries to think them out would reap madness and vexation.[^an477]

## Not all feeling is vipāka

The Sīvaka Sutta lists eight causes of feeling, of which the ripening of kamma is only one; others are bile, phlegm, wind, their combination, change of climate, careless behaviour and assault.[^sn3621]

[^abhs]: Anuruddha, *Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha*.
[^an477]: Aṅguttara Nikāya 4.77, *Acinteyya Sutta*.
[^sn3621]: Saṃyutta Nikāya 36.21, *Sīvaka Sutta*.
