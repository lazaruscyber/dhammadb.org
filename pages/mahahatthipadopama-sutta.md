The **Mahāhatthipadopama Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Mahāhatthipadopama Sutta}}), "the Greater Simile of the Elephant's Footprint", is the twenty-eighth discourse of the [[Majjhima Nikāya]], spoken by the Venerable [[Sāriputta]].

## The simile

Just as the footprint of every animal that walks fits within the footprint of the elephant, which is the largest of them, so all wholesome states are included in the [[Four Noble Truths]].[^mn]

## Teaching

Sāriputta analyses the noble truth of suffering into the five aggregates subject to clinging, and the form aggregate into the four great elements: earth, water, fire and air. He shows each element, internal and external, to be impermanent, subject to destruction, disappearance and change, and not to be regarded as "mine", "I" or "my self".

He then quotes the Blessed One: "One who sees dependent origination sees the Dhamma; one who sees the Dhamma sees dependent origination."[^mn]

[^mn]: Majjhima Nikāya 28, *Mahāhatthipadopama Sutta*.
