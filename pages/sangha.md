The **Saṅgha** ({{lang|Pāli|saṅgha}}), "community", is the third of the Three Jewels. The word is used in two senses:

- **The noble Saṅgha** ({{term|ariya-saṅgha}}): the disciples who have attained the paths and fruits, "the four pairs of persons, the eight types of individuals". This is the Saṅgha in which one takes refuge.
- **The conventional Saṅgha** ({{term|sammuti-saṅgha}}): the monastic community of [[Bhikkhu|bhikkhus]] and [[Bhikkhunī|bhikkhunīs]] ordained according to the [[Vinaya Piṭaka|Vinaya]]. For formal acts a quorum of at least four bhikkhus is required.

## The nine qualities

The qualities of the Saṅgha are recited in the formula of the [[Dhajagga Sutta]]:[^sn113]

:::columns 2
1. Practising well
2. Practising uprightly
3. Practising the true way
4. Practising properly
5. Worthy of gifts
6. Worthy of hospitality
7. Worthy of offerings
8. Worthy of reverential salutation
9. The unsurpassed field of merit for the world
:::

## The fourfold assembly

The Blessed One spoke of the four assemblies ({{term|parisā}}) that make the teaching complete: bhikkhus, nuns, male lay followers and female lay followers. Before the parinibbāna Māra urged the Blessed One to pass away, and the Blessed One answered that this would not happen until all four assemblies were well trained and able to teach the Dhamma.[^dn16]

See [[Ariya-puggala]] for the eight noble persons, and [[Bhikkhu]] for the monastic order.

[^sn113]: Saṃyutta Nikāya 11.3, *Dhajagga Sutta*.
[^dn16]: Dīgha Nikāya 16, *Mahāparinibbāna Sutta*.
