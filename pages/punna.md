**Puñña** ({{lang|Pāli|puñña}}) is merit: wholesome [[Kamma|kamma]] that purifies the mind and brings happy results. The Blessed One said: "Do not fear merit, bhikkhus. Merit is a name for happiness."[^iti22]

## Three bases

The Puññakiriyavatthu Sutta names three bases of meritorious action: giving ([[Dāna]]), virtue ([[Sīla]]) and meditation ({{term|bhāvanā}}).[^an836]

## Ten bases

The commentaries expand these to ten:[^dhsa]

:::columns 2
1. Giving
2. Virtue
3. Meditation
4. Reverence
5. Service
6. Sharing merit
7. Rejoicing in others' merit
8. Listening to the Dhamma
9. Teaching the Dhamma
10. Straightening one's views
:::

## Sharing merit

Sharing merit ({{term|pattidāna}}) with departed relatives and all beings is a central practice of Theravāda life. The Tirokuṭṭa Sutta of the [[Petavatthu]] describes departed relatives standing outside walls and at crossroads, waiting for their families to give in their name and share the merit.[^pv] At the end of an offering, water is poured while reciting verses of dedication, and those present say *sādhu*.

## Merit and Nibbāna

Merit leads to happy rebirth but belongs to the round of existence. The arahant is described as having gone beyond both merit and evil. Yet merit is indispensable on the path, since virtue and concentration are its foundation.
