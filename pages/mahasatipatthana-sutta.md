The **Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta}}), "the Great Discourse on the Foundations of Mindfulness", is the twenty-second discourse of the [[Dīgha Nikāya]]. It is the fullest canonical teaching on the four foundations of mindfulness ([[Satipaṭṭhāna]]) and is among the most widely studied and recited suttas in the Theravāda world.[^dn22]

It begins with the declaration that this is the direct path ({{term|ekāyano maggo}}) for the purification of beings, for the surmounting of sorrow and lamentation, for the passing away of pain and grief, for the attainment of the true method and for the realisation of [[Nibbāna]].

## The four foundations

| Foundation | Contemplations |
|---|---|
| Body ({{term|kāya}}) | Breathing, postures, clear comprehension, the thirty-two parts, the four elements, the nine cemetery contemplations |
| Feeling ({{term|vedanā}}) | Pleasant, painful and neutral feelings, worldly and spiritual |
| Mind ({{term|citta}}) | The mind with or without lust, hatred and delusion, and other states |
| Phenomena ({{term|dhamma}}) | The five hindrances, five aggregates, six sense bases, seven factors of awakening and Four Noble Truths |

The section on the Four Noble Truths is much longer here than in the [[Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta]] of the Majjhima Nikāya, defining each item of each truth in detail.

## The promise

The sutta ends by declaring that anyone who develops the four foundations in this way for seven years, or even for seven days, may expect one of two fruits: final knowledge here and now or, if there is a trace of clinging left, the state of non-return.[^dn22]

[^dn22]: Dīgha Nikāya 22, *Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta*.
