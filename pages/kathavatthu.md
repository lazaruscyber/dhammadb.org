The **Kathāvatthu** ({{lang|Pāli|Kathāvatthu}}), "Points of Controversy", is the fifth book of the [[Abhidhamma Piṭaka]]. It consists of more than two hundred debates in which views contrary to the teaching are examined and refuted, each in the form of question and answer between an orthodox speaker and an opponent.

According to the Theravāda tradition, the Blessed One laid down its outline, foreseeing the disputes that would arise. At the [[Third Council]], held at Pāṭaliputta in the reign of [[Asoka]], the Venerable Moggaliputta Tissa expounded it in full to refute the wrong views that had entered the Saṅgha.[^sp][^as]

The first debate examines whether a person ({{term|puggala}}) exists as an ultimate reality; the orthodox speaker shows that it does not.
