The Venerable **Ānanda** ({{lang|Pāli|Ānanda}}) was the cousin of the Blessed One and served as the Blessed One's personal attendant for the last twenty-five years of the Teacher's life. He was declared foremost in five qualities: great learning, mindfulness, good conduct, resolution, and as an attendant.[^an1]

## Going forth

Ānanda went forth together with the Sakyan princes [[Anuruddha]], Bhaddiya, Bhagu, Kimbila and [[Devadatta]], and the barber [[Upāli]]. He attained stream-entry on hearing a teaching from the Venerable Puṇṇa Mantāniputta.[^vin]

## The attendant

In the twentieth year of the teaching, the Blessed One asked for a permanent attendant. Ānanda accepted on eight conditions: that he would not be given robes or food received by the Blessed One, nor share the Blessed One's dwelling, nor go with the Blessed One to invitations; but that he could bring visitors, ask the Blessed One to repeat any teaching given in his absence, and so on, so that no one could say he served for gain.[^tha]

## Keeper of the Dhamma

Through his great memory Ānanda learned all the teachings. In the [[Theragāthā]] he says he received eighty-two thousand teachings from the Buddha and two thousand from the bhikkhus.[^th] He interceded for the going forth of women, which led to the founding of the [[Bhikkhunī|order of nuns]].

## Arahantship and the Council

At the parinibbāna Ānanda was still a trainee. On the night before the [[First Council]] he strove hard and, as he was about to lie down, before his head touched the pillow and when his feet had left the ground, he attained arahantship. At the Council he recited the Dhamma, each sutta beginning with the words *evaṃ me sutaṃ*, "thus have I heard".[^vinc]
