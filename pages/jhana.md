**Jhāna** ({{lang|Pāli|jhāna}}) is meditative absorption. The commentaries explain the word in two ways: jhāna "contemplates" its object closely, and it "burns up" the hindrances.[^vism]

## The four jhānas

| Jhāna | Factors |
|---|---|
| First | Applied thought ({{term|vitakka}}), sustained thought ({{term|vicāra}}), rapture ({{term|pīti}}), happiness ({{term|sukha}}), one-pointedness ({{term|ekaggatā}}) |
| Second | Rapture, happiness, one-pointedness |
| Third | Happiness, one-pointedness |
| Fourth | Equanimity, one-pointedness |

The [[Abhidhamma Piṭaka|Abhidhamma]] also gives a fivefold scheme, in which applied thought and sustained thought are dropped in separate stages.[^abhs]

## The similes

The [[Sāmaññaphala Sutta]] describes the four jhānas with four similes: bath powder kneaded with water so that the moisture pervades it; a lake fed by a spring from within; lotuses steeped in water from root to tip; and a person wrapped from head to foot in a white cloth.[^dn2]

## Factors against the hindrances

Each factor of the first jhāna opposes a hindrance: one-pointedness opposes sensual desire, rapture opposes ill will, applied thought opposes sloth and torpor, happiness opposes restlessness and remorse, and sustained thought opposes doubt.[^vism]

## Immaterial attainments

Beyond the fourth jhāna lie the four immaterial attainments: the bases of infinite space, infinite consciousness, nothingness, and neither perception nor non-perception. They are sometimes counted as the fifth to eighth jhānas.

## Jhāna and the path

Right concentration, the eighth factor of the [[Noble Eightfold Path]], is defined in the suttas as the four jhānas.[^sn4508] Jhāna leads to rebirth in the [[Brahmā]] realms, and when made the basis for insight it supports the realisation of [[Nibbāna]].
