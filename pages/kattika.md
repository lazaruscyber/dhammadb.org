**Kattikā** ({{lang|Pāli|Kattikā}}) is the eighth month of the Theravāda lunar calendar in the traditional Pāli listing, falling in about October to November in the Gregorian calendar. Like every month of the calendar, it takes its name from the constellation ({{term|nakkhatta}}) near which the moon is full in that month. Because it comes after [[Assayuja]], it is also called the late Kattikā ({{term|pacchima-kattikā}}).[^abh] Its full moon, which ends the four months of the rains, is called in the canon the {{term|komudī}} full moon.[^dn2]

## In the calendar

Kattikā is the last month of the rainy season ({{term|vassāna}}). Bhikkhus who entered the later rains residence in [[Sāvaṇa]] complete it with the Pavāraṇā on the full moon of Kattikā.[^vin4] For those who kept the earlier residence, this month is the robe season, the time within which the kathina robe may be offered and made.[^vin7] It has 30 days, follows [[Assayuja]] and is followed by [[Māgasira]]. Like every month, it contains four uposatha observance days: the eighth day of the waxing moon, the full moon, the eighth day of the waning moon and the new moon.

## Events

According to the commentaries, the Venerable Sāriputta, chief disciple of the Blessed One, attained parinibbāna on the full-moon day of Kattikā in the room in which he was born, at his home village of Nālaka. The Venerable Mahāmoggallāna attained parinibbāna half a month later, on the new-moon day.[^sa4713][^mbv]
