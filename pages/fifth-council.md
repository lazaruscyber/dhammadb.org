The **Fifth Council** ({{lang|Pāli|Pañcama Saṅgāyana}}) was held in Mandalay, the royal capital of Burma, in 1871 under the patronage of King Mindon. It was presided over by the elders Jāgarābhivaṃsa, Narindābhidhaja and Sumaṅgalasāmī, and 2,400 bhikkhus took part.[^mbv]

## Purpose

King Mindon wished to preserve the Tipiṭaka in a form that would endure. Before the Council, scholars compared the manuscripts of the texts and prepared an edited text, which the Council then recited and approved over five months.

## The marble scriptures

The approved text was inscribed on 729 marble slabs, each housed in its own small shrine around the Kuthodaw Pagoda (the Mahālokamarajina pagoda) at the foot of Mandalay Hill. The inscription took several years. The slabs are written in the Burmese script, and their letters were originally filled with gold. Together they are often called the world's largest book.[^mbv]

## Significance

The Fifth Council text became the basis for later Burmese editions and for the preparations of the [[Sixth Council]]. It reflects the strong support of the Burmese kings for the Saṅgha and for the preservation of the Pāli canon.

[^mbv]: Mingun Sayadaw, *Mahābuddhavaṃsa* (The Great Chronicle of Buddhas).
