**Tisaraṇa** ({{lang|Pāli|tisaraṇa}}), "the three refuges", is the act of going for refuge to the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Saṅgha. It is the doorway to the teaching: by it one becomes a lay disciple ({{term|upāsaka}} or {{term|upāsikā}}), and it is recited at the beginning of almost every Theravāda ceremony.[^khp1]

## The formula

:::quote Khuddakapāṭha 1
*Buddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi.*
*Dhammaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi.*
*Saṅghaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi.*

I go to the Buddha for refuge. I go to the Dhamma for refuge. I go to the Saṅgha for refuge.
:::

Each line is repeated a second and third time, beginning *dutiyampi* and *tatiyampi*.

## History

The first lay disciples to go for refuge were the merchants Tapussa and Bhallika, who offered the Blessed One food soon after the awakening; since the Saṅgha did not yet exist, they took refuge in two jewels only. The first to go for refuge in all three was the father of Yasa, at [[Isipatana]].[^vin] In the early period, going for refuge three times was also the form of the going forth ({{term|pabbajjā}}).

## Purity of the refuge

The commentaries explain that the refuge is broken by taking another teacher as one's refuge, and is defiled by ignorance, doubt and wrong understanding of the Three Jewels. The refuge of the noble disciple who has attained stream-entry ([[Sotāpanna]]) is unshakeable.[^khpa]
