**Buddha** ({{lang|Pāli|buddha}}) means "one who is awakened". A Fully Enlightened Buddha ({{term|sammāsambuddha}}) is one who, without a teacher, has penetrated the [[Four Noble Truths]] by their own effort, attained [[Nibbāna]], and then established the teaching for the welfare of many. The Buddha of the present age is the Blessed One, Gotama of the Sakyan clan.[^an1]

## The Buddha of this age

The Bodhisatta was born as the prince Siddhattha in [[Lumbinī]], the son of King [[Suddhodana]] of [[Kapilavatthu]]. After the four sights the Bodhisatta went forth at the age of twenty-nine, practised severe austerities for six years, and gave them up as not leading to awakening. Seated beneath the Bodhi tree at [[Uruvelā]] on the full moon of [[Vesākha]], the Bodhisatta attained full awakening at the age of thirty-five.[^jn] The Blessed One then taught for forty-five years and attained final Nibbāna at [[Kusinārā]] at the age of eighty.[^dn16]

## Kinds of Buddha

The commentaries distinguish the Fully Enlightened Buddha, who awakens alone and teaches; the Paccekabuddha, who awakens alone but does not establish a dispensation; and the disciple Buddha ({{term|sāvakabuddha}}), the arahant who awakens by hearing the teaching. The [[Buddhavaṃsa]] tells of twenty-four Buddhas before Gotama under whom the Bodhisatta fulfilled the [[Pāramī|perfections]], and the [[Mahāpadāna Sutta]] names the seven Buddhas of the last ninety-one aeons.

## Epithets

In the suttas the Buddha is most often called {{term|Bhagavā}} (the Blessed One), and refers to the Buddha's own person as {{term|Tathāgata}}, "thus gone" or "thus come". Other epithets include the Teacher ({{term|Satthā}}), the Sage of the Sakyans ({{term|Sakyamuni}}) and the Fully Enlightened One.
