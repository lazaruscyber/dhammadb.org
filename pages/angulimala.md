The Venerable **Aṅgulimāla** ({{lang|Pāli|Aṅgulimāla}}), "garland of fingers", was a murderer in the kingdom of Kosala who became a bhikkhu under the Blessed One and attained arahantship. His story is told in the [[Aṅgulimāla Sutta]].[^mn86]

## Background

According to the commentary he was born Ahiṃsaka, "harmless", the son of a chaplain of King Pasenadi. He studied at Takkasilā and excelled, but jealous fellow students turned his teacher against him. The teacher demanded as his fee a thousand human fingers, intending that Ahiṃsaka be killed in the attempt. He became a bandit in the Jālinī forest, wearing the fingers of his victims as a garland.[^mna]

## Meeting the Blessed One

When he had nine hundred and ninety-nine fingers, his own mother set out to warn him, and he was about to kill her. The Blessed One, seeing this, went to meet him first. Though he ran with all his strength he could not catch up with the Blessed One walking at a normal pace, and on hearing "I have stopped, Aṅgulimāla; you too stop", he threw away his weapons and asked for the going forth.[^mn86] When King Pasenadi came with an army to capture him, he found the bandit a peaceful bhikkhu at the Blessed One's side.

## The Paritta

Aṅgulimāla's act of truth for a woman in labour became the Aṅgulimāla [[Paritta]].

## His verses

His verses in the [[Theragāthā]] include: "Whoever was heedless before and afterwards is not heedless, lights up this world like the moon released from a cloud. Whoever's evil deed is covered by the wholesome, lights up this world like the moon released from a cloud."[^th]
