The Venerable **Mahāmoggallāna** ({{lang|Pāli|Mahāmoggallāna}}) was the second chief disciple of the Blessed One, foremost among the bhikkhus in supernormal power ({{term|iddhi}}).[^an1]

## Early life and ordination

Born Kolita in a brahmin family near [[Rājagaha]], he was the childhood friend of Upatissa, later [[Sāriputta]]. The two went forth together under Sañjaya and then, after Sāriputta met the Venerable Assaji, came together to the Blessed One.[^vin]

## Struggle with drowsiness

Moggallāna attained arahantship seven days after ordination, while meditating at Kallavāḷaputta. The Pacalā Sutta tells how the Blessed One came to him when he was nodding and taught him eight ways to overcome drowsiness: change one's perception, reflect on the Dhamma, recite it, pull the ears and rub the limbs, get up and wash the eyes, perceive light, walk up and down, and as a last resort lie down mindfully on the right side.[^an758]

## Powers

The suttas relate many deeds of his power: shaking Vejayanta palace in the Tāvatiṃsa heaven with his toe to humble Sakka; subduing the nāga king Nandopananda; and visiting the realms of hell and of ghosts to see the results of kamma, which the Blessed One then explained.[^mn37] The Blessed One compared him to a nurse who rears what Sāriputta brings to birth, training others to the highest goal.[^mn141]

## Death

According to the commentary, a band of ascetics hostile to the teaching hired thieves to kill him. Twice he escaped through his power, but the third time, recognising the ripening of an old evil kamma (killing his parents in a distant past life), he did not. His bones were broken, but he restored his body, took leave of the Blessed One, and attained final Nibbāna at Kāḷasilā.[^dhpa]
