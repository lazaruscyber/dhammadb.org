The **Brahmajāla Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Brahmajāla Sutta}}), "the Net of Brahmā", is the first discourse of the [[Dīgha Nikāya]]. It sets out sixty-two grounds for wrong views about the self and the world, and shows that the Tathāgata knows them all and has gone beyond them.[^dn1]

## Occasion

The wanderer Suppiya was travelling behind the Blessed One and the Saṅgha, speaking in dispraise of the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Saṅgha, while his pupil Brahmadatta spoke in their praise. When the bhikkhus discussed this, the Blessed One told them neither to be angry at dispraise nor elated at praise, but to explain what is untrue as untrue and what is true as true.[^dn1]

## Virtue

The Blessed One then said that an ordinary person praising the Tathāgata would speak only of virtue, and set out the short, middle and long sections on virtue ({{term|cūḷasīla}}, {{term|majjhimasīla}}, {{term|mahāsīla}}). These passages are repeated in many later suttas of the collection.

## The sixty-two views

| Concerning | Views | Number |
|---|---|---|
| The past | Eternalism, partial eternalism, finitude and infinity, evasion, fortuitous origination | 18 |
| The future | Survival with perception, without perception, with neither; annihilation; Nibbāna here and now | 44 |

All these views, the Blessed One explained, arise from feeling, conditioned by contact, in those who do not understand the origin and passing away of feelings. Just as a fisherman casting a fine net over a pond catches every creature larger than a certain size, so all who hold views about the past and future are caught in this net of sixty-two.[^dn1]

[^dn1]: Dīgha Nikāya 1, *Brahmajāla Sutta*.
