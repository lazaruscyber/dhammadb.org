The **Mahānidāna Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Mahānidāna Sutta}}), "the Great Discourse on Causation", is the fifteenth discourse of the [[Dīgha Nikāya]] and the longest treatment of [[Paṭicca-samuppāda|dependent origination]] in the collection.

## Occasion

The Venerable Ānanda remarked to the Blessed One how wonderful it was that dependent origination, though deep, appeared to him as clear as clear could be. The Blessed One answered: "Do not say so, Ānanda. This dependent origination is deep and appears deep. It is through not understanding, not penetrating this teaching that this generation has become like a tangled skein, and does not pass beyond the plane of misery, the bad destinations, the lower world, and the round of rebirth."[^dn15]

## Teaching

The sutta examines the links in turn by questioning each condition: if there were no birth, would ageing and death be discerned? It brings out especially the mutual dependence of consciousness ({{term|viññāṇa}}) and mentality-materiality ({{term|nāmarūpa}}), and traces how craving gives rise to seeking, acquisition, possessiveness and finally to the taking up of weapons, quarrels and lies.[^dn15]

It then analyses how views of self are formed and abandoned, describes the seven stations of consciousness and two bases, and ends with the eight liberations ({{term|vimokkha}}).

[^dn15]: Dīgha Nikāya 15, *Mahānidāna Sutta*.
