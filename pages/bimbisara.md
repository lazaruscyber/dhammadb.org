**Bimbisāra** ({{lang|Pāli|Bimbisāra}}) was the king of Magadha, ruling from [[Rājagaha]]. He was one of the earliest and most devoted royal supporters of the Blessed One.[^vin]

## The first meeting

The Pabbajjā Sutta tells how the Bodhisatta, newly gone forth, walked for alms in Rājagaha. King Bimbisāra saw the Bodhisatta's bearing from his palace and sent men to follow. He offered the Bodhisatta a share in his kingdom, which was declined. He then asked the Bodhisatta to return to Rājagaha after attaining awakening.[^snp]

## Stream-entry and the Bamboo Grove

After the awakening, the Blessed One came to Rājagaha with the thousand bhikkhus converted at Uruvelā, among them the Kassapa brothers. Bimbisāra came with a great retinue, heard the Dhamma and attained stream-entry. He invited the Blessed One for a meal, and afterwards poured water over the Blessed One's hand to give the Bamboo Grove ([[Veḷuvana]]) to the Saṅgha: the first monastery.[^vin]

## The Uposatha

It was at Bimbisāra's suggestion that the Blessed One established the [[Uposatha]] observance for the Saṅgha, as other sects gathered on those days to teach.[^vin2]

## Death

According to the commentary, he was imprisoned and starved by his son Ajātasattu, who had been incited by [[Devadatta]]. He survived by walking up and down in his cell in the bliss of the fruit of stream-entry until his feet were cut, and he died. He was reborn as the deva Janavasabha, as told in the Janavasabha Sutta.[^dn18]
