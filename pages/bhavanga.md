**Bhavaṅga** ({{lang|Pāli|bhavaṅga}}) is the life-continuum: the stream of resultant consciousness that flows throughout life whenever no active cognitive process ([[Vīthi]]) is taking place, as in dreamless sleep.[^abhs]

## Function

Bhavaṅga is one of the fourteen functions of consciousness. The same type of resultant consciousness performs three functions in one life: rebirth-linking ([[Paṭisandhi]]) at the first moment, bhavaṅga throughout life, and death ({{term|cuti}}) at the last moment. Its object is also the same throughout: the object presented at the end of the previous life.[^abhs]

## In the cognitive process

When an object strikes a sense door, the bhavaṅga vibrates for one moment ({{term|bhavaṅga-calana}}) and is arrested ({{term|bhavaṅgupaccheda}}), after which the cognitive process begins. When the process ends, consciousness lapses back into bhavaṅga.

## The mango simile

The commentaries illustrate the process with a man sleeping under a mango tree. A mango falls and grazes his ear (the object striking the sense organ). He wakes (adverting), opens his eyes and looks (seeing), picks up the fruit (receiving), squeezes it (investigating), smells it (determining), eats it ([[Javana|impulsion]]), swallows the last morsels (registration), and lies down to sleep again (bhavaṅga).[^dhsa]

## Brightness of mind

The commentaries identify the "luminous mind" of the Aṅguttara Nikāya, defiled by adventitious defilements, with bhavaṅga.[^an1]
