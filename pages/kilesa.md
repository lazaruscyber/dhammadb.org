**Kilesa** ({{lang|Pāli|kilesa}}) means a defilement: a state that soils and torments the mind. The commentaries explain that the defilements are so called because they afflict the mind and the beings in whom they arise.[^vism]

## The ten defilements

The [[Dhammasaṅgaṇī]] and the [[Vibhaṅga]] list ten defilements:[^dhs]

:::columns 2
1. Greed ({{term|lobha}})
2. Hatred ({{term|dosa}})
3. Delusion ({{term|moha}})
4. Conceit ({{term|māna}})
5. Wrong view ({{term|diṭṭhi}})
6. Doubt ({{term|vicikicchā}})
7. Sloth ({{term|thīna}})
8. Restlessness ({{term|uddhacca}})
9. Shamelessness ({{term|ahirika}})
10. Fearlessness of wrongdoing ({{term|anottappa}})
:::

## Three levels

The commentaries describe the defilements at three levels, each abandoned by one of the three trainings:

| Level | Pāli | Abandoned by |
|---|---|---|
| Transgression in body and speech | {{term|vītikkama}} | [[Sīla|Virtue]] |
| Arising in the mind | {{term|pariyuṭṭhāna}} | [[Samādhi|Concentration]] |
| Latent, as a tendency | {{term|anusaya}} | [[Paññā|Wisdom]] |

Virtue abandons defilements temporarily by restraint, concentration suppresses them, and the wisdom of the noble path cuts them off so that they can never arise again.[^vism]

## Related sets

Other classifications of defilements include the taints ([[Āsava]]), the hindrances ([[Nīvaraṇa]]), the fetters ([[Saṃyojana]]), the seven latent tendencies, and the sixteen imperfections of the mind in the [[Vatthūpama Sutta]].

[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapter XXII.
[^dhs]: *Dhammasaṅgaṇī*, Kilesagocchaka.
