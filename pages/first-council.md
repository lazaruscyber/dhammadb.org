The **First Council** ({{lang|Pāli|Paṭhama Saṅgāyana}}), "the first recitation", was held three months after the parinibbāna of the Blessed One. Five hundred arahants recited the Dhamma and the Vinaya so that the teaching would be preserved.[^vin]

## Cause

As the Venerable [[Mahākassapa]] was travelling to [[Kusinārā]], he heard the old bhikkhu Subhadda say: "Enough, friends, do not grieve. We are well rid of that great recluse. We were troubled by being told 'this is allowed, this is not allowed'. Now we can do what we like." Mahākassapa resolved to hold a recitation before what is not Dhamma could prosper and the Dhamma be eclipsed.[^vin]

## Preparation

Mahākassapa selected 499 arahants and kept one place for the Venerable [[Ānanda]], who was still a trainee but had heard more of the teaching than anyone. Ānanda attained arahantship on the night before the Council began. King Ajātasattu had a hall built at the entrance to the Sattapaṇṇi cave on the Vebhāra hill outside [[Rājagaha]].[^vin]

## The recitation

| Collection | Questioned by | Recited by |
|---|---|---|
| [[Vinaya Piṭaka|Vinaya]] | [[Mahākassapa]] | [[Upāli]] |
| Dhamma (the [[Sutta Piṭaka|Suttas]]) | Mahākassapa | [[Ānanda]] |

For each rule Mahākassapa asked where it was laid down, concerning whom, on what matter, and what were the rule, the additions and the offences. For each sutta he asked where it was spoken and concerning whom.[^vin] The commentary says the Abhidhamma was also recited, and that the Nikāyas were entrusted to groups of reciters ({{term|bhāṇaka}}) for preservation.[^dha]

## The lesser and minor rules

Ānanda reported that the Blessed One had said the Saṅgha might abolish the lesser and minor rules. Since he had not asked which these were, and the elders disagreed, Mahākassapa proposed that no rules be abolished and none added. The Saṅgha agreed. This decision is the basis of Theravāda conservatism in the Vinaya.[^vin]

## Purāṇa

The elder Purāṇa, arriving with his followers after the Council, said that it had been well recited, but that he would remember the teaching as he had heard it from the Blessed One.

[^vin]: Vinaya Piṭaka, Cūḷavagga 11, *Pañcasatikakkhandhaka*.
[^dha]: *Sumaṅgalavilāsinī*, the commentary to the Dīgha Nikāya, introduction.
