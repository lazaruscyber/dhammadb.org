**Kusinārā** ({{lang|Pāli|Kusinārā}}) was a small town of the Mallas, where the Blessed One attained final Nibbāna. It is the fourth of the four places of pilgrimage named in the [[Mahāparinibbāna Sutta]].[^dn16]

## Choice of the place

When the Venerable [[Ānanda]] asked the Blessed One not to pass away in such a small town in the jungle, but in a great city such as [[Rājagaha]] or [[Sāvatthī]], the Blessed One told him that Kusinārā had once been Kusāvatī, the royal capital of the righteous king Mahāsudassana, rich and prosperous, and that the Blessed One had been that king in a past life. This is told at length in the Mahāsudassana Sutta.[^dn17]

## The parinibbāna

The Blessed One lay down on the right side between the twin sāla trees in the Upavattana grove of the Mallas, on a couch with the head to the north. The trees burst into blossom out of season. During the last watch of the night, after the final words, the Blessed One passed through the jhānas and attained final Nibbāna from the fourth jhāna.[^dn16]

## The cremation

The Mallas honoured the body with music, dance, garlands and perfumes for six days, and cremated it at the Makuṭabandhana shrine east of the town. The relics were later divided among eight claimants.[^dn16]

[^dn16]: Dīgha Nikāya 16, *Mahāparinibbāna Sutta*.
[^dn17]: Dīgha Nikāya 17, *Mahāsudassana Sutta*.
