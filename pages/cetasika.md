A **cetasika** ({{lang|Pāli|cetasika}}) is a mental factor: a mental state that arises and ceases together with [[Citta|consciousness]], has the same object and the same base. The [[Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha]] lists fifty-two.[^abhs]

## The four characteristics

Every mental factor arises together with consciousness, ceases together with it, has the same object, and has the same physical base.[^abhs]

## Classification

| Group | Number | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Universals ({{term|sabbacittasādhāraṇa}}) | 7 | Contact, feeling, perception, volition, one-pointedness, life faculty, attention |
| Occasionals ({{term|pakiṇṇaka}}) | 6 | Applied thought, sustained thought, decision, energy, rapture, desire |
| Unwholesome ({{term|akusala}}) | 14 | Delusion, shamelessness, fearlessness of wrong, restlessness, greed, wrong view, conceit, hatred, envy, avarice, remorse, sloth, torpor, doubt |
| Beautiful ({{term|sobhana}}) | 25 | Faith, mindfulness, shame, fear of wrong, non-greed, non-hatred, neutrality, the six pairs of tranquillity and so on, the three abstinences, compassion, appreciative joy, wisdom |

The first thirteen are called "common to others" ({{term|aññasamāna}}), because they take on the wholesome or unwholesome quality of the consciousness they accompany.[^abhs]

## Relation to the aggregates

In terms of the five aggregates, feeling is the aggregate of feeling, perception the aggregate of perception, and the remaining fifty are the aggregate of formations.

## Combinations

The second chapter of the Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha sets out which factors combine with which types of consciousness ({{term|sampayoga}}) and which types of consciousness each factor joins ({{term|saṅgaha}}).[^abhs]

[^abhs]: Anuruddha, *Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha*. Chapter II.
