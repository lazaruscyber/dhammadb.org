The **nīvaraṇas** ({{lang|Pāli|nīvaraṇa}}) are the five hindrances: states that obstruct the mind, weaken wisdom, and prevent concentration and [[Jhāna|jhāna]].[^sn4651]

## The five hindrances and their similes

The [[Sāmaññaphala Sutta]] compares the hindrances to five kinds of hardship, and their abandoning to release from them. The Saṅgārava Sutta compares them to five kinds of water in which one cannot see one's face reflected.[^sn46]

| Hindrance | Pāli | Like a debt, a bowl of water... |
|---|---|---|
| Sensual desire | {{term|kāmacchanda}} | A debt; water mixed with dye |
| Ill will | {{term|byāpāda}} | Illness; water boiling over a fire |
| Sloth and torpor | {{term|thīnamiddha}} | Imprisonment; water covered with moss |
| Restlessness and remorse | {{term|uddhaccakukkucca}} | Slavery; water stirred by wind |
| Doubt | {{term|vicikicchā}} | A dangerous desert road; muddy water in the dark |

## Nutriment and starvation

The Blessed One taught that each hindrance has its nutriment, which makes it grow, and its denourishment, which makes it decline. Sensual desire is nourished by unwise attention to the sign of beauty and starved by wise attention to the sign of foulness; ill will is nourished by the sign of aversion and starved by the liberation of mind through loving-kindness.[^sn4651]

## Abandoning

The hindrances are suppressed by the first jhāna, the factors of which are their direct opposites, and finally abandoned by the noble paths. They are the first object of the contemplation of phenomena in the [[Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta]].

[^sn4651]: Saṃyutta Nikāya 46.51, *Āhāra Sutta*.
[^sn46]: Saṃyutta Nikāya 46.55, *Saṅgārava Sutta*.
