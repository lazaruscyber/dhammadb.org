The **satta visuddhi** ({{lang|Pāli|satta visuddhi}}) are the seven stages of purification. They are named in the [[Rathavinīta Sutta]], where the Venerable [[Puṇṇa Mantāniputta]] compared them to seven relay chariots, and form the framework of the [[Visuddhimagga]].[^mn24]

## The seven purifications

| No. | Purification | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Of virtue ({{term|sīla-visuddhi}}) | The fourfold purity of virtue |
| 2 | Of mind ({{term|citta-visuddhi}}) | Access and absorption concentration |
| 3 | Of view ({{term|diṭṭhi-visuddhi}}) | Defining mentality-materiality |
| 4 | By overcoming doubt ({{term|kaṅkhāvitaraṇa-visuddhi}}) | Discerning the conditions of mentality-materiality |
| 5 | By knowledge and vision of what is and is not the path | Overcoming the imperfections of insight |
| 6 | By knowledge and vision of the way | The nine insight knowledges |
| 7 | By knowledge and vision ({{term|ñāṇadassana-visuddhi}}) | The four noble paths |

## In the Visuddhimagga

The Visuddhimagga is arranged under virtue, concentration and wisdom. Its first part covers the first purification; its second part, the second purification; and its third part, the remaining five, which are called the trunk of wisdom, with the aggregates, bases, elements, faculties, truths and dependent origination as its soil.[^vism]

## Purpose

As in the relay chariot simile, each stage is a means for reaching the next, and none is itself the goal. The goal is final [[Nibbāna]] without clinging. See also [[Vipassanā ñāṇa]].

[^mn24]: Majjhima Nikāya 24, *Rathavinīta Sutta*.
[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapter XVIII to XXII.
