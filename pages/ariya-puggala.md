An **ariya-puggala** ({{lang|Pāli|ariya-puggala}}) is a noble person, one who has attained one of the supramundane paths or fruits. The noble Saṅgha is "the four pairs of persons, the eight types of individuals".[^an89]

## The eight noble persons

| Stage | Path | Fruit | Fetters abandoned |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Sotāpanna|Stream-entry]] | 1 | 2 | Personality view, doubt, clinging to rules and observances |
| [[Sakadāgāmī|Once-return]] | 3 | 4 | Sensual desire and ill will weakened |
| [[Anāgāmī|Non-return]] | 5 | 6 | Sensual desire and ill will abandoned |
| [[Arahant|Arahantship]] | 7 | 8 | All ten fetters ([[Saṃyojana]]) |

## Path and fruit

In the [[Abhidhamma Piṭaka|Abhidhamma]], each path ({{term|magga}}) is a single moment of supramundane consciousness that cuts off defilements, immediately followed by two or three moments of its fruit ({{term|phala}}), which experiences the peace of [[Nibbāna]]. Later the noble person can re-enter the attainment of fruition.[^abhs]

## Worldlings and trainees

All who have not attained a path are worldlings ({{term|puthujjana}}). The first seven noble persons are called trainees ({{term|sekha}}), and the arahant is one beyond training ({{term|asekha}}).

The [[Puggalapaññatti]] also classifies noble persons by their faculties, such as the faith-follower and the Dhamma-follower, the one liberated in both ways and the one liberated by wisdom.

[^an89]: Aṅguttara Nikāya 8.59, *Puggala Sutta*.
[^abhs]: Anuruddha, *Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha*. Chapter IV.
