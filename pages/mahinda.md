The Venerable **Mahinda** ({{lang|Pāli|Mahinda}}) was the son of the emperor [[Asoka]]. He went forth at the age of twenty under the Venerable Moggaliputta Tissa and attained arahantship. After the [[Third Council]] he was sent to establish the teaching in Sri Lanka.[^mhv]

## Arrival

With four bhikkhus, the novice Sumana and the lay follower Bhaṇḍuka, Mahinda travelled through the air and alighted on the Missaka mountain, today Mihintale, on the full moon of [[Jeṭṭha]]. King Devānampiya Tissa was hunting a deer there. Mahinda called him by name, and to test his understanding asked him the riddle of the mango tree: "What is this tree called? Is there another mango tree besides it? Are there other trees besides these mangoes? Is there another tree besides the other mangoes and the other trees?" The king answered, "This very tree." Mahinda then taught the Cūḷahatthipadopama Sutta, and the king and forty thousand followers took refuge.[^mhv]

## Establishing the teaching

Mahinda taught in [[Anurādhapura]], where the king gave the Mahāmegha park, which became the site of the Mahāvihāra. He had his sister [[Saṅghamittā]] sent from India to ordain the women, bringing a branch of the Bodhi tree. The first Sri Lankan bhikkhus were ordained, and Ariṭṭha, the king's nephew, recited the Vinaya, which marked the firm establishment of the teaching on the island.[^mhv]

## Commentaries

According to the tradition preserved in the [[Samantapāsādikā]], Mahinda brought the commentaries to Sri Lanka and translated them into Sinhala. [[Buddhaghosa]] later rendered them back into Pāli.[^sp] In Sri Lanka the full moon of Jeṭṭha is celebrated as Poson in his honour.
