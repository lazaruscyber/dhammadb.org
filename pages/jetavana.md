The **Jetavana** ({{lang|Pāli|Jetavana}}), "Jeta's Grove", in full Jetavana Anāthapiṇḍikārāma, was the monastery at [[Sāvatthī]] where the Blessed One spent nineteen rains residences, more than at any other place.[^dhpa] Hundreds of suttas begin with the words "On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Sāvatthī in Jeta's Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika's Park."

## The purchase

[[Anāthapiṇḍika]] wished to build a monastery for the Blessed One near Sāvatthī, neither too far from nor too near the city. Prince Jeta's park was suitable, but the prince, unwilling to sell, named the price of as many gold coins as would cover the ground. Anāthapiṇḍika agreed and had the coins brought in carts and laid edge to edge. When a small area by the gate remained uncovered, Jeta, moved by his faith, gave it himself and built a gatehouse there. The park was named after both.[^vin]

## The monastery

Anāthapiṇḍika built dwellings, cells, storerooms, halls, wells, walking paths and a fragrant hut ({{term|gandhakuṭi}}) for the Blessed One. The commentaries say that Buddhas of the past also had monasteries on the same site.[^dhpa]

## The Ānanda Bodhi tree

According to the Kāliṅgabodhi Jātaka, so that people could pay respects in the Blessed One's absence, the Venerable [[Ānanda]] planted a seed from the Bodhi tree at Uruvelā at the gate of the Jetavana. It became known as the Ānanda Bodhi.[^ja]
