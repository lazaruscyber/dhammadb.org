**Paramattha** ({{lang|Pāli|paramattha}}) means the ultimate or highest sense. In the [[Abhidhamma Piṭaka|Abhidhamma]], it is used for the four ultimate realities ({{term|paramattha dhamma}}), which exist by their own nature and are not dependent on conceptual construction:[^abhs]

| Reality | Number of kinds |
|---|---|
| [[Citta|Consciousness]] | 89 (or 121) |
| [[Cetasika|Mental factors]] | 52 |
| [[Rūpa|Matter]] | 28 |
| [[Nibbāna]] | 1 |

## Two truths

The commentaries distinguish two kinds of truth: conventional truth ({{term|sammuti-sacca}}) and ultimate truth ({{term|paramattha-sacca}}). The Buddha taught in both ways: speaking of persons, beings, men and women in conventional terms, and of aggregates, elements and bases in ultimate terms. Both are true, each in its own sense.[^ppa]

## The chariot

In the [[Milindapañha]], the Venerable Nāgasena shows King Milinda that just as "chariot" is a name for the pole, axle, wheels and body, with no chariot apart from them, so "Nāgasena" is a name for the five aggregates and there is no person to be found in the ultimate sense.[^mil] This simile is based on the verses of the nun Vajirā in the [[Saṃyutta Nikāya]].

## Characteristics

Each ultimate reality has its own characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause, by which it is defined in the commentaries. See also [[Paññatti]].

[^abhs]: Anuruddha, *Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha*. Chapter I.
[^ppa]: *Puggalapaññatti* commentary; Kathāvatthu commentary.
[^mil]: *Milindapañha*, the chariot simile; Saṃyutta Nikāya 5.10, *Vajirā Sutta*.
