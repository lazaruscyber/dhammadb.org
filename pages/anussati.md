**Anussati** ({{lang|Pāli|anussati}}) means recollection: repeated mindfulness of a chosen subject. The [[Visuddhimagga]] describes ten recollections among the forty meditation subjects.[^vism]

## The ten recollections

| Recollection | Subject |
|---|---|
| {{term|buddhānussati}} | The nine qualities of the [[Buddha]] |
| {{term|dhammānussati}} | The six qualities of the [[Dhamma]] |
| {{term|saṅghānussati}} | The nine qualities of the [[Saṅgha]] |
| {{term|sīlānussati}} | One's own virtue |
| {{term|cāgānussati}} | One's own generosity |
| {{term|devatānussati}} | The qualities of [[Deva|devas]], present also in oneself |
| {{term|maraṇassati}} | Death |
| {{term|kāyagatāsati}} | The thirty-two parts of the body |
| {{term|ānāpānasati}} | [[Ānāpānasati|Breathing]] |
| {{term|upasamānussati}} | Peace, the qualities of [[Nibbāna]] |

## Depth of concentration

Of the ten, eight lead only to access concentration, because their objects are many qualities or because recollecting them is deep. Mindfulness of the body and of breathing can lead to [[Jhāna|jhāna]].[^vism]

## Recollection of the Buddha

The [[Dhajagga Sutta]] teaches recollection of the Buddha, Dhamma and Saṅgha as a protection against fear. The Visuddhimagga explains that one who is devoted to recollecting the Buddha becomes full of faith, mindfulness, understanding and merit, and comes to feel as if living in the Teacher's presence.[^vism]

## Recollection of death

Recollection of death is practised by reflecting "death will take place, the life faculty will be cut off", or in eight ways: as a murderer, as the ruin of success, by comparison with others, as shared by the body with many, as the frailty of life, as signless, as the limited span, and as the shortness of the moment.[^vism]

[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapter VII and VIII.
