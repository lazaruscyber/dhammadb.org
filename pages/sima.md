A **sīmā** ({{lang|Pāli|sīmā}}) is a boundary. In the Vinaya it is the area within which the bhikkhus of a single community ({{term|samānasaṃvāsa}}) carry out formal acts ({{term|saṅghakamma}}) together, such as the [[Uposatha]], the [[Pavāraṇā]] and the higher ordination ([[Upasampadā]]).[^vin]

## Kinds of boundary

| Kind | Description |
|---|---|
| Established boundary ({{term|baddhasīmā}}) | Marked by the Saṅgha with boundary markers ({{term|nimitta}}) by a formal act |
| Village boundary ({{term|gāmasīmā}}) | The natural extent of a village or town, where no sīmā has been established |
| Water boundary ({{term|udakukkhepasīmā}}) | On a river or lake, the distance a man of average strength can throw water |
| Forest boundary | Seven abbhantaras (about 200 metres) in a forest |

## Establishing a sīmā

Marks such as stones, hills, trees, paths, anthills or rivers are announced, and the Saṅgha establishes the boundary by a formal act with motion and proclamation. The markers must be announced all round so that the boundary is enclosed. All bhikkhus within the sīmā must be present or send their consent, or the act is invalid.[^vin]

## Ordination halls

In Myanmar, ordination halls ({{term|thein}}) are built on established boundaries, with boundary stones placed around them. The proper establishment of sīmās has been a central concern of Vinaya scholars, since a defective boundary would make ordinations invalid.

[^vin]: Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga 2, *Uposathakkhandhaka*.
