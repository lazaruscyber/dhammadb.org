**Vipassanā** ({{lang|Pāli|vipassanā}}) means insight, literally "seeing in various ways". It is the direct seeing of mentality and materiality ({{term|nāmarūpa}}) with their [[Tilakkhaṇa|three characteristics]]: [[Anicca|impermanence]], [[Dukkha|suffering]] and [[Anattā|non-self]]. Insight is the second of the two kinds of meditation, together with tranquillity ([[Samatha]]), and leads to the wisdom of the noble path.[^vism]

## Basis

The foundation of insight is the four foundations of mindfulness ([[Satipaṭṭhāna]]). The [[Visuddhimagga]] sets out the development of insight in the framework of the seven purifications ([[Satta visuddhi]]) and the stages of insight knowledge ([[Vipassanā ñāṇa]]).[^vism]

## Two vehicles

The commentaries speak of two kinds of practitioner:

| Kind | Pāli | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| One with tranquillity as vehicle | {{term|samathayānika}} | First attains access or jhāna, then develops insight |
| One with bare insight | {{term|suddhavipassanāyānika}} | Develops insight directly, with momentary concentration ({{term|khaṇika-samādhi}}) |

The second approach was taught especially by the [[Mahāsi Sayadaw]], who based the method on the [[Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta]] and its commentary.

## The imperfections of insight

The Visuddhimagga describes ten imperfections of insight ({{term|vipassanūpakkilesa}}) that may arise at the knowledge of rise and fall, such as illumination, rapture, tranquillity and strong faith. They are not themselves defilements, but attachment to them obstructs progress. Seeing that they are not the path is the purification by knowledge and vision of what is and what is not the path.[^vism]

[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapter XVIII to XXII.
