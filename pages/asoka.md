**Asoka** ({{lang|Pāli|Asoka}}), known in the chronicles as Dhammāsoka, "Asoka of the Dhamma", was the emperor of India ruling from Pāṭaliputta. The [[Mahāvaṃsa]] places his consecration 218 years after the parinibbāna.[^mhv]

## Conversion

According to the chronicles, Asoka seized the throne after killing many of his brothers and was known as Caṇḍāsoka, "Asoka the fierce". He was converted by the young novice Nigrodha, whose calm bearing moved him; on hearing the novice recite the [[Dhammapada]] verses on heedfulness, he took refuge.[^mhv]

## Patronage

The chronicles say he built 84,000 monasteries and stūpas, one for each section of the Dhamma, and gave lavishly to the Saṅgha. When he asked the Venerable Moggaliputta Tissa whether he was now an heir of the Dhamma, the elder answered that only one who lets a son or daughter go forth is an heir of the Dhamma. Asoka then allowed his son [[Mahinda]] and daughter [[Saṅghamittā]] to go forth.[^mhv]

## The Third Council and the missions

Many heretics joined the Saṅgha for gain, and the bhikkhus refused to hold the Uposatha with them for seven years. Asoka invited Moggaliputta Tissa to purify the Saṅgha: those who held wrong views were expelled, and the elder confirmed that the Blessed One was a {{term|vibhajjavādī}}, "one who teaches with analysis". The [[Third Council]] followed. Afterwards, missions were sent to nine regions, including Mahinda to Sri Lanka and the elders Soṇa and Uttara to Suvaṇṇabhūmi.[^sp]

## Inscriptions

Asoka's edicts, carved on rocks and pillars across India, call for righteousness, non-harming, respect for parents and teachers, and generosity. The Bhābrā edict names several suttas for the bhikkhus and laity to study.
