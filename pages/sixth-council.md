The **Sixth Council** ({{lang|Pāli|Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana}}) was held at Yangon, Burma, from the full moon of [[Vesākha]] 1954 to the full moon of Vesākha 1956, which was the 2,500th year after the parinibbāna according to the Theravāda reckoning. Bhikkhus from Burma, Sri Lanka, Thailand, Cambodia and Laos took part.[^ms]

## The Great Cave

For the Council the government built the Mahāpāsāṇa Guhā, "the Great Stone Cave", an artificial hall modelled on the Sattapaṇṇi cave of the [[First Council]], near the Kabā Aye pagoda. It could seat 2,500 bhikkhus and many lay people.

## Proceedings

Before the Council, the texts of the Tipiṭaka in the Burmese, Sinhala, Thai and Cambodian editions were compared, and a single edited text prepared. At the Council, in the manner of the First Council, the [[Mahāsi Sayadaw]] put the questions and the [[Mingun Sayadaw]] answered, reciting from memory. Each section was then recited and approved by the assembly.[^ms]

The Council was presided over by the Nyaungyan Sayadaw, Abhidhaja Mahāraṭṭhaguru Revata, and was sponsored by the Burmese government under Prime Minister U Nu.

## The Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana edition

The approved text, together with the commentaries and sub-commentaries, was published in the Burmese script. This Sixth Council edition includes in the [[Khuddaka Nikāya]] the [[Milindapañha]], the Nettippakaraṇa and the Peṭakopadesa. It is the basis for the widely used digital edition prepared by the Vipassana Research Institute.
