A **bhikkhunī** ({{lang|Pāli|bhikkhunī}}) is a fully ordained nun. The Bhikkhunī Vibhaṅga of the [[Suttavibhaṅga]] gives the nuns' Pātimokkha, which has 311 rules.[^vin]

## Founding of the order

The [[Khandhaka]] records that [[Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī]], the Blessed One's foster mother, asked three times to be allowed to go forth and was refused. She then cut her hair, put on robes and followed the Blessed One to [[Vesālī]] with many Sakyan women. The Venerable [[Ānanda]] asked the Blessed One whether women were capable of realising the fruits of the path. The Blessed One said they were, and allowed the going forth on condition that Mahāpajāpatī accepted eight principles of respect ({{term|garudhamma}}).[^vin]

## Great nuns

The [[Therīgāthā]] preserves the verses of many arahant nuns. Among the foremost were [[Khemā]] and [[Uppalavaṇṇā]], the chief female disciples; [[Paṭācārā]], foremost in the Vinaya; Dhammadinnā, foremost among teachers of Dhamma (see [[Cūḷavedalla Sutta]]); and [[Kisāgotamī]], foremost in wearing coarse robes.[^an1]

## Later history

[[Saṅghamittā]], daughter of King [[Asoka]], brought the nuns' ordination to Sri Lanka.[^mhv] Higher ordination for nuns requires a dual ordination by both the bhikkhunī and the bhikkhu Saṅgha. According to the orthodox Theravāda position, the bhikkhunī lineage of the Theravāda died out, and it is held that it cannot be restored without an existing Theravāda bhikkhunī Saṅgha. Women today live the renunciate life as precept-holding nuns, such as the {{term|thilashin}} of Myanmar, who keep eight or ten precepts.
