The **Dhajagga Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Dhajagga Sutta}}), "the Top of the Banner", is the third discourse of the Sakka Saṃyutta in the [[Saṃyutta Nikāya]]. It is recited as a [[Paritta|protective discourse]] against fear and danger.[^sn113]

## The banner

The Blessed One told the bhikkhus that long ago, in a battle between the gods and the titans, Sakka, the king of the gods, told the gods that if fear arose in them they should look at the top of his banner, or the banners of the other kings of the gods, and their fear would be dispelled.

But the Blessed One added that this might or might not dispel fear, for Sakka is not free from lust, hatred and delusion; he is timid, fearful and liable to flee.[^sn113]

## The three recollections

Instead, the Blessed One said, when bhikkhus are in the forest, at the foot of a tree or in an empty hut and fear arises, they should recollect the Tathāgata: "Such indeed is the Blessed One, worthy, perfectly enlightened, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, well gone, knower of the worlds, unsurpassed trainer of persons to be tamed, teacher of gods and humans, enlightened, blessed." If they cannot recollect the Buddha, they should recollect the Dhamma; and if not the Dhamma, then the Saṅgha. Then fear will be dispelled, because the Tathāgata is free of lust, hatred and delusion, and is not timid, fearful or liable to flee.[^sn113]

These formulas of the qualities of the three jewels are the basis of the first three [[Anussati|recollections]] and are chanted daily throughout the Theravāda world.

[^sn113]: Saṃyutta Nikāya 11.3, *Dhajagga Sutta*.
