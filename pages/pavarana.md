**Pavāraṇā** ({{lang|Pāli|pavāraṇā}}), "invitation", is the ceremony that concludes the rains residence ([[Vassa]]). It is held on the full moon of [[Assayuja]] in place of the usual [[Uposatha]] recitation.[^vin]

## Origin

The Vinaya relates that some bhikkhus spent the rains in silence to avoid quarrels. The Blessed One criticised this as living like dumb sheep and laid down the Pavāraṇā instead.[^vin]

## The formula

Each bhikkhu, in order of seniority, kneels and says:

:::quote Vinaya, Mahāvagga 4
I invite the Saṅgha, friends, to speak to me on account of what has been seen, heard or suspected. Out of compassion, let the venerable ones speak to me; seeing it, I will make amends.
:::

He says this three times.

## The first Pavāraṇā of the Blessed One

In the Pavāraṇā Sutta, on the Pavāraṇā day the Blessed One sat with five hundred bhikkhus and invited them to find fault with any deed or word of the Blessed One. The Venerable [[Sāriputta]] answered that there was none. The Blessed One then invited them in turn, and declared that of the five hundred arahants, sixty had the three true knowledges, sixty the six direct knowledges, sixty were liberated in both ways, and the rest liberated by wisdom.[^sn87]

## The festival of lights

In Myanmar the Pavāraṇā full moon is celebrated with lamps, recalling the descent of the Blessed One from the Tāvatiṃsa heaven after teaching the Abhidhamma to the devas during the rains.

[^sn87]: Saṃyutta Nikāya 8.7, *Pavāraṇā Sutta*.
