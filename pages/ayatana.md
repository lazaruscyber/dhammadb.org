The **āyatanas** ({{lang|Pāli|āyatana}}), "bases", are the twelve sense bases: six internal and six external. Together they are the "all" ({{term|sabba}}) of the Sabba Sutta: "What is the all? The eye and forms, the ear and sounds, the nose and odours, the tongue and tastes, the body and tangibles, the mind and mental objects. This is called the all."[^sn3523]

## The twelve bases

| Internal | External |
|---|---|
| Eye | Visible forms |
| Ear | Sounds |
| Nose | Odours |
| Tongue | Tastes |
| Body | Tangible objects |
| Mind | Mental objects |

When an internal and an external base meet, consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact ({{term|phassa}}), and with contact as condition there is feeling.[^sn3593]

## Place in the teaching

The six sense bases ({{term|saḷāyatana}}) are the fifth link of [[Paṭicca-samuppāda|dependent origination]]. The Saḷāyatana Saṃyutta of the [[Saṃyutta Nikāya]] is devoted to them and contains the [[Ādittapariyāya Sutta]]. The [[Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta]] includes the six bases and the fetters arising dependent on them among the contemplations of phenomena.

## Restraint

Restraint of the sense faculties ({{term|indriyasaṃvara}}) means that on seeing a form with the eye, one does not grasp at its signs and features, through which unwholesome states might invade the mind.[^dn2]
