**Māra** ({{lang|Pāli|māra}}), "the one who kills", is the Evil One who tries to keep beings bound to the round of rebirth. The suttas present Māra as a powerful deity of the highest sense-sphere heaven who opposed the Bodhisatta and the Blessed One.[^sn4]

## The five Māras

The commentaries explain five kinds of Māra:[^vism]

| Māra | Meaning |
|---|---|
| {{term|kilesamāra}} | The defilements |
| {{term|khandhamāra}} | The aggregates |
| {{term|abhisaṅkhāramāra}} | Kamma formations |
| {{term|maccumāra}} | Death |
| {{term|devaputtamāra}} | Māra as a deity |

The Blessed One is called the conqueror of all five.

## The defeat of Māra

On the night of the awakening, Māra came with a great army to drive the Bodhisatta from the seat beneath the Bodhi tree. The Bodhisatta called the earth to witness the perfections fulfilled through countless lives, and Māra's army fled.[^jn] In the [[Sutta Nipāta]], Māra's armies are named as sensual desire, discontent, hunger and thirst, craving, sloth and torpor, fear, doubt, hypocrisy and obstinacy, gain, fame and honour, and praising oneself while despising others.[^snp]

## Māra in the suttas

The Māra Saṃyutta of the [[Saṃyutta Nikāya]] records many attempts by Māra to distract the Blessed One and the bhikkhus, each ending with Māra realising "the Blessed One knows me" and vanishing.[^sn4] In the [[Mahāparinibbāna Sutta]], Māra requests the Blessed One to attain final Nibbāna.

[^sn4]: Saṃyutta Nikāya 4, *Māra Saṃyutta*.
[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapter VII.
[^jn]: Jātaka Commentary, *Nidānakathā*.
[^snp]: Sutta Nipāta 3.2, *Padhāna Sutta*.
