The **Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta}}), "the Discourse on the Foundations of Mindfulness", is the tenth discourse of the [[Majjhima Nikāya]]. Its content is the same as that of the [[Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta]] of the Dīgha Nikāya, except that the section on the Four Noble Truths is given in brief.[^mn]

It declares the four foundations of mindfulness ([[Satipaṭṭhāna]]) to be the direct path for the purification of beings and the realisation of [[Nibbāna]]. The bhikkhu dwells contemplating the body as a body, feelings as feelings, mind as mind, and phenomena as phenomena, ardent, clearly comprehending and mindful, having put away covetousness and grief for the world.

The commentary to this sutta in the [[Papañcasūdanī]] is one of the principal traditional sources for the practice of insight meditation, and the sutta forms the basis of the methods taught by the Mahāsi Sayadaw and other teachers of Myanmar.

[^mn]: Majjhima Nikāya 10, *Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta*.
