The **Aggivacchagotta Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Aggivacchagotta Sutta}}), "To Vacchagotta on Fire", is the seventy-second discourse of the [[Majjhima Nikāya]].

## The undeclared questions

The wanderer Vacchagotta asked the Blessed One whether the Blessed One held each of ten views: whether the world is eternal or not, finite or infinite, whether the soul and body are the same or different, and whether a Tathāgata exists after death, does not, both, or neither. The Blessed One rejected each, explaining that such views are a thicket, a wilderness and a fetter, accompanied by suffering, and do not lead to peace or Nibbāna.[^mn]

## The fire simile

Asked where a liberated bhikkhu reappears, the Blessed One answered that "reappears" does not apply, nor do the other alternatives. Vacchagotta was bewildered, and the Blessed One asked him: if a fire burning before him went out, and he were asked in which direction it had gone, east, west, north or south, what would he say? Vacchagotta answered that the question does not apply: the fire burned in dependence on grass and sticks, and when that fuel was used up it is simply reckoned as gone out.

In the same way, the Tathāgata has abandoned the form, feeling, perception, formations and consciousness by which one might describe the Tathāgata, and is deep, immeasurable and hard to fathom like the ocean.[^mn] Vacchagotta went for refuge.

[^mn]: Majjhima Nikāya 72, *Aggivacchagotta Sutta*.
