The **requisites** ({{lang|Pāli|paccaya}}) are the four supports of monastic life: robes ({{term|cīvara}}), almsfood ({{term|piṇḍapāta}}), lodging ({{term|senāsana}}) and medicine ({{term|gilānapaccaya-bhesajja}}). A bhikkhu depends on lay people to offer them.[^vin]

## Reflection on the requisites

The [[Sabbāsava Sutta]] teaches that the bhikkhu uses each requisite with wise reflection. For almsfood: "Reflecting wisely, I use almsfood neither for amusement, nor for intoxication, nor for beautification, but only for the endurance and continuance of this body, for ending discomfort, and for assisting the holy life." Similar reflections are given for robes, lodging and medicine. These are chanted daily in monasteries.[^mn2]

## The eight requisites

In Myanmar and other Theravāda countries the requisites offered at ordination are usually counted as eight ({{term|aṭṭhaparikkhāra}}):

:::columns 2
1. The three robes
2. The almsbowl
3. A razor
4. A needle and thread
5. A belt
6. A water strainer
:::

The three robes count as three items, making eight.

## Contentment

The Blessed One praised the bhikkhu who is content with whatever requisites are received, comparing him to a bird that flies with its wings as its only burden.[^dn2] The four noble lineages ({{term|ariyavaṃsa}}) are contentment with robes, contentment with almsfood, contentment with lodging, and delight in meditation.[^an428]
