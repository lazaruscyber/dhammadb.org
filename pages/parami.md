The **pāramīs** ({{lang|Pāli|pāramī}}) are the ten perfections. They are the qualities fulfilled by the Bodhisatta over four incalculable aeons and a hundred thousand aeons, from the time of the aspiration before the Buddha Dīpaṅkara until the last birth.[^bv]

## Three levels

Each perfection is fulfilled at three levels, making thirty in all: the ordinary perfection ({{term|pāramī}}), such as giving external possessions; the higher perfection ({{term|upapāramī}}), giving limbs and organs; and the ultimate perfection ({{term|paramatthapāramī}}), giving one's life.[^cpa]

## Sources

The ten perfections are named in the [[Buddhavaṃsa]], where the Bodhisatta Sumedha reflects on them in turn after receiving the prediction from the Buddha Dīpaṅkara. The [[Cariyāpiṭaka]] gives stories of the Bodhisatta's lives illustrating them, and the commentary to the Cariyāpiṭaka contains a full treatise on the perfections.[^cpa] The [[Jātaka]] tells many more such lives; the last ten, the Mahānipāta, are traditionally associated with ten perfections, culminating in the Vessantara Jātaka of generosity.

## For disciples

The perfections are not only for Bodhisattas: every disciple who reaches arahantship has fulfilled them to the degree that this goal requires.

[^cpa]: Dhammapāla, *Paramatthadīpanī*, the commentary to the Cariyāpiṭaka, *Pakiṇṇakakathā*.
