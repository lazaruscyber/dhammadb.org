The **Aṅgulimāla Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Aṅgulimāla Sutta}}) is the eighty-sixth discourse of the [[Majjhima Nikāya]]. It tells the story of [[Aṅgulimāla]], a murderer who wore a garland of his victims' fingers, and of his transformation through meeting the Blessed One.[^mn]

## "I have stopped"

When the Blessed One walked along the road towards Aṅgulimāla's haunt, he pursued with all his strength but could not catch up, though the Blessed One walked at an ordinary pace. He called out "Stop, recluse!", and the Blessed One answered: "I have stopped, Aṅgulimāla; you too should stop." The Blessed One explained that the Tathāgata had stopped forever from violence towards living beings, while Aṅgulimāla had not. Moved, Aṅgulimāla threw away his weapons and asked for the going forth.[^mn]

## The act of truth

As a bhikkhu, Aṅgulimāla saw a woman suffering in childbirth. At the Blessed One's instruction he made an act of truth: "Sister, since I was born with the noble birth, I do not recall intentionally depriving a living being of life. By this truth may you be well and may your child be well." The woman and child were safe. These words are recited as the [[Paritta|protective]] Aṅgulimāla Paritta, especially for expectant mothers.[^mn]

Aṅgulimāla soon attained arahantship, and endured patiently the stones and sticks thrown at him by people who remembered his past.
