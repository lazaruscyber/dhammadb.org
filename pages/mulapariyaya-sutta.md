The **Mūlapariyāya Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Mūlapariyāya Sutta}}), "the Root of All Things", is the first discourse of the [[Majjhima Nikāya]] and is known as one of the most profound.

The Blessed One describes how four kinds of person relate to twenty-four bases of experience, from earth, water, fire and air, through beings, gods and the Brahmā realms, to the seen, heard, sensed and cognized, unity and diversity, all, and even [[Nibbāna]]:[^mn]

| Person | Relation to things |
|---|---|
| The uninstructed worldling | Perceives them, conceives them, and delights in them, because they have not been fully understood |
| The trainee ({{term|sekha}}) | Directly knows them, should not conceive them or delight in them, so that they may be fully understood |
| The arahant | Directly knows them and does not conceive or delight in them, being free of lust, hatred and delusion |
| The Tathāgata | Directly knows them and does not conceive or delight in them, having understood that delight is the root of suffering |

At the end of the discourse, the sutta records, the bhikkhus did not delight in the Blessed One's words.

[^mn]: Majjhima Nikāya 1, *Mūlapariyāya Sutta*.
