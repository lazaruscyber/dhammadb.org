The **Girimānanda Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Girimānanda Sutta}}) is the sixtieth discourse of the Book of Tens in the [[Aṅguttara Nikāya]]. It is recited as a [[Paritta|protective discourse]] for the sick.[^an1060]

## Occasion

The Venerable Girimānanda was ill and in pain. The Venerable [[Ānanda]] asked the Blessed One to visit him. The Blessed One said that if Ānanda went and spoke the ten perceptions to Girimānanda, it was possible that on hearing them his illness would subside. Ānanda learned them and recited them, and Girimānanda recovered.[^an1060]

## The ten perceptions

:::columns 2
1. Perception of impermanence
2. Perception of non-self
3. Perception of unattractiveness of the body
4. Perception of danger, the many afflictions of the body
5. Perception of abandoning unwholesome thoughts
6. Perception of dispassion
7. Perception of cessation
8. Perception of non-delight in the whole world
9. Perception of impermanence in all formations
10. Mindfulness of breathing
:::

The tenth perception is described with the full sixteen steps of [[Ānāpānasati|mindfulness of breathing]], as in the [[Ānāpānassati Sutta]].

[^an1060]: Aṅguttara Nikāya 10.60, *Girimānanda Sutta*.
