**Anāthapiṇḍika** ({{lang|Pāli|Anāthapiṇḍika}}), "feeder of the helpless", was the name given to the banker Sudatta of [[Sāvatthī]] because of his generosity to the poor. The Blessed One declared him foremost among the male lay followers who are givers.[^an1]

## Meeting the Blessed One

On a business visit to [[Rājagaha]], Sudatta found his brother-in-law's household busy preparing a meal for "the Buddha and the Saṅgha". Hearing the word "Buddha", he was so eager that he rose three times in the night thinking it was dawn. Setting out through the dark to the Sītavana, he was frightened as the light faded, but a deva encouraged him. The Blessed One greeted him by name, "Come, Sudatta", and taught him the graduated talk; he attained stream-entry.[^vin]

## The Jetavana

He invited the Blessed One to Sāvatthī and sought a site. Prince Jeta, not wishing to sell his park, named a price of as many gold coins as would cover the ground. Anāthapiṇḍika had cartloads of coins brought and laid edge to edge. Moved, Jeta gave the remaining area by the gate and built the gatehouse himself. The [[Jetavana]] became the Blessed One's principal residence.[^vin]

## Teachings

Many suttas are addressed to him, on the right use of wealth, the four kinds of happiness of a householder, and the five benefits of giving. At his deathbed the Venerable [[Sāriputta]] taught him not to cling to anything (MN 143). He wept, saying he had never heard such a teaching before, and after death was reborn in the Tusita heaven.[^mn143]
