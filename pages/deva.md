A **deva** ({{lang|Pāli|deva}}), "shining one", is a being of the happy destinations above the human realm. Devas are reborn spontaneously, without parents, as the result of wholesome [[Kamma|kamma]], and enjoy long lives of great pleasure. They are nevertheless subject to death and rebirth.[^abhs]

## The six heavens

| Heaven | Ruler or description |
|---|---|
| Cātumahārājika | The realm of the Four Great Kings who guard the four quarters |
| Tāvatiṃsa | The heaven of the Thirty-three, ruled by Sakka |
| Yāma | The heaven of the Yāma gods |
| Tusita | The heaven of delight, where the Bodhisatta dwelt before the last birth |
| Nimmānarati | Gods who delight in their own creations |
| Paranimmitavasavatti | Gods who control what others create |

In a broader sense, beings of the [[Brahmā]] realms are also called devas. The commentaries speak of three kinds: devas by convention (kings), devas by birth, and devas by purity (the arahants and Buddhas).

## Devas in the teaching

Devas often appear in the suttas listening to the Dhamma, asking questions, and rejoicing at merit. The whole of the Devatā Saṃyutta of the [[Saṃyutta Nikāya]] consists of verses spoken by devas. In the [[Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta]], the devas of each realm in turn proclaim that the Wheel of the Dhamma has been set in motion.[^sn5611] The Blessed One is called the teacher of gods and humans.

## Recollection of devas

The recollection of devas ({{term|devatānussati}}) is one of the ten [[Anussati|recollections]]: one reflects that the devas were reborn there through faith, virtue, learning, generosity and wisdom, and that the same qualities are present in oneself.[^vism]

[^abhs]: Anuruddha, *Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha*. Chapter V.
[^sn5611]: Saṃyutta Nikāya 56.11, *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta*.
[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapter VII.
