The **Cūḷakammavibhaṅga Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Cūḷakammavibhaṅga Sutta}}), "the Shorter Exposition of Kamma", is the 135th discourse of the [[Majjhima Nikāya]].

The young brahmin Subha asked why human beings are seen to be inferior and superior: some short-lived and some long-lived, some sickly and some healthy, some ugly and some beautiful, some uninfluential and some influential, some poor and some rich, some low-born and some high-born, some stupid and some wise.[^mn]

The Blessed One answered:

:::quote Majjhima Nikāya 135
*Kammassakā, māṇava, sattā kammadāyādā kammayonī kammabandhū kammappaṭisaraṇā. Kammaṃ satte vibhajati yadidaṃ hīnappaṇītatāya.*

Beings are owners of their kamma, heirs of their kamma; they originate from their kamma, are bound to their kamma, have their kamma as their refuge. It is kamma that distinguishes beings as inferior and superior.
:::

## The seven pairs

| Result | Cause |
|---|---|
| Short life / long life | Killing / abstaining from killing |
| Sickliness / health | Injuring beings / not injuring beings |
| Ugliness / beauty | Anger / freedom from anger |
| Little influence / great influence | Envy / freedom from envy |
| Poverty / wealth | Not giving / giving |
| Low birth / high birth | Arrogance / humility and respect |
| Stupidity / wisdom | Not asking the wise / asking the wise what is wholesome |

[^mn]: Majjhima Nikāya 135, *Cūḷakammavibhaṅga Sutta*.
