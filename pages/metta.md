**Mettā** ({{lang|Pāli|mettā}}) is loving-kindness or friendliness: the wish for the welfare and happiness of beings. It is the first of the four [[Brahmavihāra|divine abidings]] and the ninth of the ten [[Pāramī|perfections]].[^vism]

## Development

The [[Visuddhimagga]] teaches that loving-kindness should first be developed towards oneself, as an example: "May I be happy and free from suffering." It is then developed towards a respected person such as a teacher, then a dear friend, then a neutral person, and finally a hostile person. The meditator breaks down the barriers between these until loving-kindness is equal towards all, including oneself. This is compared to bandits demanding one person for sacrifice: one who has broken the barriers could not choose any of them.[^vism]

At first one should not choose a person of the opposite sex or one who has died.

## Dealing with anger

When resentment arises towards a hostile person, the Visuddhimagga gives many methods: recalling the Blessed One's advice in the Kakacūpama Sutta that even if bandits were to sever one's limbs with a saw, one who gave way to hate would not be following the teaching; reflecting on the other's good qualities; reflecting on ownership of kamma; and resolving the person into elements.[^mn21]

## Eleven benefits

One who develops loving-kindness sleeps well, wakes happily, has no bad dreams, is dear to humans and non-humans, is protected by devas, is not harmed by fire, poison or weapons, concentrates quickly, has a serene face, dies unconfused, and if not attaining higher is reborn in the [[Brahmā]] world.[^an1116]

See also [[Karaṇīyametta Sutta]].
