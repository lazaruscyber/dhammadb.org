An **arahant** ({{lang|Pāli|arahant}}) is a "worthy one", one who has destroyed all the taints, abandoned all ten fetters ([[Saṃyojana]]) and realised [[Nibbāna]]. Arahantship is the fourth and highest of the noble stages ([[Ariya-puggala]]) and the goal of the holy life.[^mn]

## The declaration

The arahant's declaration of final knowledge is given in many suttas:

:::quote Majjhima Nikāya 4
*Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā.*

Birth is destroyed, the holy life has been lived, what had to be done has been done; there is no more coming to any state of being.
:::

## Meanings of the word

The commentaries explain the word arahant in several ways: one who is far from the defilements, one who has destroyed the enemies (the defilements), one who has broken the spokes of the wheel of rebirth, one who is worthy of offerings, and one who does no evil even in secret.[^vism]

## Kinds of arahant

The Kosambi Sutta and the [[Puggalapaññatti]] distinguish the arahant liberated by wisdom, who has not attained the immaterial jhānas, and the arahant liberated in both ways, who has. Some arahants possess the three true knowledges, the six direct knowledges, or the four analytical knowledges.[^pp]

## The Buddha as arahant

The Buddha is also an arahant, the first of the nine qualities being *arahaṃ*. What distinguishes the Buddha is that the Blessed One discovered the path unaided and established it for others, and possesses qualities such as omniscient knowledge not shared by disciples.[^sn2258]

## Final Nibbāna

The arahant attains Nibbāna with residue remaining during life, and Nibbāna without residue remaining at death, after which there is no more rebirth.[^iti44]
