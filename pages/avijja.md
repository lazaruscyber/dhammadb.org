**Avijjā** ({{lang|Pāli|avijjā}}), "not knowing", is ignorance of the [[Four Noble Truths]]. It is defined in the suttas as not knowing suffering, its origin, its cessation and the way leading to its cessation.[^sn122]

## Place in the teaching

- It is the first link of [[Paṭicca-samuppāda|dependent origination]]: with ignorance as condition, formations arise.
- It is the last of the ten fetters ([[Saṃyojana]]), abandoned only at arahantship.
- It is one of the taints ([[Āsava]]) and one of the latent tendencies.
- In the [[Abhidhamma Piṭaka|Abhidhamma]], it is identified with the mental factor of delusion ({{term|moha}}), one of the three unwholesome roots ([[Akusala-mūla]]).

## Without a first beginning

Ignorance is not a first cause from which the world began. The Blessed One said: "A first point of ignorance, bhikkhus, is not seen, such that before it there was no ignorance and afterwards it came into being." Yet ignorance has its own conditions: the five hindrances ([[Nīvaraṇa]]), and before them misconduct, lack of sense restraint, lack of mindfulness, unwise attention, lack of faith, not hearing the true Dhamma, and not associating with good people.[^an1061]

## Its opposite

The opposite of ignorance is true knowledge ({{term|vijjā}}): in the highest sense the knowledge of the destruction of the taints, through which the arahant has abandoned ignorance completely.

[^an1061]: Aṅguttara Nikāya 10.61, *Avijjā Sutta*.
