**Phussa** ({{lang|Pāli|Phussa}}) is the tenth month of the Theravāda lunar calendar in the traditional Pāli listing, falling in about December to January in the Gregorian calendar. Like every month of the calendar, it takes its name from the constellation ({{term|nakkhatta}}) near which the moon is full in that month.

## In the calendar

Phussa has 30 days and belongs to the cold season ({{term|hemanta}}), one of the three seasons of four months each into which the year is divided. It follows [[Māgasira]] and is followed by [[Māgha]]. Like every month, it contains four uposatha observance days: the eighth day of the waxing moon, the full moon, the eighth day of the waning moon and the new moon.

## Events

- According to the chronicles, the Blessed One arrived at Rājagaha on the full moon of Phussa, accompanied by the thousand former matted-hair ascetics who had become arahants, and dwelt there for two months.[^mbv]
- The *Mahāvaṃsa* records the Blessed One's first visit to Sri Lanka, at Mahiyaṅgana, in the ninth month after the awakening, on the full moon of Phussa.[^mhv1] It is observed in Sri Lanka as Duruthu Poya.
