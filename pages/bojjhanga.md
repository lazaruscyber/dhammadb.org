The seven **bojjhaṅgas** ({{lang|Pāli|bojjhaṅga}}) are the factors of awakening. They are the sixth group of the thirty-seven requisites of awakening.[^sn46]

## The seven factors

:::columns 2
1. Mindfulness ({{term|sati}})
2. Investigation of states ({{term|dhammavicaya}})
3. Energy ({{term|viriya}})
4. Rapture ({{term|pīti}})
5. Tranquillity ({{term|passaddhi}})
6. Concentration ({{term|samādhi}})
7. Equanimity ({{term|upekkhā}})
:::

## Balancing

When the mind is sluggish, the Blessed One taught, it is the time to develop investigation, energy and rapture, just as one adds dry grass to revive a small fire. When the mind is excited, it is the time to develop tranquillity, concentration and equanimity, as one adds wet grass to a blazing fire. Mindfulness is useful at all times.[^sn4653]

## Development

Mindfulness of breathing, developed in sixteen steps, fulfils the four foundations of mindfulness, and these fulfil the seven factors of awakening, which in turn fulfil true knowledge and liberation ([[Ānāpānassati Sutta]]).

## Bojjhaṅga Paritta

The three discourses on the factors of awakening, recited to the Venerable Mahākassapa, the Venerable Mahāmoggallāna and the Blessed One when each was ill, are chanted together as the Bojjhaṅga [[Paritta]]. According to these suttas, each recovered on hearing the factors recited.[^sn4614]
