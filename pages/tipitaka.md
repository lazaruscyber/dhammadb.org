The **Tipiṭaka** ({{lang|Pāli|tipiṭaka}}, "three baskets") is the collection of the Buddha's teachings preserved in the Pāli language and accepted as authoritative by the Theravāda tradition. It is often called the **Pāli Canon**.

## The three baskets

### Vinaya Piṭaka

The Basket of Discipline contains the rules of conduct for monks and nuns, with the stories of how each rule came to be laid down.

### Sutta Piṭaka

{{main|Sutta Piṭaka}}

The Basket of Discourses contains the teachings of the Buddha and his chief disciples. It has five collections:

| Collection | Pāli | Arrangement |
|---|---|---|
| Long Discourses | {{term|Dīgha Nikāya}} | By length |
| Middle-length Discourses | {{term|Majjhima Nikāya}} | By length |
| Connected Discourses | {{term|Saṃyutta Nikāya}} | By subject |
| Numerical Discourses | {{term|Aṅguttara Nikāya}} | By number |
| Minor Collection | {{term|Khuddaka Nikāya}} | Miscellaneous |

### Abhidhamma Piṭaka

The Basket of Higher Teaching is a systematic analysis of mind and matter in seven books:

:::columns 2
1. Dhammasaṅgaṇī
2. Vibhaṅga
3. Dhātukathā
4. Puggalapaññatti
5. Kathāvatthu
6. Yamaka
7. Paṭṭhāna
:::

## Transmission

The teachings were first preserved by recitation. According to the Sri Lankan chronicles they were written down at Aluvihāra in the 1st century BCE.{{ref|Mahāvaṃsa, chapter 33.}} The most recent recitation of the whole canon was held at the Sixth Council ({{term|Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana}}) in Yangon, Myanmar, from 1954 to 1956.
