A **sāmaṇera** ({{lang|Pāli|sāmaṇera}}) is a novice: one who has gone forth ([[Pabbajjā]]) and keeps the ten precepts, but has not received the higher ordination ([[Upasampadā]]). A female novice is a {{term|sāmaṇerī}}.[^vin]

## The first novice

The first novice was the Venerable [[Rāhula]], the son of the Bodhisatta. When the Blessed One visited [[Kapilavatthu]], the boy Rāhula followed, asking for his inheritance. The Blessed One told the Venerable [[Sāriputta]] to give him the going forth, saying the Blessed One would give the noble inheritance of the Dhamma instead.[^vin]

## Age

A boy may become a novice from the age of seven, or when he is old enough to scare away crows. He must have his parents' permission.[^vin]

## The ten precepts

| No. | Precept |
|---|---|
| 1 to 5 | The [[Pañcasīla|five precepts]], with complete celibacy |
| 6 | No food after midday |
| 7 | No dancing, singing, music or shows |
| 8 | No garlands, perfumes or adornments |
| 9 | No high or luxurious seats and beds |
| 10 | No accepting gold and silver |

These are recited in the {{term|Dasasikkhāpada}} of the [[Khuddakapāṭha]].[^khp2] The novice also learns the ten questions of the Kumārapañha.

## In Myanmar

In Myanmar the novice ordination of boys, the {{term|shinbyu}}, is one of the most important events of family life. The boy is dressed as a prince to recall the going forth of the Bodhisatta, and then has his head shaved and receives the robes.

[^vin]: Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga 1.54 to 1.56.
[^khp2]: Khuddakapāṭha 2, *Dasasikkhāpada*.
