**Upekkhā** ({{lang|Pāli|upekkhā}}) means equanimity, literally "looking on" evenly. As a divine abiding it is impartiality towards beings, based on understanding that beings are owners of their kamma. It is the fourth of the four [[Brahmavihāra|divine abidings]], the seventh of the [[Bojjhaṅga|factors of awakening]] and the tenth of the [[Pāramī|perfections]].[^vism]

## As a divine abiding

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Characteristic | Promoting the aspect of neutrality towards beings |
| Function | Seeing equality in beings |
| Manifestation | Quieting of resentment and approval |
| Proximate cause | Seeing ownership of kamma: "Beings are owners of their kamma" |
| Fails when | It produces the equanimity of unknowing |

It can only be developed by one who has already attained the third jhāna through the first three divine abidings. It leads to the fourth jhāna.[^vism]

## Ten kinds of equanimity

The [[Visuddhimagga]] distinguishes ten kinds of equanimity, among them six-factored equanimity (of the arahant towards the six sense objects), equanimity as a divine abiding, as a factor of awakening, as energy, as a formation, as feeling, as insight, as specific neutrality ({{term|tatramajjhattatā}}), as the equanimity of jhāna, and as purifying equanimity. The equanimity about formations ({{term|saṅkhārupekkhā}}) is a high stage of [[Vipassanā ñāṇa|insight knowledge]].[^vism]

## As feeling

Upekkhā is also the name of neutral feeling, neither painful nor pleasant. The commentaries are careful to distinguish neutral feeling from the mental factor of specific neutrality, which is the equanimity of the divine abidings.

[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapters IV and IX.
