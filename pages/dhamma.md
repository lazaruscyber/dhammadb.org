**Dhamma** ({{lang|Pāli|dhamma}}) is the second of the Three Jewels. In this sense it is the teaching of the Buddha and the truth it reveals. The commentaries list many meanings of the word, among them teaching, quality, cause, and phenomenon; in the [[Abhidhamma Piṭaka|Abhidhamma]] the ultimate constituents of experience are also called dhammas.[^dha]

## The six qualities

The qualities of the Dhamma are recited in the formula of the [[Dhajagga Sutta]]:[^sn113]

| Pāli | Meaning |
|---|---|
| {{term|svākkhāto}} | Well proclaimed by the Blessed One |
| {{term|sandiṭṭhiko}} | Visible here and now |
| {{term|akāliko}} | Immediate, not delayed in its result |
| {{term|ehipassiko}} | Inviting one to come and see |
| {{term|opaneyyiko}} | Leading onward |
| {{term|paccattaṃ veditabbo viññūhi}} | To be experienced by the wise for themselves |

## The three aspects

The commentaries describe the true Dhamma ({{term|saddhamma}}) in three aspects:

- **Pariyatti**: learning the teaching, the scriptures of the [[Tipiṭaka]].
- **Paṭipatti**: practice, the threefold training of [[Sīla|virtue]], [[Samādhi|concentration]] and [[Paññā|wisdom]].
- **Paṭivedha**: penetration, the realisation of the paths, fruits and [[Nibbāna]].

The ninefold supramundane Dhamma consists of the four paths, the four fruits and Nibbāna.

## Dhamma as teacher

Before the parinibbāna the Blessed One told the Venerable [[Ānanda]]: "The Dhamma and Discipline that I have taught and laid down for you shall be your Teacher when I am gone."[^dn16]

[^sn113]: Saṃyutta Nikāya 11.3, *Dhajagga Sutta*.
