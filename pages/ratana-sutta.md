The **Ratana Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Ratana Sutta}}), "the Discourse on the Jewels", is found in the [[Khuddakapāṭha]] and the [[Sutta Nipāta]]. It is a central text of the protective chanting ([[Paritta]]).

## Occasion

According to the commentary, the city of [[Vesālī]] was struck by drought and famine. Corpses lay unburied, non-human beings entered the city, and disease broke out. The Licchavis went to [[Rājagaha]] and invited the Blessed One, who came with a great company of bhikkhus. As soon as the Blessed One set foot in Vesālī's territory, heavy rain fell. The Blessed One taught the Ratana Sutta to the Venerable [[Ānanda]], who walked around the city through the night reciting it and sprinkling water from the Buddha's bowl; the dangers passed.[^khpa]

## Contents

The sutta begins by asking all beings assembled, of earth and of the sky, to listen with attention and to show loving-kindness to humanity, who bring offerings day and night.[^khp6]

Each of the following verses declares a quality of one of the three jewels and ends with an act of truth: "This too is the precious jewel in the Buddha (or the Dhamma, or the Saṅgha). By this truth may there be well-being." The verses praise:

- The Buddha, who has no equal in any world.
- The Dhamma: the destruction, dispassion and deathless excellence realised by the Sage of the Sakyans, and the concentration with immediate result.
- The Saṅgha: the eight persons praised by the good; the stream-enterer, steady as a post driven into the earth, who has abandoned three fetters and is incapable of the six grave wrongs; and the arahants, extinguished like a lamp, their old kamma exhausted, with no new becoming.[^khp6]

The last three verses, attributed to Sakka, the king of the gods, pay homage to the Buddha, Dhamma and Saṅgha.
