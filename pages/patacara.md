The nun **Paṭācārā** ({{lang|Pāli|Paṭācārā}}) was declared by the Blessed One foremost among the nuns who knew the Vinaya.[^an1]

## Her sorrow

According to the commentary she was the daughter of a wealthy family of [[Sāvatthī]] who eloped with a servant. Travelling home to give birth to her second child, she lost everything in a single storm: her husband died of a snakebite, her newborn was carried off by a hawk, and her elder son was swept away by a river. Arriving at Sāvatthī, she learned that her parents and brother had died that same night when their house collapsed. Driven mad with grief, she wandered with her clothes falling off, and so was called Paṭācārā, "the one who walks cloakless".[^dhpa]

## Meeting the Blessed One

She came to the Jetavana where the Blessed One was teaching. People tried to drive her away, but the Blessed One said, "Sister, regain your mindfulness." She came to her senses and covered herself. The Blessed One told her that through saṃsāra she had shed more tears over the loss of loved ones than the water of the four oceans, and that in time of death no relatives can give shelter. She attained stream-entry and went forth.[^dhpa]

## Awakening

As a nun, while washing her feet, she watched the water flow away, some trickling a short way, some further, some further still, and reflected that beings die in youth, middle age and old age. That night, as she put out her lamp, her mind was liberated.[^thi]

## Teacher

She became a teacher of many women, including thirty nuns whose verses in the [[Therīgāthā]] record her guidance.
