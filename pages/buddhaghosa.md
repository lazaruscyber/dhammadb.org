The Venerable **Buddhaghosa** ({{lang|Pāli|Buddhaghosa}}), "voice of the Buddha", was the greatest of the Theravāda commentators. Working at the Mahāvihāra in [[Anurādhapura]], he rendered the Sinhala commentaries into Pāli and wrote the [[Visuddhimagga]].[^cv]

## Life

The Cūḷavaṃsa, the continuation of the [[Mahāvaṃsa]], relates that he was born a brahmin near the site of the great Bodhi tree and became learned in the Vedas. Defeated in debate by the elder Revata, he went forth to learn the Abhidhamma. While in India he wrote the Ñāṇodaya and the [[Atthasālinī]]. Revata sent him to Sri Lanka, saying that only the text had been brought to India, while the commentaries were preserved in Sinhala in the island.[^cv]

## The test

The elders of the Mahāvihāra tested him by giving him two verses from the [[Saṃyutta Nikāya]] and asking him to explain them. His answer was the Visuddhimagga. The chronicle says that the devas twice hid the text, and he wrote it again each time; when all three copies were compared they were found to be identical, word for word. The elders then gave him the whole of the commentarial literature.[^cv]

## Works

| Text | Commentary on |
|---|---|
| [[Samantapāsādikā]] | The Vinaya Piṭaka |
| [[Sumaṅgalavilāsinī]] | The Dīgha Nikāya |
| [[Papañcasūdanī]] | The Majjhima Nikāya |
| [[Sāratthappakāsinī]] | The Saṃyutta Nikāya |
| [[Manorathapūraṇī]] | The Aṅguttara Nikāya |
| [[Atthasālinī]] | The Dhammasaṅgaṇī |

He is also traditionally credited with the commentaries on the other books of the Abhidhamma and some of the [[Khuddaka Nikāya]]. In Myanmar a tradition holds that after completing his work he travelled to Thaton.

[^cv]: *Cūḷavaṃsa* 37.
