**Muditā** ({{lang|Pāli|muditā}}) is appreciative joy or sympathetic joy: gladness at the happiness, success and good qualities of others. It is the third of the four [[Brahmavihāra|divine abidings]], and its far enemy is discontent or envy.[^vism]

## Characteristics

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Characteristic | Gladness at others' success |
| Function | Being without envy |
| Manifestation | Elimination of aversion or boredom |
| Proximate cause | Seeing beings' success |
| Succeeds when | It makes aversion subside |
| Fails when | It produces worldly merriment |

## Development

The [[Visuddhimagga]] teaches that one should begin not with a dear person or a neutral person, but with a very dear companion, one who would be called a "boon companion", who is happy and successful. On seeing or hearing of their success one rejoices: "How glad this being is! How good, how excellent!" Then appreciative joy is extended to neutral and hostile persons.[^vism]

## In practice

The tradition of saying *sādhu*, "well done", three times on hearing of a meritorious deed is an expression of appreciative joy. Rejoicing in the merit of others ({{term|pattānumodanā}}) is itself counted as one of the ten bases of meritorious action ([[Puñña]]).[^dhsa]

[^dhsa]: *Atthasālinī*, the commentary to the Dhammasaṅgaṇī.
