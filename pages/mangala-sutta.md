The **Maṅgala Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Maṅgala Sutta}}), "the Discourse on Blessings", is found in the [[Khuddakapāṭha]] and the [[Sutta Nipāta]]. It is one of the most loved of the protective discourses ([[Paritta]]) and is recited throughout the Theravāda world.[^khp5]

## Occasion

The commentary relates that for twelve years gods and humans had debated what constitutes a blessing, some saying the things seen, others the things heard or sensed, without agreement. Late one night a deity of radiant beauty, lighting up the whole of Jetavana, came to the Blessed One and asked in verse: "Many gods and humans have pondered on blessings, longing for safety. Tell, then, the highest blessing."[^khpa]

## The thirty-eight blessings

The Blessed One answered in ten verses, naming thirty-eight blessings. Among them are:

- Not to associate with fools, to associate with the wise, and to honour those worthy of honour.
- To live in a suitable place, to have done merit in the past, and to set oneself on the right course.
- Much learning, skill in crafts, a well-trained discipline, and well-spoken words.
- To support mother and father, to cherish wife and children, and to follow a peaceful occupation.
- Giving, righteous conduct, helping relatives, and blameless deeds.
- Abstaining from evil and from intoxicants, and diligence in the Dhamma.
- Reverence, humility, contentment, gratitude, and hearing the Dhamma at the proper time.
- Patience, gentleness of speech, seeing ascetics, and discussing the Dhamma at the proper time.
- Austerity, the holy life, seeing the Noble Truths, and realising [[Nibbāna]].
- A mind that does not waver when touched by worldly conditions, sorrowless, stainless and secure.

The sutta closes: "Those who have done such things are undefeated everywhere and go everywhere in safety. These are for them the highest blessings."[^khp5]
