**Lumbinī** ({{lang|Pāli|Lumbinī}}) is the sāla grove where the Bodhisatta was born. It lay between [[Kapilavatthu]], the city of the Sakyans, and Devadaha, the city of the Koliyans.[^jn]

## The birth

According to the commentaries, Queen Mahāmāyā was travelling from Kapilavatthu to her parents' home in Devadaha to give birth. Passing the Lumbinī grove, with its sāla trees in full bloom, she wished to enjoy it. Standing and holding a branch of a sāla tree, she gave birth to the Bodhisatta on the full moon of [[Vesākha]]. The newborn Bodhisatta took seven steps to the north and declared: "I am the foremost in the world. This is my last birth; there is now no more renewed existence."[^mn123]

## A place of pilgrimage

In the [[Mahāparinibbāna Sutta]], the Blessed One named four places that a faithful person should see with feelings of urgency: the place where the Tathāgata was born, where the Tathāgata attained awakening, where the Tathāgata set the Wheel of the Dhamma in motion, and where the Tathāgata attained final Nibbāna. Lumbinī is the first.[^dn16]

## The pillar of Asoka

The emperor [[Asoka]] visited Lumbinī in the twentieth year of his reign and set up a stone pillar. Its inscription records that "here the Buddha, the Sage of the Sakyans, was born", and that the village of Lumbinī was freed from tax.
