The **Alagaddūpama Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Alagaddūpama Sutta}}), "the Simile of the Water Snake", is the twenty-second discourse of the [[Majjhima Nikāya]].

## Occasion

A bhikkhu named Ariṭṭha held the wrong view that the things called obstructions by the Blessed One are not able to obstruct one who indulges in them. The Blessed One rebuked him and gave a series of similes for the dangers of sensual pleasures.[^mn]

## The water snake

Some people learn the Dhamma but do not examine its meaning with wisdom; they learn it only for criticising others or winning debates. They are like a man who grasps a water snake by the coils or tail: it turns and bites him. One who grasps it rightly with a forked stick by the neck is not harmed. So the Dhamma, rightly grasped, leads to welfare and happiness.[^mn]

## The raft

A man who has crossed a great river on a raft he has built does not carry it on his head afterwards; he leaves it on the bank and goes on. "So, bhikkhus, I have taught the Dhamma as similar to a raft, which is for the purpose of crossing over, not for the purpose of grasping."[^mn]

## Non-self

The sutta continues with the six standpoints for views, and an examination of the aggregates as [[Anattā|non-self]], in the same form as the [[Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta]].

[^mn]: Majjhima Nikāya 22, *Alagaddūpama Sutta*.
