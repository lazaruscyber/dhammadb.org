The Venerable **Anuruddha** ({{lang|Pāli|Anuruddha}}) was a Sakyan prince and cousin of the Blessed One, declared foremost among the bhikkhus with the divine eye ({{term|dibbacakkhu}}).[^an1]

## Going forth

The commentaries tell that Anuruddha was so sheltered as a prince that he had never heard the words "there is not". When his brother Mahānāma explained the endless work of the household life, ploughing, sowing, watering and harvesting year after year, Anuruddha chose to go forth instead, together with [[Ānanda]], Bhaddiya, Bhagu, Kimbila, [[Devadatta]] and [[Upāli]].[^vin]

## The eight thoughts of a great man

While meditating in the Eastern Bamboo Park, Anuruddha reflected on seven thoughts of a great person: that the Dhamma is for one with few wishes, content, secluded, energetic, mindful, concentrated and wise. The Blessed One came and added the eighth: the Dhamma is for one who delights in non-proliferation ({{term|nippapañca}}). Anuruddha soon attained arahantship.[^an830]

## Harmony

In the Cūḷagosiṅga Sutta, Anuruddha, Nandiya and Kimbila lived together "blending like milk and water, viewing each other with kindly eyes". Each acted towards the others with loving-kindness of body, speech and mind, setting aside his own wishes.[^mn31]

## At the parinibbāna

At the parinibbāna, Anuruddha followed with his mind the Blessed One's passage through the jhānas. When Ānanda thought the Blessed One had passed away, Anuruddha explained that the Blessed One had entered the cessation of perception and feeling; and he described the final passing from the fourth jhāna.[^dn16]
