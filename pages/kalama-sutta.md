The **Kālāma Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Kālāma Sutta}}), also called the Kesaputtiya Sutta, is the sixty-fifth discourse of the Book of Threes in the [[Aṅguttara Nikāya]].[^an365]

## Occasion

The Kālāmas told the Blessed One that various ascetics and brahmins came to Kesaputta, each explaining and praising their own doctrine and disparaging the doctrines of others, and that the Kālāmas were in doubt as to which of them spoke the truth.[^an365]

## The teaching

The Blessed One said their doubt was proper and told them not to go by oral tradition, by lineage of teaching, by hearsay, by a collection of scriptures, by logical reasoning, by inferential reasoning, by reasoned reflection, by acceptance of a view after pondering it, by the seeming competence of a speaker, or because "the ascetic is our teacher". Instead:

> When you know for yourselves: "These things are unwholesome, blameworthy, censured by the wise; these things, if undertaken and practised, lead to harm and suffering," then you should abandon them.[^an365]

The Blessed One then questioned them: when greed, hatred and delusion arise in a person, is it for welfare or for harm? They agreed it is for harm, leading to killing, stealing, adultery and lying. Non-greed, non-hatred and non-delusion are for welfare and happiness.

## Context

The sutta does not teach that each person should accept whatever they personally prefer. The criterion it gives is what is censured or praised by the wise, together with seeing directly the results of greed, hatred and delusion. It leads into the four [[Brahmavihāra|divine abidings]] and the four assurances: whether or not there is another world and the fruit of kamma, one whose mind is free of enmity and pure gains comfort here and now.[^an365]

[^an365]: Aṅguttara Nikāya 3.65, *Kesaputtiya Sutta*.
