**Karuṇā** ({{lang|Pāli|karuṇā}}) is compassion: the state that makes the heart tremble at the suffering of others, and the wish that they be free from suffering. It is the second of the four [[Brahmavihāra|divine abidings]].[^vism]

## Characteristics

The [[Visuddhimagga]] describes compassion as follows:[^vism]

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Characteristic | Promoting the removal of suffering |
| Function | Not bearing the suffering of others |
| Manifestation | Non-cruelty |
| Proximate cause | Seeing helplessness in those overwhelmed by suffering |
| Succeeds when | It makes cruelty subside |
| Fails when | It produces grief |

## Development

The meditator first develops compassion towards one who is in misery, such as a person who has lost everything, saying: "This being has indeed fallen into misery; if only they could be freed from this suffering." If no such person is seen, compassion can be developed towards an evil-doer, even one who is happy, since that person is heading for suffering. It is then extended to a dear person, a neutral person and a hostile person, and the barriers are broken down as with [[Mettā|loving-kindness]].[^vism]

## The Great Compassion

The Great Compassion ({{term|mahākaruṇā}}) of the Buddha is listed among the knowledges unique to a Buddha. Each morning, the commentaries relate, the Blessed One entered the attainment of the great compassion and surveyed the world to see who could be helped that day.[^dhpa]
