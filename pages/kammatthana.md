**Kammaṭṭhāna** ({{lang|Pāli|kammaṭṭhāna}}), "place of work", is the name for a meditation subject. The [[Visuddhimagga]] lists forty subjects for tranquillity ([[Samatha]]) meditation.[^vism]

## The forty subjects

| Group | Number | Subjects |
|---|---|---|
| [[Kasiṇa|Kasiṇas]] | 10 | Earth, water, fire, air, blue, yellow, red, white, light, limited space |
| [[Asubha|Foulness]] | 10 | Ten stages of a corpse |
| [[Anussati|Recollections]] | 10 | Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, virtue, generosity, devas, death, the body, breathing, peace |
| [[Brahmavihāra|Divine abidings]] | 4 | Loving-kindness, compassion, appreciative joy, equanimity |
| Immaterial states | 4 | Infinite space, infinite consciousness, nothingness, neither perception nor non-perception |
| Perception | 1 | Repulsiveness of nutriment |
| Defining | 1 | [[Catudhātuvavatthāna|The four elements]] |

## Suitability to temperament

The Visuddhimagga teaches that the teacher should give a subject suited to the student's temperament ({{term|carita}}):[^vism]

| Temperament | Suitable subjects |
|---|---|
| Greedy | Foulness, mindfulness of the body |
| Hating | The divine abidings, colour kasiṇas |
| Deluded or speculative | Mindfulness of breathing |
| Faithful | The first six recollections |
| Intelligent | Recollection of death and peace, the four elements, repulsiveness of nutriment |

## Generally useful subjects

Some subjects are called generally useful ({{term|sabbatthaka-kammaṭṭhāna}}) and suitable for all: loving-kindness towards the Saṅgha and others, and recollection of death. In some lists foulness is also included.[^vism]

[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapter III.
