Every article shows a **peer-review score** under its title. It counts how many readers have read the whole article and confirmed that they believe all of the information in it is correct.

## Reading the score

| Icon | Score |
|---|---|
| {{review icon|high}} | {{review range|high}} |
| {{review icon|medium}} | {{review range|medium}} |
| {{review icon|low}} | {{review range|low}} |

The number in brackets beside the icon is the exact count. A new article shows **Not yet reviewed**.

## Adding your confirmation

1. Read the article all the way to the end.
2. Once you reach the end, a box appears asking whether everything on the page is correct.
3. If you believe it is, choose **Yes, I confirm it is correct**. The score goes up by one.

Each reader can confirm a page once. The box only appears after you have spent a reasonable time on the page, so that confirmations come from people who have actually read it.

If you find something wrong, choose **I found a mistake** instead and follow the steps on [[Help:Contribute]].

## When an article changes

A confirmation applies to the exact text you read. When an article is edited, its **Page ID** changes, and its score starts again from zero so that new readers can check the new version.

## What the score is not

The score shows how many readers agree with an article. It is not an editorial approval and does not replace checking the sources. Where an article and the [[Tipiṭaka]] differ, the Tipiṭaka is authoritative.
