**Catudhātuvavatthāna** ({{lang|Pāli|catudhātuvavatthāna}}) is the defining of the four elements: the meditation that discerns the body as made up only of the four great elements ([[Dhātu]]). It is the last of the forty meditation subjects in the [[Visuddhimagga]].[^vism]

## The butcher's simile

The [[Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta]] says: just as a skilled butcher, having killed a cow and divided it into portions, would sit at a crossroads, so the bhikkhu reviews this very body, however placed or disposed, as consisting of elements: "In this body there are the earth element, the water element, the fire element and the air element." The butcher no longer thinks "cow" but "meat", and the meditator no longer thinks "being" or "person" but only "elements".[^mn10]

## The method

The Visuddhimagga describes a brief method for those of quick understanding, taking in the whole body at once, and a detailed method for others, dividing the body into the twenty parts where earth predominates, twelve where water predominates, four aspects of fire and six aspects of air.[^vism]

| Element | Characteristic | Parts |
|---|---|---|
| Earth | Hardness | Hair, nails, teeth, skin, bones and others (20) |
| Water | Cohesion | Bile, phlegm, blood, sweat and others (12) |
| Fire | Heat | Warming, ageing, burning, digesting (4) |
| Air | Motion, support | Upward, downward and other winds, in-and-out breath (6) |

## Result

This meditation leads to access concentration, not absorption, since its object is states with their own nature. It removes the perception of a being and is a basis for insight into [[Anattā|non-self]].

[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapter XI.
[^mn10]: Majjhima Nikāya 10, *Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta*.
