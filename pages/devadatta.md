**Devadatta** ({{lang|Pāli|Devadatta}}) was a Sakyan prince, a cousin of the Bodhisatta and brother of [[Yasodharā]] according to the commentaries. He went forth together with [[Ānanda]], [[Anuruddha]], [[Upāli]] and others, and developed worldly supernormal powers, but no noble attainment.[^vin]

## Ambition

Wanting gain and honour, he won the support of Prince Ajātasattu by appearing in his lap as a child wrapped in snakes. He then asked the Blessed One to hand over the leadership of the Saṅgha. The Blessed One refused, saying the Blessed One would not hand over the Saṅgha even to [[Sāriputta]] and [[Mahāmoggallāna]].[^vin]

## Attempts to kill the Blessed One

With Ajātasattu's support Devadatta made three attempts:

1. He sent archers, but each was converted by the Blessed One.
2. He rolled a rock from the Vulture Peak; a splinter wounded the Blessed One's foot and drew blood.
3. He had the fierce elephant Nāḷāgiri released in the Blessed One's path, but the elephant was subdued by the Blessed One's loving-kindness.[^vin]

## The schism

Devadatta proposed five rules for all bhikkhus: to live in the forest, eat only almsfood, wear only rag robes, live at the foot of a tree, and never eat fish or meat. The Blessed One allowed each bhikkhu to keep these if wishing, but would not make them compulsory. Devadatta took 500 newly ordained bhikkhus to Gayāsīsa, causing a schism. Sāriputta and Moggallāna went there and brought them back.[^vin]

## End

Wounding a Buddha and causing a schism are two of the five heinous deeds ({{term|anantariya-kamma}}) that bring rebirth in hell immediately after death. The commentary relates that as Devadatta was being carried to the Blessed One to ask forgiveness, the earth opened and he fell into Avīci hell. In his last moments he took refuge in the Buddha, and the commentary says that in the distant future he will become a Paccekabuddha.[^dhpa]
