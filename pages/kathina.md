**Kathina** ({{lang|Pāli|kathina}}) is the ceremony held in the month after the rains residence, in which cloth is offered to the Saṅgha and made into a robe on the same day. The word refers to the wooden frame on which robes were sewn.[^vin]

## Origin

Thirty bhikkhus from Pāvā set out to spend the rains with the Blessed One at [[Sāvatthī]], but the rains began when they were still six leagues away at Sāketa. When they arrived after the rains, their robes were soaked and muddy. The Blessed One allowed the kathina so that bhikkhus who have completed the rains could receive new cloth.[^vin]

## The ceremony

| Step | Description |
|---|---|
| Offering | Lay people or another bhikkhu offer the cloth to the Saṅgha, not to an individual |
| Formal act | The Saṅgha gives the cloth to one bhikkhu by a formal act with motion and proclamation |
| Making | The robe is washed, cut, sewn and dyed within the same day |
| Spreading | The bhikkhu spreads the kathina and the others rejoice in it |

The kathina must be held between the end of the rains on the full moon of [[Assayuja]] and the full moon of [[Kattikā]].[^vin]

## Privileges

Bhikkhus who have taken part in the kathina receive five privileges for the following four months, relaxing certain rules about robes and meals.

## The weaving festival

In Myanmar the night of the Kattikā full moon is celebrated with the {{term|matho thingan}} robe-weaving contest, in which teams of women weave robes through the night to be offered at dawn.

[^vin]: Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga 7, *Kathinakkhandhaka*.
