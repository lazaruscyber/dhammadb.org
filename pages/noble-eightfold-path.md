The **Noble Eightfold Path** ({{lang|Pāli|ariya aṭṭhaṅgika magga}}) is the fourth of the [[Four Noble Truths]]: the way leading to the cessation of suffering. The Buddha called it the middle way, avoiding both indulgence in sensual pleasure and self-mortification.[^sn5611]

## The eight factors

The factors are traditionally grouped into the three trainings of virtue ({{term|sīla}}), concentration ({{term|samādhi}}) and wisdom ({{term|paññā}}).[^mn44]

| No. | Factor | Pāli | Training |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Right view | {{term|sammā-diṭṭhi}} | Wisdom |
| 2 | Right intention | {{term|sammā-saṅkappa}} | Wisdom |
| 3 | Right speech | {{term|sammā-vācā}} | Virtue |
| 4 | Right action | {{term|sammā-kammanta}} | Virtue |
| 5 | Right livelihood | {{term|sammā-ājīva}} | Virtue |
| 6 | Right effort | {{term|sammā-vāyāma}} | Concentration |
| 7 | Right mindfulness | {{term|sammā-sati}} | Concentration |
| 8 | Right concentration | {{term|sammā-samādhi}} | Concentration |

:::columns 3

- Right concentration

**Wisdom**
- Right view
- Right intention

## Development

The factors are not steps to be completed one after another. They are developed together, each supporting the others, and they come together fully at the moment of path knowledge.
