The **Kāyagatāsati Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Kāyagatāsati Sutta}}), "Mindfulness of the Body", is the 119th discourse of the [[Majjhima Nikāya]]. It sets out the practices of mindfulness directed to the body: mindfulness of breathing, the postures, clear comprehension, the thirty-two parts of the body, the four elements and the cemetery contemplations, followed by the four [[Jhāna|jhānas]].[^mn]

Whoever develops mindfulness of the body includes within it all wholesome states that partake of true knowledge, just as one who pervades the great ocean with the mind includes all the streams that flow into it.

The sutta lists ten benefits of this practice, among them conquering discontent and delight, conquering fear, enduring cold, heat and pain, attaining the jhānas at will, and finally the destruction of the taints.[^mn]

[^mn]: Majjhima Nikāya 119, *Kāyagatāsati Sutta*.
