The nun **Kisāgotamī** ({{lang|Pāli|Kisāgotamī}}), "the lean Gotamī", was declared foremost among the nuns who wore coarse robes.[^an1]

## The mustard seed

According to the commentary she was born into a poor family of [[Sāvatthī]] and married into a rich one, where she was despised until she bore a son. When the boy died as a toddler, she was overcome with grief and went from house to house carrying his body, asking for medicine. A wise man sent her to the Blessed One.[^dhpa]

The Blessed One told her to bring a mustard seed from a house where no one had died. She went from door to door. Everyone was willing to give mustard seed, but in every house someone had died. By evening she understood that death is common to all. She laid her son's body in the charnel ground and returned to the Blessed One, who spoke the verse: "Death carries off the one who is attached to children and cattle, whose mind clings, as a great flood sweeps away a sleeping village." She attained stream-entry and went forth.[^dhpa]

## Awakening

As a nun, watching the flames of a lamp flare up and die down, she perceived the arising and passing away of beings. The Blessed One appeared to her and spoke the verse: "Better than a hundred years lived without seeing the Deathless is a single day lived seeing the Deathless." She attained arahantship.[^dhp114]

## Verses

Her verses in the [[Therīgāthā]] and the Bhikkhunī Saṃyutta show her complete freedom from grief.
