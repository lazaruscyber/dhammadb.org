The **Fourth Council** in the Theravāda reckoning is the gathering at the Aluvihāra (Ālokavihāra) near Mātale in Sri Lanka, where the [[Tipiṭaka]] and its commentaries were written down for the first time.[^mhv]

## Cause

During the reign of King Vaṭṭagāmaṇī Abhaya, Sri Lanka suffered invasion and a terrible famine, during which many bhikkhus died. The elders saw that the oral transmission of the Tipiṭaka was in danger of being broken, since the texts depended on the memories of living bhikkhus.[^mhv]

## The writing down

The [[Mahāvaṃsa]] records: "The text of the three Piṭakas and the commentary thereon, which the most wise bhikkhus had formerly handed down orally, the bhikkhus, seeing that the people were falling away, came together, and in order that the true doctrine might endure, they wrote them down in books."[^mhv] Five hundred bhikkhus are said to have recited and checked the texts before they were inscribed on palm leaves.

## Significance

Until then the whole of the teaching had been preserved by recitation, as at the [[First Council]]. The writing down marked a new stage in its preservation. The manuscripts of the Theravāda countries, in the Sinhala, Burmese, Khmer and Thai scripts, descend from this tradition.

## Counting

In Myanmar the councils are counted in this way, with the Aluvihāra recitation as the fourth; the [[Fifth Council]] and [[Sixth Council]] were held in Myanmar. In Sri Lanka and Thailand, different counts of later councils are also used.

[^mhv]: *Mahāvaṃsa*. Chapter XXXIII.
