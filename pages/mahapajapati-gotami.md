**Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī** ({{lang|Pāli|Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī}}) was the younger sister of Queen Mahāmāyā and, after Mahāmāyā's death seven days after the birth, raised the Bodhisatta as her own son. She was the first woman to receive ordination and was declared foremost among the nuns in seniority ({{term|rattaññū}}).[^an1]

## Founding the order

After King [[Suddhodana]] died, she asked the Blessed One three times at [[Kapilavatthu]] to allow women to go forth, and was refused. She cut her hair, put on robes and walked with many Sakyan women to [[Vesālī]], arriving with swollen feet, covered in dust and in tears. The Venerable [[Ānanda]] interceded, asking whether women were able to attain the fruits of the path. The Blessed One said they were, and allowed her ordination on condition that she accept the eight principles of respect. She accepted them, she said, as a young woman would accept a garland of lotuses on her head.[^vin]

## The robe

In the Dakkhiṇāvibhaṅga Sutta, Mahāpajāpatī offered the Blessed One a robe she had made herself. The Blessed One told her to give it to the Saṅgha, explaining that a gift to the Saṅgha is of greater fruit, and also acknowledged her great kindness in raising the Bodhisatta and her own debt of gratitude to the Blessed One.[^mn142]

## Parinibbāna

She attained arahantship and, at the age of 120, passed into final Nibbāna together with five hundred nuns. Her verses in the [[Therīgāthā]] praise the Buddha who freed her from suffering.[^thi]
