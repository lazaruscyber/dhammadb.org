The **khandhas** ({{lang|Pāli|khandha}}), "aggregates" or "heaps", are the five groups into which the Blessed One analysed the whole of conditioned experience. What is taken to be a self is only these five, arising and passing away.[^sn2248]

## The five aggregates

| Aggregate | Pāli | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| Form | {{term|rūpa}} | The four great elements and matter derived from them |
| Feeling | {{term|vedanā}} | Pleasant, painful and neutral feeling |
| Perception | {{term|saññā}} | Recognising and noting colours, sounds and so on |
| Formations | {{term|saṅkhārā}} | Intention and the other mental factors |
| Consciousness | {{term|viññāṇa}} | Knowing an object through the six doors |

In [[Abhidhamma Piṭaka|Abhidhamma]] terms, form is [[Rūpa|matter]], consciousness is [[Citta|citta]], and the three middle aggregates are the fifty-two [[Cetasika|mental factors]].

## Aggregates subject to clinging

The five aggregates subject to clinging ({{term|pañcupādānakkhandhā}}) are, in brief, the noble truth of suffering.[^sn5611] The Blessed One taught that each aggregate is impermanent, suffering, and not to be regarded as "this is mine, this I am, this is my self" ([[Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta]]).

## Similes

In the Pheṇapiṇḍūpama Sutta the Blessed One compared form to a lump of foam, feeling to a water bubble, perception to a mirage, formations to a plantain trunk without heartwood, and consciousness to a magician's illusion.[^sn2295]
