**Kapilavatthu** ({{lang|Pāli|Kapilavatthu}}) was the capital of the Sakyan clan, in the foothills of the Himalaya. The Bodhisatta grew up there in the household of King [[Suddhodana]].[^jn]

## Founding

The [[Sumaṅgalavilāsinī]] relates that the sons of King Okkāka, exiled by their father, settled in the Himalaya near the hermitage of the sage Kapila, who gave them the site for their city; they named it after him. The sons married their sisters to keep the line pure, and when the king heard he exclaimed "{{term|sakyā}}", "they are capable", from which the Sakyans take their name.[^dna]

## The palace life

In the Sukhumāla Sutta the Blessed One recalls the luxury of youth: three palaces, one for winter, one for summer and one for the rains; garments of Kāsi cloth; and a white parasol held day and night. Yet the Bodhisatta reflected that the uninstructed worldling, though subject to ageing, sickness and death, is repelled and disgusted on seeing another who is aged, sick or dead; and the intoxication of youth, health and life was abandoned.[^an339]

## The return

The Blessed One returned to Kapilavatthu soon after the awakening, at the invitation of King Suddhodana. On that visit [[Rāhula]] and Nanda went forth, and later many Sakyan princes followed, among them [[Ānanda]], [[Anuruddha]] and [[Devadatta]]. The Nigrodhārāma monastery outside the city was given by the Sakyans.[^vin]

## Destruction

Towards the end of the Blessed One's life, the city was destroyed by Viḍūḍabha, son of King Pasenadi of Kosala, in revenge for an insult by the Sakyans.[^dhpa]
