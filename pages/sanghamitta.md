The nun **Saṅghamittā** ({{lang|Pāli|Saṅghamittā}}) was the daughter of the emperor [[Asoka]] and sister of the Venerable [[Mahinda]]. She went forth at the age of eighteen, on the same day as her brother.[^mhv]

## The mission to Sri Lanka

After Mahinda had brought the Dhamma to Sri Lanka, Queen Anulā and many women of the court wished to go forth. Since bhikkhus cannot give ordination to women alone, Mahinda asked the king to request his sister. Asoka reluctantly allowed her to go. She travelled by sea, accompanied by eleven nuns, bringing a branch of the southern side of the great Bodhi tree at [[Uruvelā]].[^mhv]

## The Bodhi tree

The branch was planted in the Mahāmegha park at [[Anurādhapura]] by King Devānampiya Tissa. Known as the Jayamahābodhi, it is venerated today as the oldest historically recorded tree planted by human hands.[^mhv] Saplings from it were planted throughout the island.

## The order of nuns

Saṅghamittā ordained Queen Anulā and her five hundred companions. The nunnery built for them in Anurādhapura was called the Upāsikāvihāra. She passed away at the age of fifty-nine.[^mhv] In Sri Lanka the full moon of the month of Phussa, Unduvap, is celebrated in her memory.

[^mhv]: *Mahāvaṃsa*. Chapters XVIII to XX.
