The **Āṭānāṭiya Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Āṭānāṭiya Sutta}}) is the thirty-second discourse of the [[Dīgha Nikāya]] and one of the protective discourses ([[Paritta]]).

## Occasion

The Four Great Kings came to the Blessed One at night on the Vulture Peak. The Great King Vessavaṇa said that some of the spirits ({{term|yakkha}}) were not devoted to the Blessed One, and that bhikkhus and nuns dwelling in remote places might be disturbed by them. He offered a protection, the Āṭānāṭiya verses, for the guarding and protection of the Blessed One's disciples. The next day the Blessed One taught the verses to the bhikkhus.[^dn32]

## Contents

The verses pay homage to the seven Buddhas of the past, from Vipassī to Gotama, and to the Four Great Kings who guard the four quarters. They declare that any spirit who troubles a disciple reciting them will be restrained by the spirits' own leaders. A version of the verses is recited in the Mahāparitta chanting of Myanmar.

[^dn32]: Dīgha Nikāya 32, *Āṭānāṭiya Sutta*.
