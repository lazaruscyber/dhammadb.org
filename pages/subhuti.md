The Venerable **Subhūti** ({{lang|Pāli|Subhūti}}) was the younger brother of the householder [[Anāthapiṇḍika]]. The Blessed One declared him foremost in two qualities: dwelling without conflict ({{term|araṇavihārī}}) and being worthy of gifts ({{term|dakkhiṇeyya}}).[^an1]

## Going forth

Subhūti went forth after hearing the Blessed One teach at the dedication of the [[Jetavana]] monastery. He took meditation on [[Mettā|loving-kindness]] as his subject and attained arahantship on that basis.[^tha]

## Dwelling without conflict

The Araṇavibhaṅga Sutta describes the way of no conflict: not pursuing sensual pleasure or self-mortification, not praising or disparaging, knowing the right kind of pleasure, not speaking secretly or harshly, not hurrying, and not insisting on local dialect. The Blessed One concluded that Subhūti practised this way.[^mn139]

## Worthy of gifts

The commentary says that on almsround Subhūti entered the attainment of loving-kindness at each house before receiving food, so that the donors would receive the greatest fruit.[^tha]

## The verse

The [[Theragāthā]] records his verse when, during a rainy season, King Bimbisāra forgot to roof his hut: "My hut is roofed, pleasant, sheltered from the wind. Rain, sky-god, as you please! My mind is well concentrated and freed. I am ardent. Rain, sky-god!"[^th]
