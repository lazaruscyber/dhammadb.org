Pāli: {{term|Āsava}}
Meaning: Taint, canker, outflow
:::

**Āsava** ({{lang|Pāli|āsava}}) means a taint or canker. The commentaries explain the word as that which flows: the taints flow out through the sense doors, and like long-fermented liquor they have been steeping in the mind for a very long time.[^dha]

## The kinds

| Taint | Pāli |
|---|---|
| Taint of sensual desire | {{term|kāmāsava}} |
| Taint of existence | {{term|bhavāsava}} |
| Taint of views | {{term|diṭṭhāsava}} |
| Taint of ignorance | {{term|avijjāsava}} |

The suttas usually list three, omitting views; the [[Abhidhamma Piṭaka|Abhidhamma]] lists four.[^dhs]

## The destruction of the taints

The knowledge of the destruction of the taints ({{term|āsavakkhayañāṇa}}) is the third of the three true knowledges attained on the night of the awakening, and the highest fruit of the contemplative life in the [[Sāmaññaphala Sutta]]. An arahant is called "one whose taints are destroyed" ({{term|khīṇāsava}}).[^dn2]

## Abandoning

The [[Sabbāsava Sutta]] teaches seven ways of abandoning the taints: by seeing, restraining, using, enduring, avoiding, removing and developing.

[^dha]: *Sumaṅgalavilāsinī*, the commentary to the Dīgha Nikāya, introduction.
[^dhs]: *Dhammasaṅgaṇī*, Āsavagocchaka.
[^dn2]: Dīgha Nikāya 2, *Sāmaññaphala Sutta*.
