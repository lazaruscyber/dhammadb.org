The **Saṅgīti Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Saṅgīti Sutta}}), "the Discourse on Reciting Together", is the thirty-third discourse of the [[Dīgha Nikāya]]. It was spoken by the Venerable [[Sāriputta]] at the request of the Blessed One.[^dn33]

## Occasion

The Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta had recently died at Pāvā, and his followers had split into factions, quarrelling over his teaching. Seeing this, the Venerable Sāriputta proposed that the bhikkhus should recite the Dhamma together, without dispute, so that the holy life would last long for the welfare of many.[^dn33]

## Contents

Sāriputta then recited the teachings in numerical sets, from ones (such as "all beings are sustained by nutriment") through twos, threes and so on up to tens. At the end the Blessed One approved, saying "Good, good, Sāriputta." The sutta is a model for the communal recitation of the teaching and a forerunner of the Abhidhamma's lists.

[^dn33]: Dīgha Nikāya 33, *Saṅgīti Sutta*.
