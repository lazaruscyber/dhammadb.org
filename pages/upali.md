The Venerable **Upāli** ({{lang|Pāli|Upāli}}) was declared by the Blessed One foremost among the bhikkhus who knew the Vinaya by heart.[^an1]

## Going forth

Upāli was the barber of the Sakyan princes. When [[Anuruddha]], [[Ānanda]], Bhaddiya, Bhagu, Kimbila and [[Devadatta]] left home to go forth, he accompanied them. At the border they gave him their jewels and fine clothes and sent him back. He reflected that the Sakyans were fierce and might think he had killed the princes, and that if the princes could go forth, so could he. He returned to them, and the princes asked the Blessed One to ordain Upāli first, so that their pride would be humbled by having to pay respect to their former servant.[^vin]

## The Vinaya expert

Upāli received the whole Vinaya directly from the Blessed One and asked many questions about it, preserved in the Upālipañcaka of the [[Parivāra]]. The Blessed One praised his decisions.[^vin2]

## The First Council

At the [[First Council]], the Venerable [[Mahākassapa]] questioned Upāli on each rule: where it was laid down, concerning whom, about what, and what the rule and its exceptions were. Upāli's answers became the [[Vinaya Piṭaka]].[^vinc]

In the commentarial tradition, Upāli was the first teacher in the lineage of Vinaya teachers, passing the Vinaya to Dāsaka, who passed it on to Sonaka, Siggava and Moggaliputta Tissa, the president of the [[Third Council]].[^sp]
