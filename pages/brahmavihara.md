The four **brahmavihāras** ({{lang|Pāli|brahmavihāra}}) are the divine abidings or sublime dwellings. They are also called the four immeasurables ({{term|appamaññā}}) because they are directed to beings without limit.[^vism]

## The four

| Abiding | Characteristic | Near enemy | Far enemy |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Mettā|Loving-kindness]] | Promoting welfare | Selfish affection | Ill will |
| [[Karuṇā|Compassion]] | Removing suffering | Grief | Cruelty |
| [[Muditā|Appreciative joy]] | Gladness at others' success | Worldly merriment | Discontent |
| [[Upekkhā|Equanimity]] | Balance towards beings | Ignorant indifference | Greed and resentment |

The near and far enemies are from the [[Visuddhimagga]]: the far enemy is the obvious opposite, the near enemy is a state that resembles the abiding and can easily be mistaken for it.[^vism]

## The formula

"One dwells pervading one quarter with a mind imbued with loving-kindness, likewise the second, third and fourth. So above, below, around and everywhere, and to all as to oneself, one dwells pervading the all-encompassing world with a mind imbued with loving-kindness, abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility and without ill will." The same is said of the other three.[^mn7]

## Results

Developed to the [[Jhāna|jhānas]], the divine abidings lead to rebirth in the [[Brahmā]] realms. The first three can lead to the first three jhānas (or four in the fivefold scheme); equanimity leads to the fourth. Developed together with insight, they can lead to liberation.[^vism]

[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapter IX.
[^mn7]: Majjhima Nikāya 7, *Vatthūpama Sutta*.
