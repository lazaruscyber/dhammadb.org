The four **satipaṭṭhānas** ({{lang|Pāli|satipaṭṭhāna}}) are the foundations of mindfulness. They are the first group of the thirty-seven requisites of awakening ([[Bodhipakkhiyā dhammā]]), and are taught in detail in the [[Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta]] and the [[Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta]].[^mn10]

## The four foundations

| Foundation | Pāli | Counteracts the perversion that it is |
|---|---|---|
| Contemplation of the body | {{term|kāyānupassanā}} | Beautiful |
| Contemplation of feeling | {{term|vedanānupassanā}} | Pleasant |
| Contemplation of mind | {{term|cittānupassanā}} | Permanent |
| Contemplation of phenomena | {{term|dhammānupassanā}} | Self |

The commentaries explain that the four are taught to overcome the four perversions ({{term|vipallāsa}}), and that they suit different temperaments.[^mna]

## The formula

The bhikkhu dwells contemplating the body as a body, ardent, clearly comprehending and mindful, having put away covetousness and grief for the world. One contemplates internally, externally, and both; one contemplates arising, passing away, and both; mindfulness that "there is a body" is established just to the extent needed for knowledge and mindfulness; and one dwells independent, not clinging to anything in the world.

## The bird and the hawk

The Blessed One told of a quail who strayed from its own field, the ploughed land full of clods, and was seized by a hawk. One's own field, the Blessed One said, is the four foundations of mindfulness; the field of others, where Māra finds a foothold, is the five strands of sensual pleasure.[^sn476]
