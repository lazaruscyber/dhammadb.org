**Ānāpānasati** ({{lang|Pāli|ānāpānasati}}) is mindfulness of the in-breath and out-breath. It is taught in the [[Ānāpānassati Sutta]], in the [[Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta]] and in the Ānāpāna Saṃyutta. The commentaries say that it was by this meditation that the Bodhisatta attained the jhānas on the night of the awakening.[^vism]

## Praise

The Blessed One called it peaceful and sublime, an unadulterated blissful abiding, which banishes and stills evil unwholesome states as soon as they arise, just as a great rain disperses the dust of the last month of the hot season.[^sn549] During one rains retreat the Blessed One spent three months in solitude, dwelling mostly in mindfulness of breathing, and called it the Tathāgata's dwelling.[^sn5411]

## Practice

The meditator goes to the forest, the root of a tree or an empty hut, sits cross-legged with body erect, and establishes mindfulness in front. Mindful, one breathes in; mindful, one breathes out. The [[Visuddhimagga]] teaches the stages of counting, connection, touching and fixing, and compares the meditator to a gatekeeper who watches only those passing through the gate, the point where the breath touches the nostril or upper lip.[^vism]

## A subject for great beings

The Visuddhimagga calls mindfulness of breathing the subject of the Buddhas, Paccekabuddhas and great disciples. It is hard to develop, because the breath becomes more and more subtle as the mind calms. It leads to all four jhānas and, through the last tetrad of the sixteen steps, to insight.[^vism]
