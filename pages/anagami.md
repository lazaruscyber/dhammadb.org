An **anāgāmī** ({{lang|Pāli|anāgāmī}}) is a non-returner, the third of the four noble stages ([[Ariya-puggala]]). The non-returner has abandoned all five lower fetters ([[Saṃyojana]]): personality view, doubt, clinging to rules and observances, sensual desire and ill will.[^an1013]

## Rebirth

Since sensual desire is abandoned, the non-returner is never again reborn in the sense sphere. If not attaining arahantship in this life, the non-returner is reborn spontaneously in the fine-material realms, usually in one of the five Pure Abodes ({{term|suddhāvāsa}}), and attains final Nibbāna there.[^abhs]

## Five kinds

The suttas describe five kinds of non-returner according to when they attain final Nibbāna: within the first half of the life span there, after the middle of it, without exertion, with exertion, and one who goes upstream to the highest Pure Abode, Akaniṭṭha.[^an4]

## Qualities

A non-returner has fulfilled virtue and concentration. The non-returner may still have the five higher fetters: desire for fine-material and immaterial existence, conceit, restlessness and ignorance. Among the Blessed One's lay followers, the potter Ghaṭīkāra and many devas of the Pure Abodes are named as non-returners. The Pure Abode Brahmās are said to have appeared to the Bodhisatta as the four sights.
