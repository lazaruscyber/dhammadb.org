The **Ariyapariyesana Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Ariyapariyesana Sutta}}), "The Discourse on the Noble Search", is the twenty-sixth discourse of the Majjhima Nikāya. In it the Blessed One describes two kinds of search and then recounts, in the Tathāgata's own words, the going forth, the striving under two teachers, the awakening, the decision to teach, and the first teaching to the group of five monks.[^mn26] It is one of the main canonical sources for the events before the [[Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta]].

## Setting

A group of monks wished to hear a talk on the Dhamma from the Blessed One and asked the Venerable Ānanda to help. Ānanda suggested they go to the hermitage of the brahmin Rammaka near Sāvatthī. When the Blessed One arrived there in the evening, the monks were sitting in discussion; the Blessed One waited outside until their talk ended and then entered. Finding that they had been speaking on the Dhamma, the Blessed One told them that when monks gather they should do one of two things: discuss the Dhamma, or keep noble silence.[^mn26]

## The two searches

The Blessed One then spoke of two kinds of search:[^mn26]

- **The ignoble search** ({{term|anariyā pariyesanā}}): being oneself subject to birth, ageing, sickness, death, sorrow and defilement, one seeks what is likewise subject to them: wife and children, servants, animals, gold and silver.
- **The noble search** ({{term|ariyā pariyesanā}}): being oneself subject to birth and the rest, and understanding the danger in what is subject to them, one seeks the unborn, unageing, unailing, deathless, sorrowless and undefiled supreme security from bondage, [[Nibbāna]].

## The account of the noble search

### Going forth

The Blessed One recounted that before the awakening, while still a Bodhisatta, the same reflection had arisen. While still young, a black-haired youth in the prime of life, the Bodhisatta shaved off hair and beard, put on the ochre robe and went forth into homelessness, though the mother and father wept with tearful faces.[^mn26]

### Two teachers

The Bodhisatta first went to Āḷāra Kālāma and quickly attained the base of nothingness ({{term|ākiñcaññāyatana}}) that Āḷāra taught. Āḷāra offered to lead the community jointly, but the Bodhisatta saw that this teaching led only to rebirth in that base, not to Nibbāna, and left. The Bodhisatta then went to Uddaka Rāmaputta and attained the base of neither perception nor non-perception ({{term|nevasaññānāsaññāyatana}}), which Uddaka's father Rāma had realised. Uddaka offered to make the Bodhisatta the teacher of the whole community; but again, seeing that it did not lead to Nibbāna, the Bodhisatta left.[^mn26]

### The awakening

Wandering in Magadha, the Bodhisatta came to Senānigama near Uruvelā, a delightful place with a pleasant grove and a clear-flowing river, and resolved to strive there. There the Bodhisatta attained the supreme security from bondage, Nibbāna, and the knowledge and vision arose: "My liberation is unshakeable; this is my last birth; now there is no renewed existence."[^mn26]

### The decision to teach

The Blessed One reflected that the Dhamma attained was profound, hard to see, peaceful and sublime, beyond the reach of mere reasoning, while people delighted in attachment; to teach it might bring only weariness. Then the Brahmā Sahampati appeared and pleaded that there were beings with little dust in their eyes who would understand. Surveying the world with the eye of a Buddha, the Blessed One saw beings like lotuses in a pond, some below the water, some at its surface, and some standing clear of it, and consented to teach.[^mn26]

The Blessed One first thought of Āḷāra Kālāma, but a deity announced that Āḷāra had died seven days before; then of Uddaka Rāmaputta, who had died the previous night. The Blessed One then thought of the group of five monks and set out for Bārāṇasī.[^mn26]

### Upaka and the five monks

On the road between Gayā and the place of awakening the Blessed One met the Ājīvaka Upaka, who asked about the Blessed One's teacher, and was answered that the Fully Enlightened One had no teacher. Upaka replied "May it be so, friend", shook his head and went off by a side road.[^mn26]

At Isipatana the five monks at first agreed not to rise to greet the Blessed One, but could not keep their agreement. When they addressed the Blessed One as "friend", the Blessed One told them not to address the Tathāgata in that way and declared that the Deathless had been attained. Asked whether the Tathāgata had ever spoken like this before, they admitted not, and they listened. Instructed by the Blessed One, they too attained the unborn, supreme security from bondage, Nibbāna.[^mn26]

## The five cords of sensual pleasure

The Blessed One ended by teaching the five cords of sensual pleasure: forms, sounds, odours, flavours and tangibles that are desired, agreeable and connected with sensual desire. Ascetics who enjoy them while tied to them are like a deer lying caught on a heap of snares, which can be done with as the hunter wishes. Those who are not tied to them are like a deer lying on a heap of snares without being caught. The discourse closes with a description of the jhānas and the destruction of the taints, by which a monk is said to have blinded Māra and gone beyond attachment to the world.[^mn26]
