The **Sabbāsava Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Sabbāsava Sutta}}), "All the Taints", is the second discourse of the [[Majjhima Nikāya]]. The Blessed One teaches that the destruction of the taints ([[Āsava]]) is for one who knows and sees, for one who attends wisely ({{term|yoniso manasikāra}}), and not for one who attends unwisely.[^mn]

## Unwise attention

Unwise attention takes the form of questions such as "Was I in the past? What was I in the past? Shall I be in the future? Am I? Am I not?" From such attention arise views of self, the thicket of views that binds the worldling to suffering. The noble disciple instead attends wisely to the [[Four Noble Truths]].

## The seven methods

| Taints abandoned by | Example |
|---|---|
| Seeing ({{term|dassana}}) | Attending wisely to the Four Noble Truths |
| Restraining ({{term|saṃvara}}) | Restraint of the eye and other senses |
| Using ({{term|paṭisevana}}) | Using robes, food, lodging and medicine with reflection |
| Enduring ({{term|adhivāsana}}) | Patiently bearing cold, heat, hunger and harsh words |
| Avoiding ({{term|parivajjana}}) | Avoiding a wild elephant, a thorny place, bad friends |
| Removing ({{term|vinodana}}) | Not tolerating thoughts of sensual desire, ill will and cruelty |
| Developing ({{term|bhāvanā}}) | Developing the seven factors of awakening |

[^mn]: Majjhima Nikāya 2, *Sabbāsava Sutta*.
