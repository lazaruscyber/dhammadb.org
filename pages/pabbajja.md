**Pabbajjā** ({{lang|Pāli|pabbajjā}}), "going forth", is leaving the household life for homelessness. In the Vinaya it is the lower ordination, by which one becomes a novice ([[Sāmaṇera]]).[^vin]

## The going forth of the Bodhisatta

The going forth of the Bodhisatta is the model for every going forth. Having seen the four sights, the Bodhisatta left the palace at night on the horse Kaṇḍaka with the charioteer Channa, crossed the river Anomā, and cut off the long hair with a sword.[^jn] In the [[Ariyapariyesana Sutta]] the Blessed One recalls: "While still young, a black-haired young man endowed with the blessing of youth, in the prime of life, though my mother and father wished otherwise and wept with tearful faces, I shaved off my hair and beard, put on the yellow robe, and went forth from the home life into homelessness."[^mn26]

## The procedure

In the procedure allowed by the Blessed One, the candidate has head and beard shaved, puts on the robes, arranges the upper robe on one shoulder, pays respect to the bhikkhus, squats, and with joined hands recites the three refuges ([[Tisaraṇa]]) three times. The ten precepts are then given.[^vin]

## Household life

In the [[Sāmaññaphala Sutta]], the Blessed One describes the reflection that leads to going forth: "Household life is crowded and dusty; going forth is the open air. It is not easy, living in a home, to lead the holy life utterly perfect and pure as a polished shell."[^dn2]

[^jn]: Jātaka Commentary, *Nidānakathā*.
[^mn26]: Majjhima Nikāya 26, *Ariyapariyesana Sutta*.
