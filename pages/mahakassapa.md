The Venerable **Mahākassapa** ({{lang|Pāli|Mahākassapa}}) was declared by the Blessed One foremost among the bhikkhus who kept the ascetic practices ([[Dhutaṅga]]).[^an1] After the parinibbāna he called and presided over the [[First Council]].

## Early life

Born Pipphali in a wealthy brahmin family of Magadha, he did not wish to marry, but was married to Bhaddā Kāpilānī, who also wished to renounce. They lived together in celibacy, and after his parents' death both went forth, parting at a crossroads, he taking the right and she the left.[^tha]

## Meeting the Blessed One

He met the Blessed One seated at the foot of a tree between Rājagaha and Nāḷandā, and took the Blessed One as teacher. The Blessed One exchanged robes with him, giving him the Blessed One's worn hempen rag robe, an honour given to no other disciple. He attained arahantship on the eighth day.[^sn1611]

## Ascetic life

Mahākassapa lived in the forest, ate only almsfood, and wore rag robes throughout life. When the Blessed One urged him in old age to accept robes from householders and live near the Blessed One, he declined, saying he lived in this way for his own happiness and out of compassion for future generations.[^sn165] The Blessed One praised him as one who goes among families like the moon, withdrawn in body and mind.

## The First Council

On the way to [[Kusinārā]] after the parinibbāna he heard the old bhikkhu Subhadda say, "Do not grieve, friends; now we can do as we like." Resolving to protect the Dhamma and Vinaya, he convened 500 arahants at [[Rājagaha]] three months later. The funeral pyre of the Blessed One, the [[Mahāparinibbāna Sutta]] relates, would not ignite until Mahākassapa had arrived and paid respect.[^dn16]
