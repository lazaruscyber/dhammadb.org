**Taṇhā** ({{lang|Pāli|taṇhā}}), "thirst" or craving, is named in the second noble truth as the origin of suffering:

:::quote Saṃyutta Nikāya 56.11
It is this craving which leads to renewed existence, accompanied by delight and lust, seeking delight here and there; that is, craving for sensual pleasures, craving for existence, craving for non-existence.
:::

## The three kinds

| Kind | Pāli | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Sensual craving | {{term|kāmataṇhā}} | Craving for the pleasures of the five senses |
| Craving for existence | {{term|bhavataṇhā}} | Craving associated with the eternalist view |
| Craving for non-existence | {{term|vibhavataṇhā}} | Craving associated with the annihilationist view |

The commentaries also count six kinds by object (forms, sounds, odours, tastes, tangibles and mental objects), and by multiplying these reach 108 modes of craving.[^vism]

## In dependent origination

Craving is the eighth link of [[Paṭicca-samuppāda|dependent origination]]: with feeling as condition, craving arises; with craving as condition, clinging ([[Upādāna]]). The noble disciple breaks the chain at this point by not delighting in feeling.[^sn121]

## Its abandoning

The third noble truth is "the remainderless fading away and cessation of that same craving, its giving up and relinquishing, freedom from it, non-reliance on it". The [[Dhammapada]] says: "The destruction of craving conquers all suffering."[^dhp354]

[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapter XVII.
[^sn121]: Saṃyutta Nikāya 12.1.
[^dhp354]: Dhammapada 354.
