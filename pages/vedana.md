**Vedanā** ({{lang|Pāli|vedanā}}) is feeling: the affective quality of experiencing an object as pleasant, painful or neither-painful-nor-pleasant. It is the second of the five aggregates ([[Khandha]]), one of the seven universal mental factors ([[Cetasika]]), and the seventh link of [[Paṭicca-samuppāda|dependent origination]].[^sn36]

## Kinds of feeling

| Scheme | Feelings |
|---|---|
| Threefold | Pleasant, painful, neither |
| Fivefold | Bodily pleasure, bodily pain, mental joy, mental displeasure, equanimity |
| Sixfold | Born of eye-contact, ear-contact, nose-contact, tongue-contact, body-contact, mind-contact |

The Blessed One said that feelings could be counted as two, three, five, six, eighteen, thirty-six or one hundred and eight, depending on the method of explanation.[^sn3622]

## The two darts

The Sallatha Sutta compares a worldling who feels bodily pain and then grieves and laments to a person struck by a dart and then by a second dart. The noble disciple feels only the first, bodily feeling and not the mental one.[^sn366]

## Feeling and the latent tendencies

Pleasant feeling, when not understood, gives rise to the latent tendency to lust; painful feeling to the latent tendency to aversion; neutral feeling to the latent tendency to ignorance.[^mn44]

## Contemplation

Contemplation of feelings ({{term|vedanānupassanā}}) is the second of the four foundations of mindfulness ([[Satipaṭṭhāna]]).
