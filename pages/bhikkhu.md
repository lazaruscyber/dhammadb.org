A **bhikkhu** ({{lang|Pāli|bhikkhu}}) is a fully ordained member of the Saṅgha, one who has received the higher ordination ([[Upasampadā]]). The word means "one who lives on alms", and the commentaries also explain it as "one who sees danger in saṃsāra". A bhikkhu lives by the training rules of the [[Pātimokkha]], which in the Theravāda number 227.[^vin]

## Ordination

A man becomes a bhikkhu through the higher ordination ([[Upasampadā]]), which requires an assembly of bhikkhus within a [[Sīmā|boundary]], a preceptor, and a formal act of the Saṅgha with motion and three proclamations. He must be at least twenty years old, counted from conception, and free of certain impediments.[^vin]

## The life of a bhikkhu

The bhikkhu lives on the four [[Requisites|requisites]] offered by lay people: robes, almsfood, lodging and medicine. He does not handle money, eats only between dawn and midday, observes celibacy, and keeps the three-month rains residence ([[Vassa]]). Every fortnight, on the [[Uposatha]], the bhikkhus gather to hear the recitation of the Pātimokkha.

## Seniority

Seniority among bhikkhus is counted by the number of rains since higher ordination. A bhikkhu of fewer than five rains must live under a teacher; one of ten rains or more who is learned may be a preceptor. A bhikkhu of twenty rains is called a {{term|mahāthera}}, a great elder.

## The first bhikkhus

The first bhikkhu was the Venerable [[Aññā Koṇḍañña]], ordained by the Blessed One with the words "Come, bhikkhu" ({{term|ehi bhikkhu}}) after hearing the [[Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta]].[^vin2]

## Leaving the robe

A bhikkhu may give up the training at any time by declaring it before a witness. In Myanmar and Thailand it is common for men to ordain temporarily.

[^vin]: Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga 1, and the Suttavibhaṅga.
[^vin2]: Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga 1.6.
