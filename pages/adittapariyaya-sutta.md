The **Ādittapariyāya Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Ādittapariyāya Sutta}}), "the Discourse on Burning", known as the Fire Sermon, is found in the Saḷāyatana Saṃyutta of the [[Saṃyutta Nikāya]] and in the Mahāvagga of the Vinaya. It is traditionally counted as the third discourse of the Buddha, after the [[Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta]] and the [[Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta]].[^vin]

## Occasion

After the first rains, the Blessed One went to [[Uruvelā]] and converted the three Kassapa brothers, matted-hair ascetics who worshipped the sacred fire, together with their thousand followers. With them the Blessed One went to Gayāsīsa, the hill near Gayā, and there taught this discourse.[^vin]

## Teaching

"Bhikkhus, all is burning. And what is the all that is burning? The eye is burning, visible forms are burning, eye-consciousness is burning, eye-contact is burning, and whatever is felt as pleasant, painful or neither, arising with eye-contact as condition, that too is burning. Burning with what? Burning with the fire of lust, with the fire of hatred, with the fire of delusion; burning with birth, ageing and death, with sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief and despair, I say."[^sn3528]

The same is said of the ear, nose, tongue, body and mind. Seeing thus, the instructed noble disciple becomes disenchanted, and through disenchantment becomes dispassionate; through dispassion the mind is liberated.

While the discourse was being spoken, the minds of the thousand bhikkhus were liberated from the taints by not clinging.[^sn3528]

[^vin]: Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga 1 (Mahākhandhaka).
[^sn3528]: Saṃyutta Nikāya 35.28, *Ādittapariyāya Sutta*.
