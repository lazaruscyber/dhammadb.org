**Pañcasīla** ({{lang|Pāli|pañcasīla}}), the Five Precepts, are the basic moral training of lay followers. They are undertaken after the [[Tisaraṇa|three refuges]] at almost every Theravāda ceremony.[^khp2]

## The precepts

| No. | Pāli | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Pāṇātipātā veramaṇī sikkhāpadaṃ samādiyāmi.* | I undertake the training rule to abstain from killing living beings. |
| 2 | *Adinnādānā veramaṇī sikkhāpadaṃ samādiyāmi.* | I undertake the training rule to abstain from taking what is not given. |
| 3 | *Kāmesu micchācārā veramaṇī sikkhāpadaṃ samādiyāmi.* | I undertake the training rule to abstain from sexual misconduct. |
| 4 | *Musāvādā veramaṇī sikkhāpadaṃ samādiyāmi.* | I undertake the training rule to abstain from false speech. |
| 5 | *Surāmerayamajjapamādaṭṭhānā veramaṇī sikkhāpadaṃ samādiyāmi.* | I undertake the training rule to abstain from intoxicating drinks that cause heedlessness. |

## Five great gifts

The Blessed One called keeping the five precepts five great gifts: by abstaining from these actions one gives to countless beings freedom from fear, freedom from hostility and freedom from oppression, and in return receives the same.[^an839]

## Breaking a precept

The commentaries explain the factors that make up a breach of each precept. For killing, there must be a living being, knowledge that it is a living being, the intention to kill, an effort, and death resulting from it.[^khpa] The seriousness of the action depends on the virtue of the being and the strength of the effort and defilement.

## The eight precepts

On [[Uposatha]] days many lay followers take eight precepts: the third precept becomes complete celibacy, and three more are added, abstaining from food after midday, from entertainment and adornment, and from high and luxurious seats and beds.

[^an839]: Aṅguttara Nikāya 8.39, *Abhisanda Sutta*.
[^khpa]: *Paramatthajotikā*, the commentary to the Khuddakapāṭha, on the training rules.
