A **kasiṇa** ({{lang|Pāli|kasiṇa}}), "totality", is a meditation object used to develop concentration. The meditator fixes attention on a physical device until its image appears in the mind, and then extends that image to fill all directions. There are ten kasiṇas: earth, water, fire, air, blue, yellow, red, white, light and limited space.[^vism]

## The earth kasiṇa

The [[Visuddhimagga]] describes the earth kasiṇa in detail. The meditator makes a disc about a span and four fingers across, of clay the colour of the dawn, on a frame, and sits at a suitable distance gazing at it while repeating "earth, earth". When the image appears as clearly with eyes closed as with eyes open, this is the learning sign. Continuing, the counterpart sign arises, clear and bright like a mirror taken from its case or the moon emerging from a cloud. With the counterpart sign the mind reaches access concentration and then absorption.[^vism]

## Uses

All ten kasiṇas lead to the four (or five) [[Jhāna|jhānas]]. The limited-space kasiṇa, with the meditator removing the kasiṇa image, is the basis for the immaterial attainments. Kasiṇa meditation is also the traditional basis for the development of the supernormal powers ({{term|abhiññā}}).[^vism]

## Natural kasiṇas

One who has developed the practice in past lives may be able to use a natural object, such as a ploughed field or a pool of water, as a kasiṇa without making a device.

[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapter IV and V.
