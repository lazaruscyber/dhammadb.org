**Isipatana** ({{lang|Pāli|Isipatana}}), the Deer Park ({{term|Migadāya}}) near Bārāṇasī, is where the Blessed One taught the first discourse, the [[Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta]], to the group of five ascetics. It is the third of the four places of pilgrimage named in the [[Mahāparinibbāna Sutta]].[^dn16]

## The name

The commentaries explain the name as "the place where sages alighted": Paccekabuddhas, coming through the air from the Himalaya, would alight there, and at the end of their lives would rise into the air and attain final Nibbāna there.[^spk] Deer could roam it without fear, hence the Deer Park.

## The first discourse

After the awakening, the Blessed One reflected on who would understand the Dhamma first. Seeing that the five ascetics who had attended the Bodhisatta during the austerities were living at Isipatana, the Blessed One walked there from [[Uruvelā]]. On the full moon of [[Āsāḷha]], the Blessed One taught the Middle Way and the [[Four Noble Truths]], and the Venerable [[Aññā Koṇḍañña]] attained stream-entry. Five days later, on hearing the [[Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta]], all five became arahants.[^vin]

## Yasa

At Isipatana the young man Yasa of Bārāṇasī, weary of household life, came to the Blessed One at night and became an arahant, followed by fifty-four of his friends. From there the Blessed One sent out the first sixty arahants: "Wander forth, bhikkhus, for the welfare of the many, for the happiness of the many, out of compassion for the world."[^vin]

## Other teachings

The [[Saccavibhaṅga Sutta]] was also taught at Isipatana.
