**Paññatti** ({{lang|Pāli|paññatti}}) means concept or designation. In the [[Abhidhamma Piṭaka|Abhidhamma]] it is contrasted with the ultimate realities ([[Paramattha]]). Concepts do not exist by their own nature; they are constructed by the mind from ultimate realities or from other concepts.[^abhs]

## Two aspects

The [[Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha]] explains concept in two aspects:[^abhs]

| Aspect | Pāli | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Concept as meaning | {{term|atthapaññatti}} | The thing made known, such as "person", "house", "direction", "time" |
| Concept as name | {{term|nāmapaññatti}} | The name that makes it known |

## Kinds of concept as meaning

Concepts as meaning include shapes of the land and mountains, constructions such as chariots and villages, beings and persons, directions and times, space, and the signs that arise in meditation, such as the counterpart sign of a [[Kasiṇa|kasiṇa]].

## Meditation

Tranquillity ([[Samatha]]) meditation often takes a concept as its object, such as the counterpart sign or "beings" in the [[Brahmavihāra|divine abidings]]. Insight ([[Vipassanā]]) meditation must take ultimate realities as its object, since only these show the three characteristics.[^vism]

## The Puggalapaññatti

The [[Puggalapaññatti]], the fourth book of the Abhidhamma Piṭaka, begins with a list of the kinds of designation before describing types of persons.

[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapter XVIII.
