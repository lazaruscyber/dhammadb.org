The **saṅghādisesa** ({{lang|Pāli|saṅghādisesa}}) rules are thirteen offences of the [[Pātimokkha]], second in seriousness only to the [[Pārājika|pārājika]]. The name means that the Saṅgha is needed at the beginning and at the end of the procedure for rehabilitation.[^vin]

## The rules

The thirteen include:

- Intentional emission of semen, except in a dream.
- Bodily contact, lewd speech, or speaking in praise of sexual services to a woman with a lustful mind.
- Acting as a go-between in arranging a marriage or affair.
- Building a hut without the Saṅgha's approval or of excessive size.
- Groundlessly accusing a bhikkhu of a pārājika offence.
- Attempting to cause a schism in the Saṅgha, or supporting one who does, and persisting after three admonitions.
- Being difficult to admonish, and persisting after three admonitions.
- Corrupting families and refusing to leave when asked.[^vin]

The schism rule was laid down in response to [[Devadatta]], who tried to split the Saṅgha.

## Rehabilitation

A bhikkhu who has committed a saṅghādisesa must confess it and undergo a probation ({{term|mānatta}}) of six nights, during which he loses certain privileges and must report his offence to every bhikkhu he meets. If he concealed the offence, he must first undergo an additional probation ({{term|parivāsa}}) for as many days as it was concealed. He is then rehabilitated by a Saṅgha of at least twenty bhikkhus.[^vin]

[^vin]: Vinaya Piṭaka, *Suttavibhaṅga*, Saṅghādisesakaṇḍa; Cūḷavagga 2 and 3.
