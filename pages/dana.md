**Dāna** ({{lang|Pāli|dāna}}) is giving or generosity. It is the first of the three bases of merit ([[Puñña]]), the first of the ten [[Pāramī|perfections]], and the first topic of the Blessed One's graduated talk ({{term|anupubbikathā}}), which begins with giving, virtue and the heavens before teaching the [[Four Noble Truths]].[^dn3]

## Purity of a gift

The Blessed One taught that a gift is purified in three ways: by the giver, by the receiver, or by both. The Dakkhiṇāvibhaṅga Sutta lists fourteen kinds of personal offering, from gifts to the Tathāgata down to gifts to animals, and seven kinds of offering to the Saṅgha, which are greater still.[^mn142]

The giver should be happy before giving, have a confident heart while giving, and be satisfied after giving.[^an637]

## Kinds of giving

| Kind | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gift of material things ({{term|āmisadāna}}) | Food, robes, lodging, medicine |
| Gift of fearlessness ({{term|abhayadāna}}) | Keeping the precepts, so that beings need not fear one |
| Gift of the Dhamma ({{term|dhammadāna}}) | Teaching the Dhamma |

"The gift of the Dhamma excels all gifts," says the [[Dhammapada]].[^dhp354]

## Giving to the Saṅgha

Offering the four requisites ([[Requisites]]) to the monastic community is the chief form of giving in Theravāda countries: almsfood on the morning round, robes at the [[Kathina]], lodgings and medicine. The Saṅgha is called the unsurpassed field of merit for the world.

## Examples

Among the Blessed One's great donors were [[Anāthapiṇḍika]], who bought the [[Jetavana]] by covering the ground with gold coins, and [[Visākhā]], who built the Eastern Park monastery.
