**Saṃsāra** ({{lang|Pāli|saṃsāra}}), "wandering on", is the beginningless round of birth, ageing and death in which beings are reborn again and again in the [[Thirty-one planes of existence|planes of existence]], according to their [[Kamma|kamma]].

## Without discoverable beginning

:::quote Saṃyutta Nikāya 15.3
Bhikkhus, this saṃsāra is without discoverable beginning. A first point is not discerned of beings roaming and wandering on, hindered by ignorance and fettered by craving.
:::

In the Anamatagga Saṃyutta, the Blessed One gives many similes for its length. The tears shed by a being through this long journey, weeping over the death of mother, father, brother, sister and child, are more than the water of the four great oceans. The bones of one person in a single aeon would make a heap as high as Mount Vepulla.[^sn15]

## The rarity of human birth

The Blessed One compared the chance of a being in the lower realms regaining human birth to that of a blind turtle, surfacing once every hundred years, putting its neck through a yoke with a single hole drifting on the great ocean.[^sn5647]

## Its cause and end

The round is sustained by ignorance ([[Avijjā]]) and craving ([[Taṇhā]]), and its working is set out in [[Paṭicca-samuppāda|dependent origination]]. It ends with the realisation of [[Nibbāna]], when no further birth remains. The arahant declares: "Birth is destroyed, the holy life has been lived, what had to be done has been done; there is no more coming to any state of being."[^mn]

[^sn5647]: Saṃyutta Nikāya 56.47, *Chiggaḷa Sutta*.
