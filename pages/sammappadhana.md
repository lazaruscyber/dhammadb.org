The four **sammappadhānas** ({{lang|Pāli|sammappadhāna}}) are the four right strivings. They are the second group of the thirty-seven requisites of awakening and are identical with right effort, the sixth factor of the [[Noble Eightfold Path]].[^sn4911]

## The four strivings

| Striving | Pāli |
|---|---|
| To prevent unarisen unwholesome states from arising | {{term|saṃvarappadhāna}} |
| To abandon arisen unwholesome states | {{term|pahānappadhāna}} |
| To arouse unarisen wholesome states | {{term|bhāvanāppadhāna}} |
| To maintain and perfect arisen wholesome states | {{term|anurakkhaṇappadhāna}} |

The formula says the bhikkhu "generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts the mind and strives" for each of these.[^sn4911]

## In the Aṅguttara

The Saṃvara Sutta names the four efforts as restraint (of the senses), abandoning (of unwholesome thoughts), development (of the factors of awakening) and protection (of a concentration sign such as a skeleton).[^an414]

## Removing thoughts

The Vitakkasaṇṭhāna Sutta teaches five methods for removing unwholesome thoughts: replacing them with a wholesome thought, reflecting on their danger, not attending to them, calming their formation, and finally crushing mind with mind.[^mn20]
