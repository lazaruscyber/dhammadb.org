A **sakadāgāmī** ({{lang|Pāli|sakadāgāmī}}) is a once-returner, the second of the four noble stages ([[Ariya-puggala]]). Having abandoned the first three fetters like the stream-enterer, the once-returner has also weakened sensual desire, ill will and delusion, and will come back to this world only once more before making an end of suffering.[^an1]

## Weakening, not abandoning

The once-returner has not abandoned any fetter beyond those abandoned at stream-entry. The difference lies in the attenuation of the defilements: sensual lust and ill will no longer arise strongly or often, and when they arise they are mild.[^vism]

## The meaning of "once"

The commentaries discuss which world is meant. One explanation is that a once-returner who attains in the human world is reborn once in a heavenly world and attains final Nibbāna there, or returns once to the human world. The traditional teaching recognises several kinds of once-returners according to where they attain the path and where they attain final Nibbāna.

## Lay once-returners

The suttas mention lay people who attained once-return, such as the father of Isidatta and the uncle Purāṇa, who lived celibate and non-celibate lives respectively, and whom the Blessed One declared to be of the same destination.[^an675]

[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapter XXII.
[^an675]: Aṅguttara Nikāya 6.44, *Migasālā Sutta*.
