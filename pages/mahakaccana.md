The Venerable **Mahākaccāna** ({{lang|Pāli|Mahākaccāna}}) was declared foremost among the bhikkhus who explain in detail the meaning of what was said in brief.[^an1]

## Early life

Born into the family of the royal chaplain of King Caṇḍappajjota of Ujjenī in Avanti, he was sent with seven companions to invite the Blessed One to Ujjenī. Hearing the Dhamma, all eight attained arahantship and went forth. The Blessed One sent Mahākaccāna back to Avanti, where he established the teaching.[^tha]

## Expositions

Several suttas record how, when the Blessed One gave a brief teaching and withdrew, the bhikkhus went to Mahākaccāna to explain it in detail:

- The Madhupiṇḍika Sutta (MN 18), on the origin of conceptual proliferation ({{term|papañca}}) in contact, feeling and perception. The Blessed One compared his explanation to a honey ball, sweet wherever it is tasted.[^mn18]
- The Bhaddekaratta Sutta's explanation (MN 133), on not pursuing the past or longing for the future.
- The Uddesavibhaṅga Sutta (MN 138).

## Ordination in border regions

His pupil Soṇa Kuṭikaṇṇa visited the Blessed One and, at Mahākaccāna's request, asked for concessions for Avanti, where few bhikkhus lived. The Blessed One allowed higher ordination with a quorum of five bhikkhus in border regions, along with other concessions.[^vin]

## Texts

The tradition ascribes to him the Nettippakaraṇa and the Peṭakopadesa, which are included in the [[Khuddaka Nikāya]] of the Sixth Council edition, and the oldest Pāli grammar, the Kaccāyana.
