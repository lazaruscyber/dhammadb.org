The **Sutta Piṭaka** ({{lang|Pāli|Sutta Piṭaka}}), "the Basket of Discourses", is the second of the three divisions of the [[Tipiṭaka]]. It contains the discourses ({{term|sutta}}) given by the Blessed One and by leading disciples, arranged in five collections ({{term|nikāya}}). Where the Vinaya Piṭaka sets out the discipline of the monastic order and the Abhidhamma Piṭaka analyses reality in exact terms, the Sutta Piṭaka presents the teaching in the conventional language suited to the many people and situations to whom it was given.

## The five collections

The commentaries give the following count of the discourses in each collection:[^dha]

| Collection | Pāli | Arrangement | Suttas (commentarial count) |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Long Discourses | {{term|Dīgha Nikāya}} | By length; three divisions | 34 |
| The Middle-length Discourses | {{term|Majjhima Nikāya}} | By length; three sets of fifty | 152 |
| The Connected Discourses | [[Saṃyutta Nikāya]] | By subject; 56 groups in five divisions | 7,762 |
| The Numerical Discourses | {{term|Aṅguttara Nikāya}} | By number, from ones to elevens | 9,557 |
| The Minor Collection | {{term|Khuddaka Nikāya}} | Miscellaneous | 15 books |


## The books of the Minor Collection

The commentaries list fifteen books in the Khuddaka Nikāya:[^dha]

:::columns 3
1. Khuddakapāṭha
2. Dhammapada
3. Udāna
4. Itivuttaka
5. Sutta Nipāta
6. Vimānavatthu
7. Petavatthu
8. Theragāthā
9. Therīgāthā
10. Jātaka
11. Niddesa
12. Paṭisambhidāmagga
13. Apadāna
14. Buddhavaṃsa
15. Cariyāpiṭaka

The edition of the Sixth Council, held in Yangon from 1954 to 1956, also includes three further works in the Khuddaka Nikāya: the Nettippakaraṇa, the Peṭakopadesa and the Milindapañha.

## Origin

After the parinibbāna of the Blessed One, five hundred arahants led by the Venerable Mahākassapa held the First Council at Rājagaha. The Venerable Upāli was questioned on the Vinaya, and the Venerable Ānanda on the Dhamma. Ānanda recited the discourses, each of which begins with the words *evaṃ me sutaṃ*, "Thus have I heard".[^cv11]

According to the commentaries, each collection was then entrusted to a group of bhikkhus to preserve by recitation: the Dīgha Nikāya to the Venerable Ānanda and his pupils, the Majjhima Nikāya to the pupils of the Venerable Sāriputta, the Saṃyutta Nikāya to the Venerable Mahākassapa and his pupils, and the Aṅguttara Nikāya to the Venerable Anuruddha and his pupils.[^dha]

## Commentaries

Each collection has its commentary ({{term|aṭṭhakathā}}), compiled in Pāli by the Venerable Buddhaghosa from the ancient commentaries preserved in Sri Lanka:

| Collection | Commentary |
|---|---|
| Dīgha Nikāya | *Sumaṅgalavilāsinī* |
| Majjhima Nikāya | *Papañcasūdanī* |
| Saṃyutta Nikāya | *Sāratthappakāsinī* |
| Aṅguttara Nikāya | *Manorathapūraṇī* |

The books of the Khuddaka Nikāya have their own commentaries, many by the Venerable Dhammapāla.
