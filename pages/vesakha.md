**Vesākha** ({{lang|Pāli|Vesākha}}) is the second month of the Theravāda lunar calendar in the traditional Pāli listing, falling in about April to May in the Gregorian calendar. Like every month of the calendar, it takes its name from the constellation ({{term|nakkhatta}}) near which the moon is full in that month. Its full-moon day is the most sacred day of the Theravāda year, on which the birth, the awakening and the parinibbāna of the Buddha are remembered.

## In the calendar

Vesākha has 30 days and belongs to the hot season ({{term|gimhāna}}), one of the three seasons of four months each into which the year is divided. It follows [[Citta]] and is followed by [[Jeṭṭha]]. Like every month, it contains four uposatha observance days: the eighth day of the waxing moon, the full moon, the eighth day of the waning moon and the new moon.

## Events

According to the commentaries, three of the great events in the life of the Buddha took place on the full-moon day of Vesākha:[^jn][^dn16a]

| Event | Place |
|---|---|
| The birth of the Bodhisatta | Lumbinī, between Kapilavatthu and Devadaha |
| The awakening, as the Fully Enlightened One | Beneath the Bodhi tree at Uruvelā |
| The parinibbāna | Between the twin sāla trees at Kusinārā |

The full moon of Vesākha is therefore observed across the Theravāda world, in Myanmar as the Full Moon of Kason and in Sri Lanka as Vesak Poya.
