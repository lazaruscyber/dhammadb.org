The **Rathavinīta Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Rathavinīta Sutta}}), "the Relay Chariots", is the twenty-fourth discourse of the [[Majjhima Nikāya]]. It records a conversation between the Venerable [[Sāriputta]] and the Venerable [[Puṇṇa Mantāniputta]], who had been praised by bhikkhus of his home region as one who lived and taught the Dhamma.[^mn]

Sāriputta asks Puṇṇa whether the holy life is lived for the sake of purification of virtue, of mind, of view and so on through seven purifications. Puṇṇa answers no to each. The holy life is lived for the sake of final Nibbāna without clinging.

He explains with a simile. King Pasenadi, travelling urgently from Sāvatthī to Sāketa, used seven relay chariots, each taking him only as far as the next. In the same way each purification serves only to reach the next, and the seventh to reach final Nibbāna without clinging.[^mn] These seven purifications ([[Satta visuddhi]]) later became the framework of the [[Visuddhimagga]].

[^mn]: Majjhima Nikāya 24, *Rathavinīta Sutta*.
