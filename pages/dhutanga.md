The **dhutaṅgas** ({{lang|Pāli|dhutaṅga}}), "factors for shaking off", are thirteen ascetic practices that a bhikkhu may undertake voluntarily to reduce wants, cultivate contentment and shake off defilements. They are described in the second chapter of the [[Visuddhimagga]].[^vism]

## The thirteen practices

| Group | Practices |
|---|---|
| Robes | Wearing rag robes; wearing only the three robes |
| Almsfood | Going on almsround; not skipping houses on the round; eating at one sitting; eating only from the bowl; refusing food offered later |
| Lodging | Living in the forest; at the root of a tree; in the open air; in a charnel ground; in any allotted lodging |
| Effort | The sitter's practice: never lying down |

## Grades

Each practice can be kept at three grades: strict, medium and mild. The Visuddhimagga explains how each is undertaken, its benefits, and what breaks it.[^vism]

## Not obligatory

The Blessed One refused [[Devadatta]]'s demand that some of these practices be made compulsory for all bhikkhus, allowing each to be kept by those who wished.[^vin]

## The great practitioner

The Venerable [[Mahākassapa]] was declared foremost among those who kept the ascetic practices. When the Blessed One urged him in old age to give them up, he declined, saying he kept them for his own comfortable abiding here and now and out of compassion for future generations, who would follow his example.[^sn515]

## The forest tradition

Bhikkhus who keep these practices, especially living in the forest, are known in Myanmar as {{term|tawya}} bhikkhus.
