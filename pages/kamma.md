**Kamma** ({{lang|Pāli|kamma}}) means "action". In the teaching of the Buddha it means intentional action. The Blessed One declared: "It is intention, bhikkhus, that I call kamma. Having intended, one acts by body, speech or mind."[^an663]

## Wholesome and unwholesome

Kamma rooted in greed, hatred or delusion ([[Akusala-mūla]]) is unwholesome and brings painful results; kamma rooted in non-greed, non-hatred and non-delusion ([[Kusala-mūla]]) is wholesome and brings pleasant results. The ten unwholesome courses of action are:

| Door | Unwholesome actions |
|---|---|
| Body | Killing, stealing, sexual misconduct |
| Speech | False speech, divisive speech, harsh speech, idle chatter |
| Mind | Covetousness, ill will, wrong view |

The ten wholesome courses are the abstention from these.

## Ownership of kamma

:::quote Aṅguttara Nikāya 5.57
I am the owner of my kamma, heir of my kamma, born of my kamma, bound to my kamma, and have my kamma as my refuge. Whatever kamma I do, good or evil, of that I shall be the heir.
:::

This is the first of the five subjects for frequent reflection.[^an557]

## Classes of kamma

The [[Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha]] classifies kamma by function (producing, supporting, obstructing and destroying), by order of ripening (weighty, near death, habitual, and reserve kamma), and by time of ripening (in this life, in the next life, in subsequent lives, and defunct).[^abhs]

## Not fatalism

Not everything experienced is the result of past kamma. The Blessed One rejected the view that whatever a person feels is caused by past action, since this would leave no room for present effort.[^an361] Kamma is one of the five orders ({{term|niyāma}}) described in the commentaries.

See also [[Vipāka]], [[Cūḷakammavibhaṅga Sutta]], [[Saṃsāra]].
