The **Vatthūpama Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Vatthūpama Sutta}}), "the Simile of the Cloth", is the seventh discourse of the [[Majjhima Nikāya]].

## The simile

A cloth that is stained and dirty takes dye badly, however good the dye; a clean cloth takes it well. In the same way, when the mind is defiled a bad destination may be expected; when it is undefiled, a good destination.[^mn]

## The defilements

The Blessed One names sixteen imperfections of the mind ({{term|cittassa upakkilesā}}), including covetousness, ill will, anger, revenge, contempt, envy, avarice, deceit, obstinacy, conceit, arrogance and negligence. Having understood and abandoned them, the disciple gains unwavering confidence in the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Saṅgha, dwells pervading the world with the four [[Brahmavihāra|divine abidings]], and attains liberation.

## Bathing within

The brahmin Sundarika Bhāradvāja, who believed that bathing in sacred rivers washed away evil, asked the Blessed One whether the Blessed One would go to bathe in the river Bāhukā. The Blessed One answered that no river can purify one who does evil deeds; one should bathe instead in harmlessness, truthfulness and generosity. The brahmin went forth and became an arahant.[^mn]
