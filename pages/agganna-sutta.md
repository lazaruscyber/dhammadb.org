The **Aggañña Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Aggañña Sutta}}), "the Discourse on Origins", is the twenty-seventh discourse of the [[Dīgha Nikāya]]. It was given to two young brahmins, Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja, who had gone forth and were being reviled by other brahmins for leaving the highest caste.[^dn27]

The Blessed One showed that brahmins are born of women like everyone else, and that people of every caste may do good or evil and reap the results. The Blessed One then told how, when the world re-evolves, beings come from the Ābhassara realm, how through craving for the savoury earth their bodies grew coarse and their radiance faded, and how property, theft, government and the social classes arose by convention.[^dn27]

The sutta concludes that the Dhamma is the best thing among people, both in this life and the next, and that one who is an arahant is declared chief among them.

[^dn27]: Dīgha Nikāya 27, *Aggañña Sutta*.
