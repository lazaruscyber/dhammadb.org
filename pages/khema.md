The nun **Khemā** ({{lang|Pāli|Khemā}}) was one of the two chief female disciples of the Blessed One, foremost among the nuns in great wisdom.[^an1]

## Pride in beauty

According to the commentary Khemā was the chief queen of King [[Bimbisāra]], and very beautiful. Having heard that the Blessed One spoke of the faults of beauty, she avoided visiting the Blessed One at the Bamboo Grove ([[Veḷuvana]]). The king had poets sing of the grove's beauty until she went to see it.[^dhpa]

## The vision

As she approached, the Blessed One caused her to see a beautiful woman fanning the Blessed One, more lovely than Khemā herself. As she watched, the woman grew old, grey and wrinkled and fell to the ground. Khemā's pride was broken. The Blessed One spoke the verse: "Those infatuated with lust fall back into the stream, as a spider into the web it has spun." At the end of the verse she attained arahantship, still in her lay clothes, and with the king's consent went forth.[^dhpa]

## The Khemā Sutta

In the Khemā Sutta, King Pasenadi asked her whether the Tathāgata exists after death. She answered that this has not been declared, and explained with a simile: just as one could not count the grains of sand in the Ganges or the water in the ocean, so the Tathāgata, released from the reckoning of form and the other aggregates, is deep and immeasurable. When the king later asked the Blessed One, the answer was the same, word for word.[^sn441]
