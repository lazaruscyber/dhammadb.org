The Venerable **Sāriputta** ({{lang|Pāli|Sāriputta}}), "son of Rūpasārī", was the chief disciple of the Blessed One, foremost among the bhikkhus in great wisdom.[^an1] The Blessed One called him the general of the Dhamma ({{term|dhammasenāpati}}), and said that just as the eldest son of a wheel-turning monarch keeps the wheel turning, so Sāriputta keeps rolling the Wheel of the Dhamma set rolling by the Tathāgata.[^mn111]

## Early life

Born Upatissa in a brahmin family in the village of Nālaka near [[Rājagaha]], he was a close friend from childhood of Kolita, later [[Mahāmoggallāna]]. Watching a festival, both realised the emptiness of worldly pleasures, and they went forth under the wanderer Sañjaya. Not satisfied, they agreed that whoever first found the Deathless would tell the other.[^vin]

## Meeting Assaji

Upatissa saw the Venerable Assaji, one of the first five disciples, on almsround in Rājagaha, and was struck by his serene bearing. Asked for the teaching, Assaji spoke one verse:

:::quote Vinaya, Mahāvagga 1.23
Of those things that arise from a cause, the Tathāgata has told the cause, and also what their cessation is. Such is the doctrine of the Great Recluse.
:::

On hearing it Upatissa attained stream-entry, and when he repeated it to Kolita, Kolita attained it too. The two went with 250 of Sañjaya's followers to the Blessed One at the Bamboo Grove ([[Veḷuvana]]) and were ordained.[^vin] Sāriputta attained arahantship two weeks later, while fanning the Blessed One during the teaching of the Dīghanakha Sutta.[^mn74]

## Teacher

Sāriputta taught many of the great discourses: the [[Sammādiṭṭhi Sutta]], the [[Mahāhatthipadopama Sutta]], the [[Saccavibhaṅga Sutta]] and the [[Saṅgīti Sutta]] among them. The commentaries relate that the Blessed One taught the Abhidhamma to Sāriputta in summary each day during the rains in the Tāvatiṃsa heaven, and that Sāriputta passed it on to his pupils in full.[^dhsa]

## Death

Sāriputta passed away before the Blessed One, in the room where he was born at Nālaka, after bringing his mother to stream-entry. His relics were brought by the novice Cunda to the Blessed One, who praised his virtue.[^sn4713]
