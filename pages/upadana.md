**Upādāna** ({{lang|Pāli|upādāna}}) means clinging or grasping. The word also means "fuel", as the grass and sticks on which a fire depends. Clinging is the ninth link of [[Paṭicca-samuppāda|dependent origination]]: with craving as condition, clinging arises; with clinging as condition, existence.[^sn122]

## The four kinds

| Clinging | Pāli |
|---|---|
| Clinging to sensual pleasures | {{term|kāmupādāna}} |
| Clinging to views | {{term|diṭṭhupādāna}} |
| Clinging to rules and observances | {{term|sīlabbatupādāna}} |
| Clinging to a doctrine of self | {{term|attavādupādāna}} |

The [[Visuddhimagga]] explains that clinging is craving made firm: craving is the longing for an object not yet reached, clinging is the grasping of an object reached.[^vism]

## The aggregates of clinging

The five aggregates ([[Khandha]]) when they are objects of clinging are called the aggregates subject to clinging ({{term|upādānakkhandhā}}), and in brief they are suffering. In the Cūḷavedalla Sutta the nun Dhammadinnā explains that clinging is neither the same as the five aggregates nor something apart from them; it is the desire and lust for them.[^mn44]

## Nibbāna without clinging

The holy life is lived for the sake of final Nibbāna without clinging ({{term|anupādā parinibbāna}}), as the Venerable Puṇṇa explained in the [[Rathavinīta Sutta]].

[^sn122]: Saṃyutta Nikāya 12.2, *Vibhaṅga Sutta*.
[^vism]: Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*. Chapter XVII.
[^mn44]: Majjhima Nikāya 44, *Cūḷavedalla Sutta*.
