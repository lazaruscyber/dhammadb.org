The **Ledi Sayadaw** (Pāli name Ñāṇadhaja) was one of the most influential Burmese bhikkhus of the modern era. A great scholar of the Abhidhamma, he also taught meditation and encouraged lay people to study and practise the Dhamma.[^ledi]

## Life

He was born in 1846 in Saing-pyin village in the Shwebo district of Upper Burma. He became a novice as a boy and received higher ordination at twenty. After many years of study in Mandalay, he retired to the Ledi forest near Monywa in 1886, where a monastery grew up; the name "Ledi Sayadaw" comes from it.

## Writings

The Sayadaw wrote more than seventy manuals, many in Pāli and many in Burmese for lay readers. The Paramattha Dīpanī, a sub-commentary on the [[Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha]], was well known for its criticism of earlier commentaries. Others include the Bodhipakkhiya Dīpanī on the [[Bodhipakkhiyā dhammā|thirty-seven requisites of awakening]], the Vipassanā Dīpanī, the Niyāma Dīpanī, the Anattā Dīpanī and the Magganga Dīpanī.

## Lay practice

At a time when meditation was largely confined to forest bhikkhus, the Ledi Sayadaw taught that lay people too could and should practise insight meditation in the present age. The Sayadaw taught mindfulness of breathing and contemplation of feeling, and encouraged the formation of Abhidhamma study groups. Among those taught was the lay teacher Saya Thetgyi, and through him a line of lay meditation teachers.

## Death

The Ledi Sayadaw passed away in 1923 in Pyinmana.
