The **Dīgha Nikāya** ({{lang|Pāli|Dīgha Nikāya}}), "the Long Discourses", is the first of the five collections of the [[Sutta Piṭaka]]. It contains thirty-four of the longest discourses, arranged in three divisions.[^dha] According to the commentaries it was entrusted after the [[First Council]] to the Venerable [[Ānanda]] and his pupils to preserve.[^dha]

## Divisions

| Division | Suttas | Notable suttas |
|---|---|---|
| {{term|Sīlakkhandhavagga}}, the Division on Virtue | DN 1–13 | [[Brahmajāla Sutta]], [[Sāmaññaphala Sutta]] |
| {{term|Mahāvagga}}, the Great Division | DN 14–23 | [[Mahāpadāna Sutta]], [[Mahānidāna Sutta]], [[Mahāparinibbāna Sutta]], [[Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta]] |
| {{term|Pāthikavagga}}, the Pāṭika Division | DN 24–34 | [[Aggañña Sutta]], [[Sigālovāda Sutta]], [[Āṭānāṭiya Sutta]], [[Saṅgīti Sutta]] |

The first division takes its name from the long passage on virtue repeated in each of its suttas. The second contains many of the "great" discourses. The third ends with two suttas, the Saṅgīti and Dasuttara, that collect teachings in numerical lists.

[^dha]: *Sumaṅgalavilāsinī*, the commentary to the Dīgha Nikāya, introduction (*nidānakathā*).
