The **Karaṇīyametta Sutta** ({{lang|Pāli|Karaṇīyametta Sutta}}), named after its opening words "what should be done", is the discourse on loving-kindness ([[Mettā]]). It is found in the [[Khuddakapāṭha]] and the [[Sutta Nipāta]] and is one of the most frequently recited [[Paritta|protective discourses]].[^khp9]

## Occasion

The commentary relates that five hundred bhikkhus went to meditate in a forest for the rains. The tree deities, unable to stay in their trees while the bhikkhus lived beneath them, frightened the bhikkhus with terrible sights, sounds and smells. The bhikkhus returned to the Blessed One, who taught them this sutta both as a meditation subject and as a protection. When they went back reciting it, the deities welcomed and protected them, and all five hundred became arahants by the end of the rains.[^khpa]

## Contents

The first part describes the qualities of one skilled in good, who wishes to attain the state of peace: able, upright, straightforward, easy to speak to, gentle, humble, contented, easily supported, with few duties, living simply, with calm senses, prudent, not insolent, and not greedy among families.

The second part is the wish for all beings:

:::quote Karaṇīyametta Sutta
*Sukhino vā khemino hontu, sabbe sattā bhavantu sukhitattā.*

May all beings be happy and secure; may they be happy at heart.
:::

It continues with the simile of the mother: just as a mother would protect her only child with her own life, so one should cultivate a boundless heart towards all beings, above, below and across, without obstruction, hatred or enmity, whether standing, walking, sitting or lying down. This is called the divine abiding here.

The last verse says that one who does not fall into views, who is virtuous and endowed with vision, and who has removed greed for sensual pleasures, will not come again to lie in a womb.[^khp9]
