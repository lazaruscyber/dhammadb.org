The four **iddhipādas** ({{lang|Pāli|iddhipāda}}) are the bases of spiritual power or success. They are the third group of the thirty-seven requisites of awakening.[^sn511]

## The four bases

| Base | Pāli |
|---|---|
| Desire to act | {{term|chanda}} |
| Energy | {{term|viriya}} |
| Mind | {{term|citta}} |
| Investigation | {{term|vīmaṃsā}} |

Each is developed as "concentration due to desire (energy, mind, investigation) accompanied by formations of striving". The commentaries explain that a person achieves concentration by making one of these four predominant.[^vism]

## Power

The bases of power are so named because they lead to the supernormal powers ({{term|iddhi}}), such as becoming many, walking on water and travelling through the air, and above all to the destruction of the taints.[^sn511]

## Prolonging life

In the [[Mahāparinibbāna Sutta]], the Blessed One told the Venerable [[Ānanda]] that one who has fully developed the four bases of power could, if wishing, remain for an aeon or the remainder of an aeon. Ānanda, his mind overcome by Māra, did not request the Blessed One to do so.[^dn16]

## In daily practice

The four are also known in Myanmar as the four roads to success in any undertaking, worldly or spiritual.
