**Paritta** ({{lang|Pāli|paritta}}), "protection", is the recitation of particular discourses of the Buddha to ward off danger, bring blessing, and calm the mind. The practice has its basis in the suttas themselves, where the Blessed One taught certain discourses as a protection, such as the [[Āṭānāṭiya Sutta]] given for protection against harmful spirits and the [[Karaṇīyametta Sutta]] given to frightened bhikkhus in a forest.[^dn32]

## How paritta works

The Theravāda understanding is that the protective power of paritta comes from the truth of the Dhamma it declares, from the virtue and loving-kindness of the one who recites, and from the faith and attention of the one who listens. In the [[Milindapañha]], the Venerable Nāgasena explains to King Milinda that paritta protects those who are not obstructed by the results of their own kamma, by defilements or by lack of faith; it does not protect those whose life span has come to an end.[^mil]

Several parittas contain an act of truth ({{term|saccakiriyā}}): a solemn declaration of something true, followed by a wish for well-being, as in the [[Ratana Sutta]] and the [[Aṅgulimāla Sutta|Aṅgulimāla Paritta]].

## Practice

In Myanmar the collection of eleven parittas recited together is called the Mahāparitta. Parittas are chanted by bhikkhus at blessings, house dedications, funerals, illnesses and the opening of new buildings; the chanting is often accompanied by a thread held by those present and a vessel of water, which are afterwards kept as blessed.
