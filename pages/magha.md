**Māgha** ({{lang|Pāli|Māgha}}) is the eleventh month of the Theravāda lunar calendar in the traditional Pāli listing, falling in about January to February in the Gregorian calendar. Like every month of the calendar, it takes its name from the constellation ({{term|nakkhatta}}) near which the moon is full in that month. Its full moon is remembered for the great assembly at which the Blessed One gave the Ovāda Pāṭimokkha.

## In the calendar

Māgha has 29 days and belongs to the cold season ({{term|hemanta}}), one of the three seasons of four months each into which the year is divided. It follows [[Phussa]] and is followed by [[Phagguṇa]]. Like every month, it contains four uposatha observance days: the eighth day of the waxing moon, the full moon, the eighth day of the waning moon and the new moon.

## Events

**The fourfold assembly.** According to the commentaries, on the full-moon day of Māgha in the first year after the awakening, 1,250 bhikkhus came together at the Bamboo Grove ({{term|Veḷuvana}}) in Rājagaha, where the Blessed One recited the Ovāda Pāṭimokkha, the exhortation that sums up the teaching.[^dn14a] The assembly is called fourfold ({{term|cāturaṅgika}}) because:

1. it took place on the full moon of Māgha;
2. the bhikkhus gathered without being summoned;
3. all of them were arahants;
4. all had been ordained by the Blessed One with the words "Come, bhikkhu".

**The relinquishing of the life process.** At the Cāpāla shrine near Vesālī the Blessed One relinquished the life process ({{term|āyusaṅkhāra}}) and announced that the parinibbāna would take place in three months.[^dn16] Since the parinibbāna fell on the full moon of [[Vesākha]], the commentarial tradition places this event on the full moon of Māgha.[^dn16a]

## Observances

The day of the assembly is observed in Thailand, Laos and Cambodia as Māgha Pūjā, and in Sri Lanka as Navam Poya.
