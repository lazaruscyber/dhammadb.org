**Paññā** ({{lang|Pāli|paññā}}) is wisdom: the understanding of things as they really are ({{term|yathābhūta}}). It is the third of the three trainings, growing out of [[Sīla|virtue]] and [[Samādhi|concentration]], and it is wisdom that finally liberates the mind. The Blessed One taught that the mind developed with wisdom is completely liberated from the taints of sensuality, existence and ignorance.[^dn16]

## In the Noble Eightfold Path

Two factors of the [[Noble Eightfold Path]] make up the training in wisdom:[^mn44]

| Factor | Pāli | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Right view | {{term|sammā-diṭṭhi}} | Knowledge of suffering, its origin, its cessation and the way leading to its cessation |
| Right intention | {{term|sammā-saṅkappa}} | Intentions of renunciation, of non-ill will and of harmlessness |

The Mahācattārīsaka Sutta distinguishes two kinds of right view. Mundane right view, still affected by the taints, knows that there is merit in giving, that actions bear fruit, and that there are this world and the next. Supramundane right view is the wisdom of the noble path itself.[^mn117]

## Meaning of the word

The *Visuddhimagga* defines wisdom as insight knowledge associated with profitable consciousness, and describes it as follows:[^vism14]

| Aspect | Description |
|---|---|
| Characteristic | Penetrating the individual essence of states |
| Function | Abolishing the darkness of delusion, which conceals the individual essence of states |
| Manifestation | Non-delusion |
| Proximate cause | Concentration, since one who is concentrated knows and sees things as they really are |

## Three kinds of wisdom

The suttas name three kinds of wisdom by the way they arise:[^dn33]

1. **Wisdom by learning** ({{term|sutamayā paññā}}): understanding gained by hearing and studying the Dhamma.
2. **Wisdom by reflection** ({{term|cintāmayā paññā}}): understanding gained by one's own thinking.
3. **Wisdom by development** ({{term|bhāvanāmayā paññā}}): understanding gained through meditation, which alone reaches direct knowledge.

## The seven purifications

The *Visuddhimagga* is arranged according to seven stages of purification ({{term|visuddhi}}), which the Venerable Puṇṇa Mantāniputta compared to a relay of seven chariots, each carrying the traveller only as far as the next:[^mn24][^vism]

1. Purification of virtue ({{term|sīla-visuddhi}})
2. Purification of mind ({{term|citta-visuddhi}})
3. Purification of view ({{term|diṭṭhi-visuddhi}})
4. Purification by overcoming doubt ({{term|kaṅkhāvitaraṇa-visuddhi}})
5. Purification by knowledge and vision of what is the path and what is not the path
6. Purification by knowledge and vision of the way
7. Purification by knowledge and vision ({{term|ñāṇadassana-visuddhi}})

The first two are virtue and concentration; the remaining five are the development of wisdom, through insight into the three characteristics of [[Anicca|impermanence]], [[Dukkha|suffering]] and [[Anattā|non-self]], up to the knowledge of the noble paths.

## The goal of wisdom

Wisdom reaches its completion in the penetration of the [[Four Noble Truths]] at the moment of the noble path, when suffering is fully understood, its origin abandoned, its cessation realised and the path developed.[^vism22] The object of that path knowledge is [[Nibbāna]].
